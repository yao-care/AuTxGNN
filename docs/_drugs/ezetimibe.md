## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for ezetimibe: "Ezetimibe reduces blood cholesterol by inhibiting the absorption of cholesterol by the small intestine. The molecular target of ezetimibe has been shown to be the sterol transporter, Niemann-Pick C1-Like 1 (NPC1L1), which is involved in the intestinal uptake of cholesterol and phytosterols." ([DailyMed: ezetimibe prescribing information, A-S Medication Solutions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=c37c695b-fb40-423c-9b73-d1aa58a34db9), label version 2026-10-08; accessed 2026-11-19.) Based on general knowledge, ezetimibe inhibits NPC1L1-mediated intestinal cholesterol absorption and lowers LDL cholesterol.

CETP deficiency is characterised by raised HDL cholesterol and reduced LDL cholesterol. On this basis there is no clear therapeutic reason to add an LDL-lowering agent. The high score (0.9909) comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence. The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis only.
