# Acetylsalicylic Acid: From Rheumatoid Arthritis and Migraine to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin) is a long-established analgesic and antiplatelet drug, used for conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis, migraine, myocardial infarction, osteoarthritis and the common cold.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**, but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only indirect literature (mostly on general migraine with aura) supporting this specific prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on established pharmacology, aspirin inhibits cyclooxygenase (COX), reducing prostaglandin and thromboxane production and platelet aggregation.

Aspirin is already used in the acute treatment of migraine, and one retrospective study suggests low-dose aspirin may help prevent migraine with aura. Platelet-mediated microembolic or vasoreactive triggers could plausibly contribute to aura, and a small physiological study reported that aspirin activates an antinociceptive brain-stem reflex.

No study targets the brainstem aura subtype specifically. The evidence is extrapolated from migraine with aura in general, so the prediction is plausible but unproven.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered for migraine with brainstem aura.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question only)**

**Rationale:**
Aspirin is an established migraine treatment with some prophylactic data in migraine with aura, but there are no trials specific to brainstem aura. The evidence is indirect and mostly retrospective or mechanistic. The high TxGNN score alone does not justify progressing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and a clearer link to brainstem aura pathophysiology
- Prospective or randomised evidence in migraine with aura subtypes, including brainstem aura
- Approved indication text and product details for the Australian ARTG entry
- Label warnings and contraindications from the TGA Product Information, with a bleeding-risk assessment for chronic preventive use

### Other Predictions Noted

Other predicted indications were assessed in the data compiled for this report:

- **Thrombotic disease** (score 99.14%, evidence level L1): The strongest supporting evidence. Aspirin's antiplatelet action is established for arterial thrombosis prevention, so this is largely an existing use rather than true repurposing. The only Phase 3 trial (NCT07228663) is not yet recruiting and tests aspirin plus rivaroxaban. Recommendation: Proceed with Guardrails (bleeding risk, anticoagulant interactions, patient selection).
- **Thrombophilia** (score 99.04%, L2): Heterogeneous evidence. A completed pilot feasibility trial (NCT04153760) and a Phase 3 post-CABG study of unknown status (NCT01046942) do not confirm efficacy. Recommendation: Research Question.
- **Raynaud disease** (score 98.67%, L3): Only a small 1987 double-blind trial of aspirin plus dipyridamole. Recommendation: Research Question.
- **Trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia** (score 99.40%, L4), **heparin cofactor 2 deficiency**, **factor 5 excess with spontaneous thrombosis**, **antithrombin deficiency type 2**, **atrophoderma vermiculata** and **ulerythema ophryogenesis**: No supporting trials or literature, and no plausible mechanistic rationale (for the coagulation-factor disorders, anticoagulants are the standard). Recommendation: Hold.
