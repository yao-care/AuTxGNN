## Raltegravir: Simian immunodeficiency virus infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Raltegravir is an HIV-1 integrase strand transfer inhibitor, originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, a non-human primate disease.
Support comes from **0 registered clinical trials** and **15 publications**, all preclinical (mostly macaque and in vitro studies).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, raltegravir is an HIV-1 integrase strand transfer inhibitor. Its efficacy in HIV infection is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to SIV.

SIV integrase is structurally close to HIV-1 integrase. Macaque studies show raltegravir-containing regimens suppress SIVmac251, and resistance-profiling studies show similar integrase mutation patterns in SIV and HIV. Raltegravir has also been used as a component of antiretroviral regimens in SIV and SHIV macaque models.

SIV infection is a non-human primate disease and an animal model for HIV. The prediction therefore mainly confirms the drug's existing antiviral activity. It does not create a new human therapeutic opportunity beyond the current HIV indication.

Other predictions in the list are weaker:
- **AIDS related complex** and **congenital HIV** are subsets of HIV disease already covered by the approved indication. The congenital HIV literature is mostly pregnancy safety surveillance rather than efficacy.
- **Feline AIDS** is a veterinary indication with no supporting evidence.
- The remaining predictions (for example neurodevelopmental disorder, benign neoplasms, Brenner tumour, fibrocystic breast disease) have no identified mechanistic link and look like TxGNN model (deep learning) artefacts.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from drug-disease links, not label text): myopathy, muscular disease, skeletal muscle disease, familial periodic paralysis, rhabdomyolysis and nodular fasciitis. These warrant attention to muscle-related adverse effects.
- **Drug Interactions** (36 recorded; the main ones are listed below):
  - **Major**: zinc sulfate, zinc acetate, aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate. These polyvalent cation products may reduce raltegravir absorption.
  - **Moderate**: magnesium oxide, magnesium sulfate, magnesium chloride, orlistat.
  - **Minor**: H2 blockers and proton pump inhibitors (for example famotidine, ranitidine, omeprazole, pantoprazole), pioglitazone and dexamethasone.
- **Pregnancy**: Cohort and surveillance data on integrase inhibitor exposure in pregnancy exist, including UK/Ireland surveillance of raltegravir. They are relevant to safety assessment only.

Please also refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The predicted indication is a non-human primate disease. Evidence is entirely preclinical (L4), with no registered clinical trials. It adds no new human indication beyond the approved HIV use.
- Other predicted indications are either subsets of existing HIV disease, veterinary, or unsupported computational predictions.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (the available data lacks MOA).
- Label warnings and contraindications from the TGA Product Information.
- Approved indication text for the ARTG entry.
- Reframing the question toward a human-relevant use (for example, a defined clinical question in a specific HIV population), since the SIV prediction does not translate into a human repurposing opportunity.
