## Afatinib: From EGFR-Mutated Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer to HER2-Positive Breast Carcinoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Afatinib is an oral, irreversible ErbB family blocker (EGFR, HER2, HER4) that is marketed in Australia as Giotrif. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **HER2-positive breast carcinoma**, supported by **4 clinical trials** (3 completed Phase 2 studies) and **20 publications**, including a Phase 3 trial (LUX-Breast 1) that did not show superiority over trastuzumab.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From the published literature, afatinib is an irreversible ErbB family blocker that covalently binds the kinase domains of EGFR, HER2 and HER4. Direct HER2 inhibition is mechanistically coherent with HER2-driven breast cancer.

Resistance to trastuzumab and lapatinib may be mediated by other ErbB family members. This is the rationale behind testing broader ErbB blockade, either alone or alongside trastuzumab, vinorelbine or paclitaxel. Preclinical work also found that adding HER-targeted TKIs, including afatinib, enhanced the response to trastuzumab in HER2-positive breast cancer cells.

Clinical results have been modest, however. Phase II studies showed some activity, but tolerability was limited by diarrhoea and rash. LUX-Breast 1 (afatinib plus vinorelbine versus trastuzumab plus vinorelbine) did not show superiority.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There are three completed Phase 2 studies in HER2-positive breast cancer, and the mechanism is plausible. Efficacy has been modest, however, and the one Phase 3 trial (LUX-Breast 1) did not show superiority over trastuzumab plus vinorelbine. Tolerability is also a concern. Current evidence supports a research question rather than a move to clinical adoption.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the detailed results of LUX-Breast 1, 2 and 3 and the neoadjuvant trials, including response, survival and toxicity.
- Identification of a defined subgroup (for example, brain metastases or trastuzumab-resistant disease) where afatinib could add value beyond current standard HER2-targeted options.
- The TGA Product Information, including the approved indication, warnings and contraindications, and a review of the interactions flagged above.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data.

**Note on other predictions:** The other predictions in the data are weak. Multiple endocrine neoplasia, normal breast-like subtype, marcothrombocytopenia with mitral valve insufficiency, hereditary thrombocytopenia with normal platelets and dense granule disease have no supporting indication-specific evidence. Thrombocytopenia appears to reflect an adverse effect rather than a benefit. The progesterone-receptor positive and negative and luminal A or B breast cancer predictions depend on HER2 status rather than the named subtype.
