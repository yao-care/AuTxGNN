## One-Sentence Summary

Candesartan is an angiotensin II type 1 (AT1) receptor blocker that is currently marketed in Australia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, in particular migraine prevention.
This direction is supported by **3 clinical trials directly studying migraine** (two randomised, placebo-controlled trials and one observational study) and **20 publications**, including a 2025 randomised trial in *The Lancet Neurology*.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Candesartan blocks the angiotensin II type 1 receptor (gene *AGTR1*). Modulating the renin-angiotensin system is proposed to reduce migraine frequency through effects on cerebrovascular tone, neuroinflammation and sympathetic activity. Detailed mechanism-of-action data beyond this receptor-blocking role are not available in the data compiled for this report.

The prediction is supported by clinical work in migraine itself, not only by mechanism. A placebo-controlled, double-dummy trial compared candesartan with propranolol. A larger multicentre, triple-blind trial tested two doses (8 mg and 16 mg) against placebo. Its publication is a randomised trial in episodic migraine. A real-world Spanish observational study also examined response predictors and tolerability in patients who had failed several earlier preventive drugs.

An Australian review (*Medical Journal of Australia*, 2008) described the evidence for candesartan in migraine prophylaxis as "reasonably good but limited". A 2025 scoping review and a systematic review of blood-pressure-lowering medicines for episodic migraine prevention also address this question.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L2. Two randomised, placebo-controlled trials (a phase 2/3 crossover trial and a phase 2 parallel-group trial, the latter published in 2025) and a real-world observational study directly address migraine prevention. There are no multiple completed phase 3 trials, and the only listed Australian product is the 8 mg tablet.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the full results of NCT04574713 and PMID 40975098, including the efficacy of the 8 mg and 16 mg doses and their tolerability.
- Confirmation of whether a confirmatory phase 3 trial or independent replication is needed.
- Review of the TGA-approved PI for warnings, contraindications and interactions, which the data compiled for this report do not provide.
- A safety monitoring plan for patients who may be pregnant or have renal impairment, and for those taking other renin-angiotensin system agents.
- Clarification of dose and strength availability in Australia, since only an 8 mg tablet is listed locally.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data linking AT1 receptor blockade to migraine prevention.
