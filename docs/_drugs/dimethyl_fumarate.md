# Dimethyl Fumarate: From Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis to Progressive Multiple Sclerosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Dimethyl fumarate (DMF) is an oral disease-modifying therapy originally used for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (RRMS).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **progressive multiple sclerosis**,
with **3 clinical trials** and **19 publications** compiled for this direction. However, the only Phase 3 trial was terminated early and the completed Phase 2 trial in primary progressive MS did not meet its primary endpoint.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge of DMF pharmacology, DMF and its active metabolite monomethyl fumarate activate the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway and modulate immune cell populations (a shift from Th1/Th17 toward Th2, and reduced B-cell and CD8+ T-cell counts).

Both RRMS and progressive MS are forms of the same disease. These antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects could plausibly provide neuroprotection relevant to progressive disease. A caveat is that progression is driven largely by compartmentalised inflammation within the central nervous system and neurodegeneration, which peripheral immunomodulation may only partially address. The mechanistic link is therefore plausible but unproven.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 potential interactions were identified. The listed examples are all rated Moderate and include:
  - Corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisolone, prednisone, methylprednisolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone)
  - Naltrexone
  - Mercaptopurine
  - Dupilumab
  - Roflumilast
  - Dexrazoxane
  - Radiopharmaceuticals (ibritumomab tiuxetan, iobenguane I-131, tositumomab)
  - Levamisole
  - Trimetrexate
- **Literature-derived safety signals**: Lymphopenia and the associated risk of progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) are documented with DMF (see PMIDs 33091427 and 32808554).

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for formal warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The best available evidence for progressive MS is a small, completed Phase 2 trial in PPMS that did not meet its primary endpoint, and a Phase 3 trial in SPMS that was terminated after 58 participants. The mechanistic rationale is plausible but unproven, so current evidence supports this as a research question rather than a basis for use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A adequately powered, completed Phase 3 randomised trial in progressive MS (primary or secondary) with clinical disability endpoints
- Detailed mechanism of action data and clarification of CNS penetration relevance
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications, including a lymphopenia and PML monitoring plan
- Confirmation of the approved indication text for the Australian ARTG entry
