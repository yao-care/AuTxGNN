## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available in this evidence pack (`original_moa: [Data Gap]`). Based on general pharmacological knowledge, risperidone is a second-generation antipsychotic with combined 5-HT2A and D2 receptor antagonism, and its efficacy in schizophrenia and bipolar mania is well established. Mechanistically, this same 5-HT2A/D2 dual-antagonist profile is shared by other second-generation antipsychotics (aripiprazole, quetiapine) that are already approved as adjunctive therapy for treatment-resistant major depressive disorder — supporting biological plausibility for risperidone in the same role.

Major affective disorder (encompassing both major depressive disorder and bipolar disorder) sits close to risperidone's existing psychiatric indications, and augmentation of antidepressants or mood stabilisers with an atypical antipsychotic is an established, guideline-recognised strategy for treatment resistance. This is reflected in the evidence base: a large, completed, placebo-controlled Phase 3 RCT (NCT00095134, n=630) directly tested risperidone augmentation in MDD, and multiple systematic reviews/network meta-analyses have since evaluated antipsychotic augmentation strategies as a class, generally including risperidone.

Taken together, the mechanistic rationale (shared receptor pharmacology with already-approved adjunctive agents) and the depth of clinical evidence (multiple completed Phase 3 RCTs across both MDD and bipolar disorder) make this one of the better-supported candidates in the pack, distinguishing it from several other TxGNN hits that carry the same or higher prediction scores but essentially no corroborating evidence.
