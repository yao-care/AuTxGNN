# Nifedipine: From Hypertension to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Nifedipine is an L-type calcium channel blocker, originally used to treat hypertension and Prinzmetal (vasospastic) angina.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder** (the top-ranked prediction, migraine with brainstem aura, is a subtype of this condition). Support is **weak**: only **1 registered clinical trial** (not indication-specific) and a body of small, dated, mostly negative or mixed publications.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on known pharmacology, nifedipine is a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker. Its efficacy in hypertension and vasospastic angina is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to migraine.

The proposed link is that calcium channel blockade may reduce cerebrovascular tone and neuronal excitability, including cortical spreading depression. Calcium channel dysfunction (for example CACNA1A) is implicated in familial hemiplegic and brainstem-aura migraine. For that specific subtype the evidence is plausible but indirect, because the available papers address migraine in general.

The clinical record is not encouraging. Older small studies of nifedipine for migraine prophylaxis gave variable results, and headache is itself a common adverse effect of nifedipine. Nifedipine is not a guideline-preferred prophylactic agent.

## Safety Considerations

The data compiled for this report carry no label-based warnings or contraindications. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for these.

- **Disease associations flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** porphyria and porphyrin metabolism disease, kidney disease, liver disease and cirrhosis, gallbladder disease, hypotensive disorder, acute coronary syndrome and myocardial infarction, coronary thrombosis, heart failure, haemoglobinopathy, pyloric stenosis and intestinal obstruction. These need verification against the PI.
- **Drug interactions:** The data list 100 interactions, of which 20 are shown. One is Major (cisapride). Most others are Moderate, including aprepitant, aspirin, betamethasone, budesonide, bupropion, canagliflozin, cimetidine, clarithromycin and a range of calcium salts. Ranitidine is Minor. Grapefruit juice also affects nifedipine exposure (PMID 15449971).
- **Tolerability in migraine studies:** Headache and an erythromelalgia-like symptom complex led to frequent withdrawals in prophylaxis trials.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Randomised data for nifedipine in migraine are small, dated and largely negative or mixed. Nifedipine is not guideline-preferred and often causes headache. The only registered trial is not indication-specific, and there is no direct evidence for the brainstem aura subtype.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for nifedipine, and confirmation of what the 2026 AAN/AHS reviews conclude about it
- A head-to-head appraisal against guideline-preferred prophylactic agents
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications, since none were available
- Evidence specific to migraine with brainstem aura, if that subtype is the target
- A formulation and dose rationale, since the only ARTG product is a 60 mg controlled-release tablet
- Consideration of primary Raynaud's phenomenon as a separate, better-supported question
