# Rizatriptan: From Acute Migraine to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Rizatriptan is a triptan-class 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist established globally for the acute treatment of migraine, though it is not currently marketed or ARTG-registered in Australia. The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction flags **Migraine with Brainstem Aura** as a candidate indication, but this is a subtype where triptans have traditionally been used with caution due to theoretical vasoconstriction risk. ## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this Evidence Pack (Data Gap DG002). Based on known pharmacology, Rizatriptan is a member of the triptan class of selective 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonists. Activation of these receptors produces cranial vessel constriction, inhibition of neuropeptide release (e.g. CGRP, substance P), and reduced transmission along trigeminal pain pathways — the standard mechanism underlying triptan efficacy in acute migraine, and one that is well validated for the broader migraine/headache class (see the "headache disorder" evidence below).

Migraine with brainstem aura (historically termed "basilar-type" or "basilar migraine") is a migraine subtype, so on a purely mechanistic basis the same 5-HT1B/1D agonism could plausibly relieve attacks. However, this is precisely the subtype where the mechanism becomes a double-edged sword: because brainstem aura involves posterior-circulation/brainstem symptoms, triptan-induced vasoconstriction has conventionally raised concern about exacerbating brainstem ischaemia, and product labelling for triptans as a class has historically listed this subtype as a relative contraindication. The one publication directly on point (PMID 11903526, "Triptans in the treatment of basilar migraine and migraine with prolonged aura") reflects this exact tension — supportive anecdotal/clinical use alongside caution.

In short: the mechanistic link is genuine, but it is also the source of the safety concern, so it cannot be treated as a straightforward positive signal without further safety data.

## Safety Considerations
