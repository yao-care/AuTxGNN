## Osimertinib: From EGFR-Mutant Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer to Thrombocytopenia (Prediction Not Supported)

## One-Sentence Summary

Osimertinib is an oral EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor marketed in Australia as Tagrisso. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **thrombocytopenia**, but the compiled literature (**20 publications, no registered clinical trials**) describes thrombocytopenia as an **adverse effect** of osimertinib, not a benefit. The prediction most likely reflects a toxicity signal in the knowledge graph rather than therapeutic potential.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this report. The literature identifies osimertinib as a third-generation, irreversible EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor that targets EGFR-sensitising and T790M resistance mutations. It is used for EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).

**This prediction is not mechanistically plausible as a treatment.** The published literature shows thrombocytopenia as a recognised adverse event of osimertinib:
- A meta-analysis of randomised trials characterised its haematological toxicities.
- Case reports describe severe thrombocytopenia, including one involving rechallenge after recovery.
- Pharmacovigilance and network meta-analyses list thrombocytopenia among the adverse effects.

The high TxGNN score most likely reflects a drug-disease link in the knowledge graph arising from this toxicity signal. The direction of effect is harm, not benefit.

The other top-ranked predictions show the same pattern. Heart disease, cardiovascular disease, heart conduction disease and thrombotic disease are documented osimertinib toxicities (cardiomyopathy, heart failure, QT prolongation, thrombotic events). Heart neoplasm has no supporting evidence. The remaining rare thrombocytopenia and platelet disorders have no evidence at all.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- There are no clinical trials for the predicted indication, and the literature shows thrombocytopenia as a harm of osimertinib. The high TxGNN score appears to be a toxicity signal, and the evidence level is L4 at best.
- Repurposing osimertinib for thrombocytopenia is not supported and could cause patient harm.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Any rationale or data showing a therapeutic (not toxic) effect on platelet count, which is currently absent.
- The TGA Product Information (indication, warnings and contraindications), which was not available in the data compiled for this report.
- Mechanism of action data to assess any mechanistic link.
- Redirecting the review toward toxicity surveillance (haematological and cardiovascular), or toward other candidate indications.
