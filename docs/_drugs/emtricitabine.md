## Emtricitabine: From HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Emtricitabine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor, originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS (always in combination with other antiretrovirals).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, with **0 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** (mostly macaque studies) currently supporting this direction.
This is an animal-model finding that largely mirrors the existing HIV indication, not a new human use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, emtricitabine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI) that acts on HIV-1 reverse transcriptase. Its efficacy in HIV is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to related lentiviruses that rely on similar reverse transcriptase enzymes.

SIV and SHIV (simian/human immunodeficiency virus) infection in macaques are the standard preclinical models of HIV. Emtricitabine combined with tenofovir (as TDF or TAF) protected macaques from SHIV acquisition in several prophylaxis studies. The emtricitabine resistance pathway (M184V mutation) has also been characterised in SIV. The mechanistic link is therefore well supported.

However, the evidence is entirely animal-model data that serves as a proxy for the already-approved human HIV indication. SIV infection is not a human disease, so this prediction does not represent a genuinely new human indication. The TxGNN score reflects network proximity to HIV rather than independent clinical validation.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The supporting evidence is entirely preclinical (macaque and virology studies) and is a proxy for the existing HIV indication. SIV infection is not a human disease, so no new human indication is established. The top-ranked feline prediction rests on a single paper whose design could not be verified.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A clear definition of the intended human use, if any, since the current evidence maps onto the already-approved HIV treatment and prophylaxis indications
- Mechanism of action data (MOA) for emtricitabine
- Approved indication text and Product Information (warnings and contraindications) for the ARTG-listed products
- Full-text review of the literature, since many study designs were inferred from titles and abstracts
