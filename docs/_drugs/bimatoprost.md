## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, bimatoprost is a synthetic prostamide F2-alpha analogue. Its efficacy in lowering intraocular pressure is established. Mechanistically it may also be applicable to hair loss.

Hair growth is a known effect of the drug class. Patients using prostaglandin analogues for glaucoma developed longer, thicker and darker eyelashes, which led to a bimatoprost eyelash product being approved overseas. Prostamide/prostaglandin receptors on hair follicles are thought to prolong the growth (anagen) phase and promote follicle activity. That is why scalp alopecia is a reasonable extension.

Important caveats:
- Eyelash follicles behave differently from scalp follicles, so eyelash efficacy does not automatically translate to the scalp.
- No Phase 3 trial has been completed and no scalp indication has been approved.
- For alopecia areata, bimatoprost may stimulate follicles but does not address the autoimmune cause.

The other nine top predictions lack support. Several are implausible: Ambras hypertrichosis (bimatoprost causes hypertrichosis, so this would be counterproductive), Dandy-Walker malformation, pulmonary arteriovenous malformation and the dental malformation syndrome. These probably reflect graph artefacts. Hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp, congenital hypotrichosis milia, isolated hair shaft abnormality and genetic alopecia are biologically plausible but have little or no disease-specific evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails (alopecia only); Hold for all other predicted indications**

**Rationale:**
- Several completed Phase 2 trials (about 850 participants across the three largest) examine bimatoprost in androgenetic alopecia and female pattern hair loss, and the eyelash effect is a well-recognised class action. However, no Phase 3 trial exists, results are not summarised here, and no scalp indication has been approved anywhere. The other nine predictions have no clinical evidence, and some (Ambras hypertrichosis, Dandy-Walker malformation, pulmonary arteriovenous malformation) are mechanistically implausible.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published efficacy and safety results from the Phase 2 scalp trials (NCT01325337, NCT01325350, NCT01904721)
- A Phase 3 trial, or regulatory advice on the evidence needed for a scalp indication
- A suitable topical formulation, since only an ophthalmic product is registered in Australia
- TGA Product Information safety data, including warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data
- Assessment of the graph-flagged conditions (for example uveitis, macular oedema, liver disease) and the potential interactions for the intended population
