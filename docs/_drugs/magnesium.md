# Magnesium: From Unspecified Original Indication to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Magnesium (as magnesium aspartate) is marketed in Australia as an oral tablet, but the data compiled for this report do not record an approved indication text.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, with **8 registered clinical trials** (4 completed) and **20 publications** (including a meta-analysis of RCTs) supporting this direction.

*Note on evidence level:* The data compiled for this report assign L1. This rests on the completed Phase 3 trial NCT05967442 and the Phase 2/3 trial NCT02322333. These two trials have similar enrolment (n=157) and design, and may overlap, so they may not be independent. The exact intervention in NCT02322333 is also unconfirmed. L1 should therefore be treated as provisional, and a strict reading could place the evidence closer to L2.

---

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available from the standard drug record. Based on the mechanistic literature compiled for this report, magnesium modulates NMDA receptor activity and calcium channels. It also reduces cortical spreading depression and neuronal hyperexcitability, and affects serotonin and nitric oxide pathways and platelet aggregation.

Low brain and serum magnesium have been reported in people with migraine. This gives biological plausibility for oral supplementation as prophylaxis and for intravenous magnesium in acute attacks. Because the original approved indication is not recorded, the link between old and new use cannot be assessed directly. The rationale rests on magnesium's role as a replacement or supplement for a deficiency state that is itself implicated in migraine.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed controlled trials, a meta-analysis of RCTs and a plausible mechanism support magnesium in migraine. However, the meta-analysis noted equivocal findings, the Cochrane review is still at protocol stage, and two of the key trials may overlap. The oral aspartate tablet marketed in Australia differs from the IV magnesium sulfate used in the acute-treatment trials.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Safety review from the TGA-approved PI (warnings, contraindications, renal impairment considerations), which was not available
- Confirmation of the approved indication for the ARTG entry
- Verification that NCT02322333 and NCT05967442 are independent and that magnesium is the intervention in NCT02322333
- Review of the results of the Cochrane review when published
- Consideration of route and formulation differences between oral tablets and the IV trials
