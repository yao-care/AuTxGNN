## Lenalidomide: From Plasma Cell Myeloma to Myeloid Leukaemia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Lenalidomide is an oral immunomodulatory drug, originally used for plasma cell myeloma, mantle cell lymphoma and myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myeloid leukaemia**,
with **44 registered clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently linked to this direction. Most of the trials are small, early-phase or single-arm studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for the original indications is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the mechanistic rationale compiled for this prediction, lenalidomide binds cereblon (CRBN) and redirects the CRL4-CRBN E3 ubiquitin ligase to degrade the transcription factors IKZF1 and IKZF3, as well as CK1alpha. Degradation of CK1alpha underlies its activity in del(5q) MDS. Lenalidomide also activates T cells and natural killer (NK) cells, which may support activity in AML and MDS.

Myeloid leukaemia is closely related to MDS, which is already a listed indication. MDS frequently progresses to acute myeloid leukaemia (AML). Several trials have enrolled MDS and AML patients together, especially those with chromosome 5 abnormalities.

The biological rationale is strong. However, the clinical benefit in AML without del(5q) appears modest and is mostly seen in small single-arm studies.

## Safety Considerations

Label-based warnings and contraindications are not available in the data compiled for this report.

- **Disease-level contraindication signals (knowledge graph, not label text):** thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, pulmonary embolism, impaired renal function, cancer and embryoma.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Major interactions are listed with **rosuvastatin** and **simvastatin**. Moderate interactions are listed with **eliglustat** and **naltrexone**. Many others have an unclassified severity, including metformin, morphine, prednisone, omeprazole and pantoprazole.
- **Secondary malignancy signal:** the linked literature reports lenalidomide-associated secondary B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukaemia and therapy-related myeloid neoplasms. This is relevant when considering use in haematological malignancy.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- Many phase 1 and 2 trials, including some randomised studies, examine lenalidomide in AML and MDS, and the biological rationale is strong. However, the studies are mostly small, single-arm and early-phase. Clinical benefit is modest outside del(5q) disease, and no completed phase 3 evidence is identified, so the evidence level is L2.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TGA Product Information, including warnings, contraindications and approved indications for ARTG 11029L
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Review of randomised results (for example NCT01358734 and NCT01522976) and a restriction to settings with cytogenetic selection, such as del(5q)
- A monitoring plan covering myelosuppression, thromboembolism, renal function, secondary malignancy risk and pregnancy prevention
- Review of the interaction profile with statins and other co-medications
