## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for bromocriptine: "CYCLOSET contains bromocriptine mesylate, a sympatholytic, dopamine D2 receptor agonist. In patients with type 2 diabetes, timed morning administration of CYCLOSET is associated with increased insulin sensitivity and glucose disposal and reduced fasting and postprandial hyperglycemia throughout the meals of the day without raising plasma insulin levels." ([DailyMed: Cycloset (bromocriptine mesylate) prescribing information, Avvisto Therapeutics LLC](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=e42ba916-16b9-4d1c-8d09-9d2fbb1b6c20), label version 2026-01-12; accessed 2026-11-19.) Bromocriptine is generally known as a dopamine D2 receptor agonist, but the data supplied do not establish any mechanism that would link this action to congenital disorder of glycosylation with defective fucosylation.

No mechanistic link was identified. The high score is a model-based prediction only. It is not supported by any trial, publication or biological rationale in the data compiled for this report. The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis for investigation, not as evidence of benefit.
