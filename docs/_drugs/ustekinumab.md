## One-Sentence Summary

Ustekinumab is an IL-12/IL-23 antagonist, originally used to treat psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis and related conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatitis** (in the available evidence, mainly atopic dermatitis),
with **6 clinical trials** (2 directly testing ustekinumab in atopic dermatitis) and **20 publications** identified. However, the clinical results to date have been modest and inconsistent.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, ustekinumab blocks the p40 subunit shared by IL-12 and IL-23, which inhibits the Th1 and Th17 pathways. This is well established in psoriasis.

Psoriasis and dermatitis are both chronic inflammatory skin diseases. However, atopic dermatitis is mainly driven by Th2 immunity, with variable Th1/Th17 contributions (for example in chronic, intrinsic, paediatric or Asian-phenotype disease). The mechanistic rationale is therefore plausible but only partial.

Clinical results in atopic dermatitis have been modest and inconsistent. Th2-targeted agents such as dupilumab and tralokinumab are clearly more effective.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Two Phase 2 randomised trials and several systematic reviews have tested ustekinumab in atopic dermatitis, but the results have been modest and inconsistent. The mechanistic fit is only partial, and Th2-targeted agents are clearly more effective. This is better treated as a research question than a repurposing candidate for now. The other nine predictions (for example neonatal dermatomyositis, pityriasis lichenoides) have no supporting trials or literature.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A critical appraisal of the Phase 2 results (NCT01945086, NCT01806662 and PMIDs 27304428, 28338223) to confirm the magnitude of effect
- Evidence for a defined subgroup (for example chronic, intrinsic, paediatric or Asian-phenotype disease) where Th1/Th17 activity predominates
- Mechanism of action data and TGA Product Information safety details (warnings and contraindications)
- Comparison with approved Th2-targeted therapies in the Australian context
