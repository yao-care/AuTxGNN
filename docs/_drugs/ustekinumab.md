# Ustekinumab: From Psoriasis to Dermatitis (Atopic Dermatitis)

## One-Sentence Summary

Ustekinumab is a human IL-12/IL-23 monoclonal antibody with established use in psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Dermatitis** — with the underlying evidence specifically concentrated on **Atopic Dermatitis** — supported by **7 clinical trials** (including 2 completed Phase 2 RCTs) and **17 publications**. A Blocking data gap on TGA Product Information means the safety picture cannot yet be finalised.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the structured drug-level field for this candidate (data gap). However, the literature captured in this evidence pack describes ustekinumab as a human monoclonal IgG1 antibody that binds the shared p40 subunit of interleukin-12 (IL-12) and interleukin-23 (IL-23), thereby suppressing Th1, Th17 and Th22 immune activation (PMID 27304428). This IL-12/23 blockade is the accepted basis for ustekinumab's efficacy in psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis — all conditions in which Th17/Th22-driven inflammation plays a central role.

Atopic dermatitis shares some of this immunological architecture: while Th2 signalling predominates, Th22 (and, in Asian populations and chronic lesions, Th17) contribution is well documented. This overlap is the biological rationale that has been directly tested — two completed Phase 2 RCTs (NCT01945086 in Japanese patients, NCT01806662 in patients with suboptimal response to prior therapy) evaluated ustekinumab specifically in moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis.

The evidence, however, is mixed rather than confirmatory. A real-world effectiveness analysis (PMID 33849369) explicitly describes "conflicting results," and a review of off-label ustekinumab use (PMID 36208443) frames atopic dermatitis as an off-label application, with IL-4/13-targeted biologics (e.g., dupilumab) remaining first-line. The mechanistic rationale is therefore plausible but not yet strongly validated at the efficacy level, and is entirely unassessed at the safety/regulatory level for this specific indication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 2 RCTs and several systematic reviews provide a genuine, if mixed, efficacy signal for ustekinumab in atopic dermatitis, and the IL-12/23 mechanism offers plausible biological rationale. However, a Blocking data gap on TGA-approved warnings/contraindications means this candidate cannot yet enter safety assessment (S1), and the apparent "not marketed / 0 ARTG entries" status needs reconciliation before any further action.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TGA-approved Product Information for ustekinumab (warnings, contraindications, drug interactions) — currently the Blocking data gap (DG001)
- Confirmation of structured mechanism-of-action data in DrugBank (DG002)
- Reconciliation of ARTG registration status against actual Australian market presence of ustekinumab
- A focused evidence synthesis on atopic dermatitis efficacy, given the conflicting real-world results noted in PMID 33849369, before considering any guardrailed off-label positioning
