# Tetracosactide: From Diagnostic ACTH Stimulation Testing to Adrenal Cortex Disease (Autoimmune Addison's Disease)

## One-Sentence Summary

> Tetracosactide is a synthetic ACTH(1‑24) analogue whose established role internationally is stimulating adrenocortical cortisol/aldosterone secretion, most familiar as the basis of the Synacthen® stimulation test.
> Of the ten candidate indications generated by TxGNN, only **adrenal cortex disease** (which includes autoimmune Addison's disease) is supported by an actual body of clinical and literature evidence — the other nine TxGNN top-ranked candidates (ultra-rare congenital steroidogenesis defects) were reviewed and mechanistically rejected within the evidence pack itself.
> This candidate has **20+ clinical trials** and **20 publications** in the supporting evidence, including a cohort study specifically testing tetracosactide in autoimmune Addison's disease, giving it **L2** evidence strength.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed manufacturer mechanism-of-action data (DrugBank MOA field) is not available in this evidence pack (data gap DG002). However, the evidence pack's mechanistic rationale confirms that tetracosactide is a synthetic ACTH(1‑24) analogue whose pharmacology is, by definition, targeted at the adrenal cortex — it directly stimulates glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid secretion from adrenocortical cells that retain functional capacity.

This is not a repurposing leap in the conventional sense so much as an extension along the drug's core pharmacological axis: diagnostic use (the ACTH/cosyntropin stimulation test is a well-established, globally used tool for assessing adrenocortical reserve) into a treatment-adjacent role in **autoimmune Addison's disease**, where a subset of patients retain residual adrenocortical tissue capable of responding to ACTH stimulation. The cohort literature identified here (Gan et al., 2014) specifically reports improvement in residual adrenal function following tetracosactide treatment in this population — supporting biological plausibility beyond simple diagnostic testing.

The distinction from the nine rejected candidates is important: those conditions involve structural absence of adrenal tissue, receptor-level ACTH resistance (e.g. familial glucocorticoid deficiency via MC2R/MRAP defects), or enzyme blocks downstream of ACTH receptor activation — none of which an ACTH analogue can overcome. Adrenal cortex disease/Addison's disease, by contrast, is a setting where some patients retain ACTH-responsive tissue, making the mechanism directly applicable.
