## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Alprostadil is prostaglandin E1 (PGE1). Its receptor-mediated effect is well established: it relaxes the smooth muscle of the ductus arteriosus and keeps the duct open.

This matters for aortic lesions in which the systemic circulation depends on a patent duct, such as coarctation of the aorta and interrupted aortic arch. In these neonates, alprostadil acts as a temporising bridge until surgical repair. The mechanistic link is therefore plausible, but it supports a bridging role rather than treatment of the malformation itself.

The evidence level is L5 because none of the cited studies directly studies aortic malformation as a defined disease. The studies concern congenital heart disease in general or individual lesions (for example interrupted aortic arch in a single case report). They are described below as supportive context only.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications:** Label-based contraindication data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI).
- **Reported safety signals in the literature:** Prolonged prostaglandin E1 infusion in neonates has been linked to pseudo-Bartter syndrome and to gastric outlet obstruction (antral foveolar hyperplasia or hypertrophic pyloric stenosis).
- **Drug Interactions:** 59 interactions were identified. Moderate-level interactions include canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, lorcaserin and sapropterin. Others, with an unclassified severity level, include morphine, metformin, omeprazole, pantoprazole, prednisolone, prednisone, simvastatin, vancomycin, atropine and amphotericin B.
- **Note on the local product:** The only Australian licence is an intracavernosal injection. It is not a neonatal intravenous infusion product, so route and formulation compatibility with the predicted use has not been established.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high and the mechanism for duct-dependent aortic lesions is plausible. However, no cited study directly addresses aortic malformation as a defined disease, there are no registered trials, and the only Australian product is an intracavernosal formulation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Studies or trials that directly address aortic malformation or specific duct-dependent aortic lesions
- Confirmation of an appropriate neonatal intravenous formulation and its regulatory status in Australia
- Mechanism of action data and label-based warnings and contraindications from the TGA Product Information
- A neonatal safety monitoring plan, including gastric outlet obstruction and electrolyte disturbance during prolonged infusion
