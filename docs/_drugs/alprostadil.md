## Alprostadil: From Erectile Dysfunction and Congenital Heart Defects to Aortic Malformation
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Alprostadil (prostaglandin E1) is originally used for erectile dysfunction and to keep the ductus arteriosus open in newborns with ductal-dependent congenital heart defects.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **aortic malformation**, but this is largely a broader grouping of uses it already has (such as aortic coarctation).
There are **no registered clinical trials** and **8 publications** (mostly case reports and indirect studies) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on established knowledge, alprostadil is a synthetic form of prostaglandin E1. It relaxes smooth muscle, including that of the fetal ductus arteriosus, and this is how it is used in newborns with ductal-dependent heart lesions.

Several conditions already listed among its original indications (aortic coarctation, atypical coarctation, tricuspid atresia, tetralogy of Fallot) depend on the ductus staying open to maintain blood flow. "Aortic malformation" is a broader category that includes these lesions, such as interrupted aortic arch. The prediction is therefore largely a generalisation of existing use rather than a truly new indication.

The supporting literature is indirect. It includes a 1982 evaluation in infants with congenital heart disease, case reports (interrupted aortic arch, aortopulmonary window), and perioperative anti-inflammatory use during cardiopulmonary bypass. None of it is specific to a new aortic malformation indication.

The other nine predictions in the data (for example congenital tricuspid stenosis, tricuspid valve agenesis, heart septal defect) are mostly graph-proximity predictions with no trials or literature. Tricuspid valve prolapse and endemic goitre have no plausible mechanistic link, and are likely artefacts of the knowledge graph.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from knowledge graph associations, not label text)**: Priapism, Peyronie disease, urethral stricture, balanitis and balanoposthitis, urethritis, sickle cell disease and other haemoglobinopathies, polycythaemia, thrombocytosis, plasma cell myeloma, leukaemia, blood coagulation disorders, hypotensive disorders, and respiratory conditions (newborn respiratory distress syndrome, acute respiratory distress syndrome, lung disease). Pyloric stenosis is also listed.
- **Relevant adverse event reports**: Prolonged prostaglandin E1 therapy in a neonate was linked to antral foveolar hyperplasia and hypertrophic pyloric stenosis (PMID 23521358). Pseudo-Bartter syndrome was reported during infusion (PMID 12811550). Neonatal use also carries known risks such as apnoea, fever and flushing.
- **Drug Interactions**: 59 interactions were recorded. The moderate-level ones are SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin), lorcaserin and sapropterin. Others, such as metformin, morphine, prednisone, simvastatin, vancomycin and proton pump inhibitors, are of unknown severity.

Label warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is largely a generalisation of alprostadil's established use in ductal-dependent heart lesions, and evidence is limited to case reports, older clinical evaluations and indirect studies (L4). There are no registered trials, and the only Australian product is an intracavernosal formulation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications
- Clarification of whether "aortic malformation" adds anything beyond the existing coarctation and related indications
- Confirmation of a neonatal intravenous formulation available in Australia
- Review of the literature for direct evidence in aortic malformation subtypes
- A neonatal safety monitoring plan covering apnoea, gastric outlet obstruction and electrolyte disturbance
