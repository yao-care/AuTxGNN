## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for roxithromycin is not available in this evidence pack. Based on known pharmacology, roxithromycin is a 14-membered ring macrolide antibiotic that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by reversibly binding the 50S ribosomal subunit — the same mechanistic class as clarithromycin and erythromycin.

Multiple in vitro and murine studies included in this pack demonstrate that macrolides as a class have activity against *Mycobacterium leprae*, the causative organism of leprosy. Clarithromycin in particular has been adopted by WHO as an alternative agent in multidrug leprosy therapy, lending plausibility to a class effect for roxithromycin.

However, roxithromycin itself has not been directly tested against *M. leprae* in the cited literature — one comparative animal study (PMID 1648889) found roxithromycin bactericidal but inferior to clarithromycin in mouse footpad infection models. The mechanistic rationale is therefore reasonable at the class level, but drug-specific human efficacy data for roxithromycin in leprosy is currently absent.
