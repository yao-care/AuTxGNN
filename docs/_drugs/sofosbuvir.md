# Sofosbuvir: From Chronic Hepatitis C to Hepatitis B Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Sofosbuvir is a nucleotide polymerase inhibitor, originally used to treat chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatitis B virus infection**, but the supporting data come almost entirely from HCV/HBV co-infection studies. Only **1 small Phase 2 study (n=21)** has looked at HBV directly, and there is no clear mechanistic basis for antiviral activity against HBV.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for sofosbuvir are not available in the data compiled for this report. From what is known, sofosbuvir is a nucleotide analogue that inhibits the HCV NS5B RNA-dependent RNA polymerase. It is marketed in Australia as part of fixed-dose combinations with velpatasvir (Epclusa) and with velpatasvir plus voxilaprevir (Vosevi).

The mechanistic link to HBV is weak. HBV is a DNA virus that replicates through a reverse-transcriptase step, so sofosbuvir has no established direct activity against it. The clinical data come from HCV/HBV co-infection, where clearing HCV can shift HBV-DNA and HBsAg levels. The main safety concern in that setting is HBV reactivation, and the evidence supports monitoring or prophylaxis (for example with tenofovir alafenamide) rather than using sofosbuvir to treat HBV. The model's score therefore likely reflects the closeness of viral hepatitis diseases in the knowledge graph rather than a proven treatment effect.

The single most relevant study is a Phase 2 open-label trial of ledipasvir/sofosbuvir in HBV mono-infection, which was based on a modest HBsAg reduction seen retrospectively in co-infected patients.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions** (8 interactions recorded):
  - **Major**: apalutamide, enzalutamide, lorlatinib
  - **Moderate**: fostamatinib, warfarin, dicoumarol, encorafenib
  - **Minor**: cyclosporine
- **HBV reactivation**: The literature in this report repeatedly shows that HBV can reactivate when HCV is cleared with direct-acting antivirals in co-infected patients. This is directly relevant to any HBV-related use.

For other warnings and contraindications, please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There is no mechanistic basis for direct anti-HBV activity, and the clinical evidence is limited to HCV/HBV co-infection plus one small, single-arm Phase 2 study. The data point towards HBV reactivation risk and monitoring rather than treatment benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and any in vitro evidence of anti-HBV activity
- Controlled studies of HBV efficacy in HBV mono-infection, with virological endpoints (HBsAg, HBV DNA)
- Approved indication text and PI safety information (warnings, contraindications) for the Australian products
- A monitoring or prophylaxis plan for HBV reactivation in co-infected patients
