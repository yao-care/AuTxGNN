## Valine: From Nutritional/Metabolic Supplementation to Sclerosing Cholangitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Valine is an essential branched-chain amino acid (BCAA) supplied in Australia as a dietary management product for metabolic conditions. The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant to **sclerosing cholangitis**, but this rests on a model score alone. No clinical trials are registered, and only **2 publications** were retrieved, both associative (a cohort study and a Mendelian randomization study). There is no evidence that valine treats the disease.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Valine is a branched-chain amino acid, and the data compiled for this report do not include a formal mechanism of action.

Altered amino acid profiles, including BCAA and aromatic amino acid ratios, have been described in cholestatic liver disease. A 2024 Mendelian randomization study examined causal relationships between blood metabolites and cholestatic liver diseases (primary biliary cholangitis and primary sclerosing cholangitis). The link is metabolic and associative. There is no evidence that giving valine changes the course of sclerosing cholangitis. The 2005 paper concerns plasma tyrosine and fatigue, not valine therapy.

The high model score should therefore be treated as a hypothesis-generating signal only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question only)**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no clinical trial support, and the two retrieved publications are associative and do not test valine as a treatment. The data compiled for this report contain no mechanism of action and no safety information, so the candidate cannot progress to safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Approved indication text and Product Information (warnings, contraindications) for both ARTG entries
- Mechanism of action data, and a plausible rationale for why valine supplementation would affect sclerosing cholangitis rather than merely correlate with it
- Preclinical or mechanistic studies testing valine or BCAA supplementation in cholestatic liver disease models
- Assessment of route and formulation suitability, given the current products are oral dietary-management powders designed for metabolic disorders

**Note on other predictions:** The other top-ranked predictions (angle-closure glaucoma, hyperthyroidism, aqueous misdirection, traumatic glaucoma, neovascular glaucoma, glaucomatous optic disc atrophy, thyroid hormone resistance, hyperthyroxinaemia) have even weaker support. Several literature matches appear to be artefacts of "Val" mutation nomenclature (for example TSH receptor and thyroid hormone receptor beta variants) rather than studies of valine as a drug. The hyperthyroidism predictions have only indirect, associative BCAA metabolism data. One prediction ("obsolete bundle branch block") is an obsolete ontology term and should likely be excluded.
