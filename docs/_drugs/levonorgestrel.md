## One-Sentence Summary

Levonorgestrel is a synthetic progestin, a hormone-like drug that acts on progesterone receptors.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **acne** (score 0.9988). The compiled data contain **no registered clinical trials** and **19 publications** for this prediction.
Only one publication is a randomised placebo-controlled trial, and it tested an oral contraceptive combining levonorgestrel with ethinyl estradiol, so it does not isolate levonorgestrel's own effect. The evidence level is **L2**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not currently available for levonorgestrel. It is a progestin with some residual androgenic (male-hormone-like) activity (PMID 7825629). In theory this is a concern for acne rather than a benefit, because androgens stimulate the oil glands of the skin.

Dermatological benefit in acne is documented mainly for combined oral contraceptives that contain progestins with anti-androgenic properties, such as chlormadinone acetate (PMIDs 21895044, 15025547). In one review, a chlormadinone-containing pill was reported to be more effective for mild-to-moderate acne than an ethinyl estradiol/levonorgestrel pill.

A randomised placebo-controlled trial (PMID 12196750) did test a low-dose pill containing ethinyl estradiol 20 µg and levonorgestrel 100 µg in moderate acne. In that product, the estrogen component may contribute to any benefit.

The high TxGNN score probably reflects the drug's similarity to hormonal contraceptives as a class. It is not evidence of a direct benefit from levonorgestrel. Any effect on acne may be indirect and could be neutral or adverse, depending on the formulation. Acne has been reported as a side effect of levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine systems and implants.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L2, based on one randomised placebo-controlled trial. That trial tested a levonorgestrel and ethinyl estradiol combination, not levonorgestrel alone. Levonorgestrel's residual androgenic activity, and acne reports with some levonorgestrel formulations, argue against assuming benefit from the drug itself.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Trial results separating levonorgestrel's effect from that of ethinyl estradiol, ideally for the formulations marketed in Australia
- Safety data from the TGA-approved Product Information (PI), including warnings and contraindications
