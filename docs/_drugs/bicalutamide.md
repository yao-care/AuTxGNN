## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for bicalutamide: "CASODEX is a non-steroidal androgen receptor inhibitor. It competitively inhibits the action of androgens by binding to cytosol androgen receptors in the target tissue. Prostatic carcinoma is known to be androgen sensitive and responds to treatment that counteracts the effect of androgen and/or removes the source of androgen." ([DailyMed: Casodex (bicalutamide) prescribing information, ANI Pharmaceuticals, Inc.](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=dfee4fe7-8478-4a3e-925d-00be3cd0ab67), label version 2026-02-27; accessed 2026-11-19.) Based on its known drug class, bicalutamide is a nonsteroidal androgen receptor (AR) antagonist.

Androgen signalling drives hair growth in androgen-sensitive follicles, so blocking the AR could plausibly reduce androgen-dependent hair growth. This is the mechanistic basis for the model's prediction.

The only supporting literature is a 2022 comment on a retrospective review of 35 patients, in which bicalutamide improved minoxidil-induced hypertrichosis in female pattern hair loss. That review itself is not among the records compiled for this report, so the evidence is indirect and secondary. It has not been confirmed in a controlled trial.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only human evidence is a comment letter on a small retrospective review, and there are no registered trials. The mechanistic rationale is plausible but unconfirmed, so the evidence level is L4.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Retrieval and appraisal of the source retrospective review of 35 patients
- Controlled clinical data on bicalutamide for hypertrichosis
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications for safety screening, especially liver function monitoring
- Assessment of safety in the intended population, including women, given the drug's anti-androgen effects
