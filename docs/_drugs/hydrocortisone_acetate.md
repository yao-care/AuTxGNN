## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Hydrocortisone is a glucocorticoid, and glucocorticoids are generally anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune disease affecting the hair follicle. Corticosteroids are an established treatment class for it, so the mechanistic link is plausible.

Hydrocortisone is a low-potency corticosteroid. Higher-potency agents (for example clobetasol or intralesional triamcinolone) are generally preferred, which limits the expected efficacy of hydrocortisone itself.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only trial of alopecia areata used hydrocortisone 1% cream as the low-potency comparator, and the one other directly relevant publication is a 1973 case series without an abstract. The small, comparator-only evidence does not show that hydrocortisone acetate is effective. Higher-potency corticosteroids are generally preferred.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the full NCT01453686 results to see how the hydrocortisone arm performed
- Comparative data against higher-potency corticosteroids in alopecia areata
- Mechanism of action data
- Warnings and contraindications from the TGA-approved Product Information
