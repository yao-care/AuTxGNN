## Hydrocortisone Acetate: Alopecia areata
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Hydrocortisone acetate is a low-potency corticosteroid, originally used for autoimmune and inflammatory conditions such as Crohn disease, inflammatory bowel disease, adrenocortical insufficiency and congenital adrenal hyperplasia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**,
with **1 clinical trial** (in which hydrocortisone was the comparator) and **2 older publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, hydrocortisone acetate is a glucocorticoid with anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive activity, its use in autoimmune and inflammatory disease is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to alopecia areata.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune disease affecting the hair follicle. Glucocorticoids are an established treatment class for it, so the link is plausible. However, hydrocortisone is a low-potency corticosteroid. Higher-potency agents (such as clobetasol or intralesional triamcinolone) are generally preferred, which limits the expected efficacy of hydrocortisone.

The other nine predictions in the data (for example telogen effluvium, alopecia mucinosa, folliculitis decalvans and idiopathic steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome) have no trial or literature support. They are not assessed further here. Several appear to reflect network proximity to other hair-loss disorders rather than a specific mechanism.

## Safety Considerations

The compiled data contain no label-based warnings, contraindications or drug interaction results. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for safety information.

The drug-disease knowledge graph lists the following conditions as potential contraindications. These are not label text and should be verified against the PI:
- **Cardiovascular and metabolic:** hypertension, hyperglycaemia
- **Gastrointestinal, hepatic and renal:** gastric ulcer, kidney disease, pyoureter, liver disease, gallbladder disease
- **Neurological:** epilepsy
- **Infections:** measles, herpes simplex virus keratitis
- **Pituitary and endocrine:** hypopituitarism, growth hormone deficiency and related pituitary deficiency disorders, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (including Kallmann syndrome), and Sheehan syndrome

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only trial used hydrocortisone as a low-potency comparator against a stronger steroid, and the two publications are decades old and not informative about efficacy. Higher-potency corticosteroids already serve as the standard approach, so hydrocortisone acetate offers little expected advantage in alopecia areata. The high TxGNN score alone does not justify progressing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The results of NCT01453686, to see how hydrocortisone 1% performed against clobetasol
- Mechanism of action data, which are currently unavailable
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the TGA PI, because the safety data are incomplete and this blocks safety screening
- Evidence for a hydrocortisone acetate formulation specifically, and a comparison with currently preferred treatments for alopecia areata
- Confirmation of the approved indications for the registered Australian product (Cortic-DS 1%)
