# Pibrentasvir: From Hepatitis C Virus Infection to Hepatitis B Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Pibrentasvir is an NS5A inhibitor, used in the glecaprevir/pibrentasvir combination (Maviret) to treat chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatitis B virus infection**, but the data compiled for this report contain **no clinical trials** and **no publications** showing anti-HBV activity.
The high score appears to reflect network proximity among viral hepatitis conditions rather than a demonstrated mechanism.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the compiled data. Pibrentasvir is known to be an HCV NS5A inhibitor, and its efficacy in HCV is well established. Its role in treating HBV is not supported mechanistically.

HBV has no NS5A homologue, so there is no clear shared drug target. HBV and HCV are both hepatotropic viruses and cause chronic liver disease, which is probably why they sit close together in the knowledge graph. The score of 0.998 likely reflects this network proximity, not a true mechanistic link.

The literature retrieved concerns HCV treatment. Some general viral hepatitis reviews mention HBV, but none shows anti-HBV activity of pibrentasvir. The prediction should therefore be treated as a hypothesis only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There is no clinical or published evidence of anti-HBV activity, and no shared target between pibrentasvir (an HCV NS5A inhibitor) and HBV. The high TxGNN score likely reflects network proximity among viral hepatitis nodes, so this prediction is a model output only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- In vitro HBV antiviral activity data for pibrentasvir
- Mechanism of action data to support any proposed HBV target
- The approved indication text and Product Information for the Australian ARTG entry
- Safety review, including HBV reactivation considerations in HBV/HCV co-infected patients, using the TGA PI
