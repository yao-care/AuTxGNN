# Triptorelin: From Its Registered Australian Product to Precocious Puberty

## One-Sentence Summary

Triptorelin is a GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) agonist. The data compiled for this report do not list an approved indication for the Australian product.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is hypertrichosis, which has no real support. This report instead evaluates the best-supported prediction, **precocious puberty** (rank 8, score 99.89%), backed by **8 clinical trials** (5 completed Phase 3 studies in central precocious puberty) and **20 publications**.
Note that this is largely a label-aligned use in other jurisdictions rather than a novel repurposing.

**Note on the other top-10 predictions:** Apart from precocious puberty, none has meaningful clinical support. Hypertrichosis (rank 1) is supported only by a case report of ciclosporin-induced hypertrichosis in a patient who was taking triptorelin, which does not show a treatment effect. Familial male-limited precocious puberty has case-level reports only, and the more appropriate therapies act downstream of the GnRH axis. Aromatase excess syndrome has no clinical evidence (a research question only). The remaining predictions are model artefacts with no plausible mechanism (Hold).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Triptorelin is a well-characterised GnRH receptor agonist. Continuous exposure desensitises pituitary gonadotrophs and suppresses LH and FSH, and in turn sex steroids.

This mechanism directly counteracts **central precocious puberty (CPP)**, which is caused by premature activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis. GnRH agonists are the standard therapy for CPP. Several depot formulations (1-, 3- and 6-month) have been studied.

Guardrails on the reasoning: the evidence concerns central precocious puberty, not peripheral (gonadotropin-independent) forms. In familial male-limited precocious puberty, for example, the driver is an activating LHCGR mutation, so a GnRH agonist alone is not expected to work.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails** (for central precocious puberty only; all other top-10 predictions remain **Hold**, with aromatase excess syndrome a research question)

**Rationale:**
- Five completed Phase 3 trials and supporting literature support triptorelin in central precocious puberty, consistent with its GnRH agonist mechanism. The Phase 3 trials are open-label and single-arm, and conducted mainly in other populations (e.g. Chinese children).
- The Australian ARTG entry (Decapeptyl 100 microgram pre-filled syringe) has no indication text in the data compiled, so Australian label alignment is unconfirmed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of the approved indication and formulation for the Australian Decapeptyl product (the 100 microgram daily injection may not match the depot formulations studied in CPP trials)
- Review of the TGA Product Information for warnings and contraindications, and checking of the knowledge-graph signals against it
- Confirmation of CPP-specific dosing and the availability of 1-, 3- and 6-month depot formulations in Australia
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A monitoring plan for glucose, lipids, bone density and growth in children
