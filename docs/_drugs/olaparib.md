## Olaparib: From Ovarian Cancer to Female Breast Carcinoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Olaparib is an oral PARP inhibitor, originally used to treat BRCA-associated ovarian cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**, with **33 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this prediction, including several large randomised Phase 3 trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, olaparib is a PARP inhibitor. PARP inhibition produces synthetic lethality in tumours with BRCA1/2 or other homologous recombination repair deficiencies.

Germline BRCA-mutated breast cancer shares this vulnerability with BRCA-associated ovarian cancer, the original indication. Both cancers arise from the same underlying DNA repair defect, which is why the mechanism is expected to carry over.

This is not just a model prediction. Randomised Phase 3 trials (OlympiA in early breast cancer and OlympiAD in metastatic breast cancer) have reported benefit in patients with germline BRCA1/2 mutations and HER2-negative disease. Benefit is tied to BRCA/HRD status, so biomarker testing matters.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Large randomised Phase 3 trials (OlympiA and OlympiAD) support olaparib in germline BRCA-mutated, HER2-negative breast cancer, and the mechanism is shared with the original ovarian indication. Benefit depends on BRCA/HRD status. The evidence in patients without BRCA mutations is limited.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm the TGA-approved breast cancer indications and the PBS listing status for Lynparza, as the ARTG entry in the compiled data has no indication text
- Germline BRCA1/2 testing to select eligible patients
- Review of the PI for warnings, contraindications and dosing
- Mechanism of action data to complete the evidence record
- A medication review plan for the major interactions identified
- A monitoring plan for haematological toxicity

Other predicted indications (for example ovarian neoplasm and ovarian cancer susceptibility) largely overlap with the existing ovarian indication. The remaining ones (germ cell, mesothelioma, adenosarcoma, Sertoli-Leydig tumours and others) are model predictions only and should be held.
