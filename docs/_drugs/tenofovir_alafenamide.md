# Tenofovir Alafenamide: From HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) is an antiretroviral originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**,
with **no registered clinical trials** and **8 preclinical publications** (mainly macaque studies) currently supporting this direction.
SIV infection is an animal research model rather than a human disease, so this prediction mostly reflects a translational proxy for the already-approved HIV indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, TAF is a prodrug of tenofovir and a nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor active against HIV-1 and HIV-2. It delivers tenofovir diphosphate efficiently into lymphoid cells after oral administration.

SIV and the chimeric SHIV are closely related lentiviruses that use the same reverse transcriptase target as HIV. The antiviral mechanism is therefore directly applicable, and the high TxGNN score is consistent with this similarity to the original HIV indication.

However, SIV infection is a veterinary and preclinical research model, not a human disease. The macaque studies are best read as supporting evidence for HIV prevention and treatment, not as a new human therapeutic indication. The other predictions in the list (feline AIDS, and a rare neurodevelopmental disorder) have no supporting studies. The neurodevelopmental prediction has no plausible mechanistic link and is likely a knowledge-graph artefact.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only supporting evidence is preclinical (macaque and humanised mouse models), and SIV infection is not a human disease. It offers no new human therapeutic indication beyond the already-approved HIV indication. The other predicted indications have no supporting evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Reframing the question toward a human indication, such as HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis, if that is not already covered by current approvals
- Approved indication text and safety information (warnings, contraindications) from the TGA Product Information for each ARTG entry
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A review of whether any of the macaque PrEP or PEP findings have human trial counterparts
