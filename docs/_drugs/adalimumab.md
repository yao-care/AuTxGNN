# Adalimumab: From Crohn's Disease and Inflammatory Arthritis to Rheumatoid Vasculitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Adalimumab is an anti-TNF-alpha monoclonal antibody, originally used for Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, juvenile idiopathic arthritis, hidradenitis suppurativa and non-infectious uveitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Rheumatoid Vasculitis** (the top-ranked prediction), but support is thin: **1 clinical trial** that is only tangentially related and **case reports** that point in conflicting directions.
Of the ten predictions, only inflammatory spondylopathy and polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis have strong evidence, and both largely overlap with already-approved uses.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Adalimumab is known to be a fully human monoclonal antibody that neutralises TNF-alpha, a central driver of inflammation in rheumatoid arthritis and other immune-mediated diseases.

Rheumatoid vasculitis is a serious extra-articular complication of rheumatoid arthritis, an approved indication for adalimumab. Because TNF-alpha contributes to vascular inflammation, a link is plausible. However, the direction of effect is uncertain. One case report describes digital vasculitis responding well to adalimumab, and another describes a pulmonary hypertension crisis in rheumatoid vasculitis after adalimumab dose reduction. Other reports describe vasculitis, antiphospholipid syndrome, lupus-like reactions and ANCA-associated nephritis arising *during* anti-TNF therapy. TNF blockade can therefore both treat and trigger vasculitic phenomena.

**Other predictions in the top 10** (for context):
- **Inflammatory spondylopathy** (score 99.77%, L1): overlaps with the approved ankylosing spondylitis and spondyloarthropathy indications. It is better described as a label-concordant rediscovery than true repurposing.
- **Polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis** (score 99.72%, L1): also already covered by the juvenile idiopathic arthritis indication.
- **Vertebral disease** (L4): the term is too broad. The evidence found relates to inflammatory axial disease, which is already covered.
- **Hypermobility of coccyx, Kummell disease, and polyps of the middle ear, vocal cord, ureter and frontal sinus** (L5): no trials or literature and no plausible TNF-driven mechanism. These appear to be knowledge-graph artefacts.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions** (100 interactions were identified; the main ones are summarised below):
- **Major:** corticosteroids, including betamethasone, budesonide, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, prednisolone, prednisone and triamcinolone (including ophthalmic); deferiprone; and radiopharmaceuticals and related agents (ibritumomab tiuxetan, iobenguane I-131, tositumomab, tositumomab I-131).
- **Moderate:** metronidazole, tinidazole, rosuvastatin and simvastatin.
- **Minor:** zinc acetate, zinc gluconate and zinc sulfate.

**Safety signals from the literature:** infection and tuberculosis reactivation risk, and rare immune-mediated events such as lupus-like reactions, vasculitis and ANCA-associated nephritis. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for formal warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for rheumatoid vasculitis)

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link is plausible, but the evidence is limited to a few case reports with conflicting directions of effect and no relevant trials. Adalimumab can itself trigger vasculitic reactions, so benefit and harm are hard to separate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled or at least well-designed observational data on adalimumab in rheumatoid vasculitis.
- Detailed mechanism of action data.
- The ARTG approved indication text and the Product Information warnings and contraindications for Hadlima.

**Notes on other predictions:**
- *Inflammatory spondylopathy* and *polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis* are **Proceed with Guardrails** (L1). These are effectively label-concordant uses supported by multiple Phase 3 trials and a placebo-controlled RCT (PMID 18716298). Guardrails include confirming the disease subtype, weight-based paediatric dosing, infection and tuberculosis screening, and long-term safety monitoring.
- *Vertebral disease* is a research question: the disease definition needs narrowing first.
- All remaining predictions (hypermobility of coccyx, Kummell disease, and middle ear, vocal cord, ureter and frontal sinus polyps) should be **Held**, as they have no supporting evidence.
