## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, phylloquinone is a cofactor for gamma-glutamyl carboxylase, which activates vitamin K-dependent proteins such as osteocalcin and matrix Gla protein (MGP). These proteins are involved in bone mineralisation and in preventing soft-tissue calcification.

Renal tubular acidosis is commonly associated with nephrocalcinosis, bone demineralisation and altered calcium and phosphate handling. These features overlap with the drug's original indications (osteoporosis, nephrocalcinosis, hypercalcaemia). This gives a plausible but speculative link.

However, no direct mechanistic link is established. Phylloquinone does not address the primary defect in renal tubular acidosis, which is impaired tubular acid-base transport. The high TxGNN score is not backed by clinical trials or published studies.
