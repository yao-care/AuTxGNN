# Albendazole: From Cystic Echinococcosis and Cysticercosis to Alveolar Echinococcosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Albendazole is a benzimidazole anthelmintic, originally used to treat cystic echinococcosis (hydatid disease) and cysticercosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alveolar echinococcosis**,
with **3 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Albendazole is already the standard parasitostatic therapy for this condition in expert consensus guidelines.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the drug record. Based on known pharmacology, albendazole is a benzimidazole that binds parasite beta-tubulin, inhibiting microtubule polymerisation and glucose uptake.

Alveolar echinococcosis is caused by *Echinococcus multilocularis*, a close relative of *E. granulosus*, which causes cystic echinococcosis (an established indication). The same mechanism therefore applies. Expert consensus guidelines already recommend albendazole as the standard parasitostatic treatment for alveolar echinococcosis. This is a closely related, guideline-supported extension rather than a novel repurposing.

An important caveat: in alveolar echinococcosis albendazole appears to be parasitostatic rather than parasiticidal (PMIDs 19296876, 17631693). It can slow or stabilise disease, and long-term or lifelong treatment is often needed when surgery is not possible.

## Cytotoxicity

Not applicable. Albendazole is an anthelmintic, not an antineoplastic drug. Note that bone marrow suppression is a recognised concern (see Safety Considerations).

## Safety Considerations

Product Information warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full safety information.

- **Conditions flagged in the knowledge graph as contraindications** (from drug-disease relationships, not label text): liver disease, gallbladder disease, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia, macrocytic anaemia, pancytopenia, idiopathic aplastic anaemia and aplastic anaemia. Baseline and periodic full blood count and liver function monitoring is prudent, particularly with long-term use as in alveolar echinococcosis.
- **Drug interactions**: 96 interactions were identified. Examples listed as moderate include aprepitant, cobicistat, deferasirox, alemtuzumab, amprenavir, anagrelide, apalutamide, bendamustine, bexarotene, binimetinib, boceprevir and bosentan. Minor examples include cimetidine, dexamethasone, lidocaine and armodafinil. Several of these affect blood counts or drug metabolism, so a medication review is recommended.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Albendazole is already guideline-endorsed for alveolar echinococcosis, supported by a completed Phase 2 trial (n=194) and extensive review literature. Evidence is graded L2, but randomised controlled evidence is limited and the drug is parasitostatic rather than curative.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The TGA-approved Product Information (indications, warnings, contraindications) for the Eskazole listing, and confirmation of whether alveolar echinococcosis is covered by the approved indication
- Confirmation of the design and results of NCT07182305 (randomisation, control arm, outcomes)
- A monitoring plan for long-term use (full blood count, liver function)
- Medication review against the identified interactions
- Specialist (infectious diseases/hepatobiliary) input on surgery versus long-term drug therapy and criteria for stopping treatment

*Other predicted indications for albendazole in the data compiled:* coenurosis and intestinal helminthiasis are supported by L1 evidence, though these mostly restate established anthelmintic use or rely on related cestode disease trials. Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans, lung benign neoplasm, lung hilum carcinoma, fibroma of lung, hamartoma of lung and an inclusion body myopathy syndrome have little or no supporting evidence (L4 to L5) and are on Hold.
