## Minoxidil: From Hypertension to Hypotrichosis Simplex of the Scalp
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Minoxidil is a vasodilator, recorded in the knowledge graph for hypertensive disorders and alopecia. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp**, a rare hereditary hair loss disorder. Support is limited to **3 published case reports or case series** and **no registered clinical trials**.

**Note on evidence level:** The three publications do directly study hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp. However, all are single-patient or small case reports, mostly using minoxidil combined with other treatments (growth factors, botanical extracts, platelet-rich plasma). The data compiled for this report graded the evidence L4. Because the studies are case-level and confounded by co-treatment, I have taken the conservative view that they establish no more than a hypothesis. If the team accepts case reports as meeting the "directly studies the disease" threshold, the level would be L4.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, minoxidil is a potassium channel opener. Its active metabolite, minoxidil sulfate, is thought to prolong the anagen (growth) phase of the hair cycle and stimulate follicular proliferation and vascularisation.

Hereditary hypotrichosis simplex involves miniaturised or poorly sustained hair follicles. A drug that prolongs growth and supports follicles is therefore a plausible symptomatic treatment. The condition is caused by genetic variants (for example *CDSN*, *APCDD1*, *RPL21*), and minoxidil does not address these causes, so any benefit would likely be symptomatic rather than curative.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but the supporting evidence is three small combination-therapy case reports with no registered trials. Minoxidil does not address the genetic cause, and the only Australian licence is for a systemic oral tablet whose cardiovascular risks must be weighed against a cosmetic benefit in a rare, often paediatric, condition.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled or at least larger series data on minoxidil alone in hypotrichosis simplex
- Review of the Loniten PI for warnings and contraindications (currently a data gap)
- Mechanism of action data to support the mechanistic link
- A risk-benefit assessment of low-dose oral versus topical use, including paediatric safety
