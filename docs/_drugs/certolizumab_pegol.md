# Certolizumab Pegol: From Crohn's Disease and Inflammatory Arthritis to Rheumatoid Vasculitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Certolizumab pegol is a PEGylated anti-TNF agent originally used for Crohn's disease, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis and ankylosing spondylitis.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **rheumatoid vasculitis**, but the supporting evidence is **very weak**: no registered clinical trials and **7 publications**, almost all case reports, several of which describe vasculitis as a *side effect* of anti-TNF therapy.
The best-supported prediction in the list is actually **inflammatory spondylopathy** (rank 3), which largely overlaps with existing approved indications.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, certolizumab pegol is a PEGylated Fab' fragment that neutralises TNF-alpha. Its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis is established, and TNF-alpha is a shared inflammatory driver, so it may be mechanistically applicable to rheumatoid vasculitis, a complication of severe rheumatoid arthritis.

The link is uncertain, however. Only one case report (PMID 34786446) describes a benefit, in leg ulcers due to rheumatoid vasculitis. Several other reports describe anti-TNF-induced vasculitis (leukocytoclastic vasculitis, urticarial vasculitis, medium-vessel vasculitis) and glomerulonephritis after certolizumab pegol. This is a paradoxical safety signal that argues against simple extrapolation from the arthritis indications.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; the main ones are below):
- **Major:** corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisolone, prednisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone including the ophthalmic form), deferiprone, and radiopharmaceuticals (ibritumomab tiuxetan, iobenguane I-131, tositumomab I-131)
- **Moderate:** metronidazole, tinidazole, pegvaliase, rosuvastatin, simvastatin
- **Minor:** zinc salts (acetate, gluconate, sulfate)

**Additional signals from the literature and predictions:**
- Case reports describe vasculitis and glomerulonephritis as adverse reactions to certolizumab pegol.
- Anti-TNF therapy raises the risk of mycobacterial infection, including tuberculosis. The model's prediction for mendelian susceptibility to mycobacterial disease (IL12B deficiency, rank 7) is potentially harmful, not beneficial.
- Standard anti-TNF precautions apply: infection, tuberculosis screening, demyelinating disease and heart failure.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for rheumatoid vasculitis)

**Rationale:**
The evidence for rheumatoid vasculitis is limited to a single positive case report, outweighed by several case reports of vasculitis caused by the drug itself. The score is high, but the clinical support is minimal and the safety signal is paradoxical.

**Other predictions in the list:**
- **Inflammatory spondylopathy (rank 3): Proceed with Guardrails (L1).** Multiple completed Phase 3 randomised trials support it, but it is largely an on-label use rather than true repurposing. Guardrails are the standard anti-TNF precautions plus pregnancy and lactation data.
- **Polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis (rank 5): Research Question (L3).** One open-label, single-arm Phase 3 trial (NCT01550003, n=193) and a 9+-year follow-up publication support it.
- **Vertebral disease (rank 6):** Hold. Evidence is indirect and should probably be consolidated with inflammatory spondylopathy.
- **Tenosynovitis (rank 10):** Hold. It is a plausible TNF-related feature, but no specific trials or literature were identified.
- **Hypermobility of coccyx, Kummell disease, brachydactyly-syndactyly syndrome, colobomatous microphthalmia-rhizomelic dysplasia syndrome (ranks 2, 4, 8, 9):** Hold. Predictions only, with no plausible TNF mechanism.
- **IL12B deficiency (rank 7):** Hold, and avoid. Potential harm.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- TGA-approved Product Information warnings and contraindications, and the approved indication text for ARTG 10137M
- For rheumatoid vasculitis: controlled studies or a systematic case series, with a pharmacovigilance assessment of drug-induced vasculitis
- For juvenile arthritis: confirmation of the Australian regulatory status in paediatric use
