# Salmeterol: From Asthma/COPD Bronchodilator to Chronic Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Salmeterol is a long-acting β2-adrenergic agonist (LABA) established for maintenance bronchodilation in asthma and COPD. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Chronic Bronchitis**, with **16 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction — much of it evidence already generated for adjacent COPD indications.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on known pharmacology, salmeterol is a long-acting β2-adrenergic receptor agonist that activates airway smooth muscle β2 receptors, raising intracellular cAMP and producing sustained bronchodilation while also inhibiting mast-cell mediator release. This mechanism is already the pharmacological basis for its established use in asthma and COPD.

Chronic bronchitis is one of the classic clinical phenotypes of COPD, characterised by chronic airflow obstruction, cough, sputum production and impaired mucociliary clearance. LABA-induced bronchodilation directly targets the airflow-limitation component of this pathology, and supporting literature (e.g. PMID 15970448) shows salmeterol also improves mucociliary and cough clearance — a mechanism specifically relevant to the mucus-retention pathology of chronic bronchitis. Because chronic bronchitis sits within the same disease spectrum as COPD and asthma (both of which are salmeterol's recognised use domains), the mechanistic link is direct rather than speculative.

It is worth noting that much of the identified evidence involves salmeterol as part of a fixed-dose combination with fluticasone propionate (e.g. Seretide/Advair), rather than salmeterol monotherapy — this should be considered when interpreting the strength of drug-specific evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 (and supportive Phase 2/4) randomised trials, including several directly involving salmeterol or salmeterol/fluticasone combinations, support efficacy in COPD and chronic bronchitis, meeting the L1 evidence bar. However, salmeterol has no current ARTG registration in this dataset and this specific drug-level evidence pack is missing PI safety data, so guardrails are required before clinical application.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TGA Product Information (warnings, precautions, contraindications) — currently a Blocking data gap preventing safety pre-screening
- Confirmed mechanism of action (MOA) data from DrugBank — currently a High-severity data gap
- Clarification of Australian ARTG/market status for salmeterol (monotherapy vs combination products)
- Confirmation of drug-specific (not just combination-product) efficacy data, since several trials evaluate salmeterol/fluticasone combinations rather than salmeterol alone
