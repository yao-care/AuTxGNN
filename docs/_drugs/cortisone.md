## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. As a class, corticosteroids have lympholytic activity: they act through the glucocorticoid receptor and can trigger apoptosis in lymphoid cells. This offers a plausible biological link to T-cell lymphoma.

The link is general rather than specific to cortisone. The literature on this disease consists of mid-20th-century case reports on mycosis fungoides, the most common form of cutaneous T-cell lymphoma. No modern study tests cortisone in this condition. The mechanistic argument therefore rests on the glucocorticoid class, not on evidence for cortisone itself.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No cited study directly tests cortisone in primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma in a controlled or modern setting. The only evidence is 1950s–1960s case reports, and the single linked trial concerns a different (B-cell) lymphoma. The prediction is therefore at the model-prediction level (L5), with a plausible but class-level glucocorticoid mechanism.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for cortisone specifically
- Contemporary clinical evidence for cortisone, or for systemic corticosteroids more broadly, in cutaneous T-cell lymphoma
