## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on general pharmacological knowledge, racementhol is a topical agent that activates TRPM8 cold receptors, producing a cooling sensation that is widely used to blunt itch and mild pain signalling — this is the basis of its established, non-prescription use in antipruritic and rubefacient products.

Neurodermatitis (lichen simplex chronicus) is a chronic itch–scratch cycle condition where itch relief is the primary therapeutic target. Menthol's TRPM8-mediated antipruritic action gives this pairing more mechanistic coherence than any of the other nine TxGNN candidates reviewed, which involve autoimmune, infectious, or vasculitic pathology (e.g. dermatomyositis variants, post-infectious vasculitis, Lyme-related skin disease) with no plausible link to a topical cooling agent.

That said, mechanistic plausibility is not clinical evidence. No trials or publications specifically test racementhol/menthol in neurodermatitis, so this remains a research hypothesis rather than a validated repurposing candidate.
