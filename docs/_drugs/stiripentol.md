# Stiripentol: From Dravet Syndrome to West Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Stiripentol is an antiepileptic GABAergic drug whose only established indication (per the literature in this evidence pack) is Dravet syndrome, a severe infantile epileptic encephalopathy. The TxGNN model predicts a possible role in **West syndrome** (infantile spasms), another infantile epileptic encephalopathy, but **no clinical trials and no publications directly study this indication** — the 18 papers retrieved all discuss Dravet syndrome or general epileptic-encephalopathy management, so the link is currently analogical, not evidential.

*Note on candidate selection*: TxGNN's highest-scoring prediction for this drug was restless legs syndrome (98.76%), but the evidence pack itself flags that link as mechanistically implausible with zero supporting trials or literature, and recommends it be treated as noise. West syndrome, ranked lower by score (94.97%) but the only candidate with any supporting literature and a coherent mechanistic rationale, is used as the subject of this report instead.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for stiripentol was not available from DrugBank in this evidence pack. Based on the literature retrieved, stiripentol is a GABA-A receptor positive allosteric modulator, and its only clinically established use is as an adjunct antiepileptic for Dravet syndrome — a severe, drug-resistant developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE) of infancy driven predominantly by SCN1A mutations that impair GABAergic interneuron function.

West syndrome (infantile spasms) sits in the same broad DEE spectrum as Dravet syndrome: both present in infancy with drug-resistant seizures, neurodevelopmental comorbidity, and a shared conceptual framework of "epileptic encephalopathy" in which ongoing epileptic activity itself drives developmental deterioration. This shared disease category is the basis of the TxGNN prediction and gives it biological plausibility.

However, one retrieved review (PMID 26933940) is explicit that stiripentol and vigabatrin are each approved for a *different* infantile-onset epilepsy — stiripentol for Dravet syndrome, vigabatrin for infantile spasms — meaning the two drugs are not interchangeable in current clinical practice. No paper in this evidence pack reports stiripentol use, efficacy, or safety data specifically in West syndrome. The prediction should therefore be read as a mechanistically plausible hypothesis drawn from disease-category proximity, not as evidence of clinical effect in West syndrome itself.

## Conclusion and Next Steps
