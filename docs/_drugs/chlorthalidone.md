## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Chlorthalidone is a thiazide-like diuretic that acts on ion transport and fluid balance, and it is established as an antihypertensive.

A diuretic could in theory lower intraocular pressure through effects on ion transport or fluid balance. This is speculative and is not supported by any data provided. Primary hereditary glaucoma is largely driven by genetic defects in aqueous outflow, so a systemic diuretic mechanism is not a clear fit. The high score should be treated as a model signal that needs verification, not as evidence of benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations of concern:** The data compiled for this report list conditions in which chlorthalidone is flagged as contraindicated or to be used with caution. These come from model-source relations, not from label text. They include:
  - Kidney and liver: kidney disease, liver failure, anuria
  - Metabolic: gout, hyperuricaemia, type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypercalcaemia, potassium deficiency
  - Cardiovascular: myocardial infarction, sudden cardiac arrest
  - Other: pancreatitis, peptic ulcer disease, systemic lupus erythematosus, asthma, psoriasis, epilepsy
- **Drug interactions:** 210 interactions were recorded. Major-level interactions include **cisapride** and **dolasetron**. Many moderate-level interactions involve glucose-lowering agents (metformin, acarbose, alogliptin, albiglutide), corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, triamcinolone, beclomethasone), amphotericin B, bupropion, morphine, and the proton pump inhibitors omeprazole and rabeprazole. Minor interactions include atropine, hyoscyamine and doxycycline.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for authoritative warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

The prediction for primary hereditary glaucoma has no supporting trials or publications, and the proposed mechanism is speculative and a poor fit for a genetically driven outflow disorder. Evidence is L5 (model prediction only).
