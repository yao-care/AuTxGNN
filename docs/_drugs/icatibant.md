# Icatibant: From Hereditary Angioedema to C1 Inhibitor Deficiency

## One-Sentence Summary

Icatibant is a bradykinin B2 receptor antagonist, originally used to treat acute attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **C1 inhibitor deficiency**, a closely related condition that is essentially the drug's approved use,
with **11 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Icatibant is a selective antagonist of the bradykinin B2 receptor (with pharmacology data also listing the B1 receptor). Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank are not available in the data compiled for this report, but the receptor pharmacology is well established.

C1 inhibitor deficiency, whether hereditary or acquired, leads to uncontrolled activation of the kallikrein-kinin system. This produces excess bradykinin, which drives the swelling attacks. Blocking the B2 receptor addresses this pathway directly.

This prediction is essentially the drug's existing use rather than a true repurposing case. The only extension is acquired C1 inhibitor deficiency, which shares the same bradykinin-mediated mechanism. Support for that group comes mainly from review articles and a small real-world observational series (8 patients, 48 attacks treated), not from controlled trials.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions** (12 records returned; 10 rated Moderate):
- **ACE inhibitors (Moderate):** benazepril, captopril, enalapril, fosinopril, lisinopril, moexipril, perindopril, quinapril, ramipril and trandolapril. The pharmacology of the combination is clinically relevant, because ACE inhibitors raise bradykinin levels and icatibant blocks its receptor.
- **Receptor pharmacology records:** bradykinin B1 and B2 receptors (the drug's targets, not clinical interactions).

No warnings or contraindications were available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 trials, including randomised placebo-controlled and active-comparator studies, support icatibant in HAE with C1 inhibitor deficiency, and the drug is already marketed in Australia. This indication is largely the drug's existing use, so the main uncertainty is the extension to acquired C1 inhibitor deficiency, which rests on small observational data and reviews.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The approved indication text and PI warnings and contraindications for ARTG 1976B (currently missing)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Controlled or larger prospective data for acquired C1 inhibitor deficiency
- A monitoring plan for patients taking ACE inhibitors

**Other predicted indications:** The nine lower-ranked predictions (for example serpinopathy with toxic serpin polymerisation, pseudo-von Willebrand disease and Glanzmann thrombasthenia) have no supporting trials or literature and no plausible mechanistic link to B2 receptor antagonism. They are model predictions only (L5), and the recommendation for each is **Hold**.
