# Valaciclovir: From Herpes Simplex/Varicella-Zoster Infections to Rheumatoid Arthritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Valaciclovir is an antiviral agent (the oral prodrug of aciclovir) established for treating herpes simplex and varicella-zoster virus infections. The TxGNN model predicts a possible link to **Rheumatoid Arthritis**, but the supporting evidence — **2 clinical trials (both unrelated mismatches) and 16 publications** — largely describes herpesvirus reactivation as a *complication* of immunosuppressive RA therapy, not evidence that valaciclovir treats RA itself.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for valaciclovir is not available in this evidence pack (data gap DG002). Based on general pharmacological knowledge, valaciclovir is a nucleoside analogue antiviral that is rapidly converted to aciclovir, which inhibits viral DNA polymerase to suppress HSV and VZV replication. This mechanism has no established, direct anti-inflammatory or immunomodulatory action relevant to autoimmune joint disease.

The literature returned for this prediction does **not** support valaciclovir as a treatment for rheumatoid arthritis. Instead, it consistently describes the reverse relationship: patients with RA who are treated with immunosuppressive agents (TNF-alpha antagonists, tofacitinib, methotrexate) experience herpesvirus reactivation (herpes zoster, HSV keratitis, VZV reinfection) as an adverse consequence of their RA treatment, and valaciclovir is used to *treat that complication* — not the RA itself. The evidence pack's own mechanistic assessment flags this explicitly: unless one invokes an unproven "virus-triggers-autoimmunity" hypothesis, there is no supported causal pathway for antiviral therapy improving RA outcomes.

Both clinical trials returned by the search (a wet AMD real-world safety study and an aplastic anaemia trial) are unrelated to either valaciclovir or RA and appear to be indexing/matching artefacts rather than genuine supporting evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The available evidence does not support a therapeutic mechanism linking valaciclovir to rheumatoid arthritis — the literature instead documents herpesvirus reactivation as a *side effect* of RA immunosuppressive therapy, with valaciclovir used reactively to treat that infection. Both matched clinical trials are unrelated to either the drug or the indication. This is a low-confidence, model-only signal (evidence level L4) that does not currently justify progression.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TGA-approved Product Information (warnings, contraindications, DDIs) — currently a blocking data gap
- Verified mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A clearly defined, testable hypothesis (e.g., antiviral prophylaxis to reduce viral-reactivation complications during RA immunosuppressive therapy, rather than valaciclovir as an RA treatment per se)
- Dedicated observational or controlled studies specifically designed to test that hypothesis, rather than incidental case reports
