# Carboplatin: From Ovarian Cancer to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Carboplatin is a platinum-based chemotherapy drug, originally used to treat ovarian cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**, particularly triple-negative and BRCA-associated disease.
The data compiled for this report include **40 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** on this indication. These include several randomised phase 2 and phase 3 studies of carboplatin-containing regimens.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Carboplatin is a platinum agent that is known to damage tumour DNA by cross-linking, and its efficacy in ovarian cancer is established. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to breast cancer.

Cancers with defective DNA repair (homologous recombination deficiency) are particularly vulnerable to DNA-damaging platinum drugs. This biology is shared by BRCA-associated and triple-negative breast cancers and by many ovarian cancers. This is the most plausible link between the original and the predicted indication.

Randomised phase 2 neoadjuvant trials (GeparSixto, CALGB 40603, BrighTNess) and a randomised phase 2 trial against docetaxel in metastatic BRCA-related disease support this link. No completed phase 3 trial of carboplatin itself in breast cancer is included in the data, so L1 is not assigned. Two recent phase 3-type publications (PMIDs 41436191 and 41115255) report on adding carboplatin in triple-negative breast cancer.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions flagged as contraindication-related in the drug knowledge graph (not label text):** kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia), blood coagulation disease, peripheral neuropathy and other peripheral nervous system disease, hearing and auditory system disorders, and functional visual loss.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found. Those shown are mostly moderate, including:
  - proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, rabeprazole, dexlansoprazole)
  - aminoglycosides and related antibiotics (kanamycin, neomycin, streptomycin, vancomycin)
  - metronidazole and tinidazole
  - statins (rosuvastatin, simvastatin)
  - levofloxacin (minor)

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several randomised phase 2 and phase 3 trials of carboplatin-containing regimens support activity in breast cancer, particularly triple-negative and BRCA-associated disease. However, most trials test combination regimens or an added agent rather than carboplatin alone. No completed phase 3 trial of carboplatin itself is included in the data, so the evidence level is L2 rather than L1.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Restrict use to triple-negative or BRCA-mutated subtypes until broader evidence is available.
- Review the long-term survival data from the recent phase 3-type publications (PMIDs 41436191 and 41115255) before any broad recommendation.
- Obtain the TGA-approved Product Information for Carboplatin Accord (warnings, contraindications and approved indications), which is missing from the data.
- Obtain detailed mechanism of action data.
- Put in place a haematological, renal, neurological and hearing monitoring plan.
