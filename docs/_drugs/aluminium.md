# Aluminium: From an Unspecified Original Indication to Peptic Ulcer Disease

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on the published literature, aluminium-containing compounds (aluminium hydroxide antacids and sucralfate, a basic aluminium salt of sucrose octasulfate) neutralise gastric acid and reduce peptic activity. They are also reported to provide mucosal cytoprotection, through stimulation of bicarbonate secretion, release of endogenous prostaglandins and formation of a protective barrier at the ulcer site.

This prediction is better described as recognition of existing practice than true repurposing. Reviews report that aluminium-based antacids heal duodenal ulcers faster than placebo. Evidence in gastric ulcer is less consistent, and results are comparable to H2 blockers in several studies. Sucralfate 1 g four times daily was reported to increase the healing rate of duodenal and gastric ulcers over 4 to 8 weeks.

Other predicted indications ranked lower. Gastroduodenitis (score 97.23%) has only indirect, mostly old evidence at L4. Dermatitis (score 96.70%) is not supported, because aluminium is a contact allergen and vaccine adjuvant that causes dermatitis and itchy granulomas. The remaining predictions have no meaningful support.

## Safety Considerations

- **Aluminium accumulation:** Volunteer data show aluminium can accumulate in liver tissue and cerebrospinal fluid with short-course anti-ulcer therapy, and serum aluminium changes have been reported with sucralfate.
- **Renal impairment:** Reduced elimination may increase aluminium exposure in patients with impaired kidney function.
- **Neurological signal:** An epidemiological study examined dementia mortality in gastroduodenal ulcer patients with high aluminium-antacid intake. This is a safety signal, not proof of harm.

No drug interactions were identified in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Aluminium-containing antacids and sucralfate are established treatments for peptic ulcer disease, supported by controlled trials and reviews, so the indication is credible. However, there are no registered trials, much of the evidence is dated, and aluminium accumulation and renal safety concerns need active management.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The approved indication, dosage form and Product Information for ARTG 7642F, which are missing from the compiled data and block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A safety plan for patients with renal impairment and for long-term use, including aluminium exposure limits
- Comparison against current standard care (proton pump inhibitors and *H. pylori* eradication)
