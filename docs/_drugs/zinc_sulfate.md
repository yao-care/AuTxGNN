## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Zinc is generally recognised for local antiviral and mucosal-protective effects. Zinc lozenges release zinc ions directly in the oropharynx. Zinc may also support mucosal repair and taste and sensory recovery.

These properties make a role in throat inflammation plausible. However, the supporting evidence concerns sore throat after intubation and radiation-related throat injury, not infectious pharyngitis. The mechanistic link is therefore plausible but indirect.

## Safety Considerations

Warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for safety information.

**Drug Interactions** (100 interactions were identified; the main ones are listed):
- **Moderate:** alendronic acid, risedronic acid, ibandronate, tetracycline, baloxavir marboxil
- **Minor:** many immunosuppressants and biologics (for example abatacept, adalimumab, azathioprine, baricitinib) and corticosteroids (for example hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone)

Zinc is known to bind some oral drugs in the gut, so dose timing relative to bisphosphonates and tetracyclines deserves attention.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L2. One randomised placebo-controlled trial of zinc lozenges reduced postoperative sore throat, but a randomised trial in radiation-induced pharyngitis showed no benefit. No study has tested zinc in infectious pharyngitis, and the safety data are incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A randomised trial of zinc lozenges or a similar formulation in infectious pharyngitis
- Mechanism of action data
- Review of the PI for warnings, contraindications and dose limits
- Confirmation of a suitable local formulation and route (for example lozenge)
- Guidance on interaction management, particularly timing with bisphosphonates and tetracyclines
