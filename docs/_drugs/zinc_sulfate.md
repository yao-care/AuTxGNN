## One-Sentence Summary

Zinc sulfate is a zinc salt used as a mineral supplement. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **pharyngitis** (score 0.9985). The supporting evidence is **1 completed clinical trial** and **2 publications**, and both are indirect (post-surgical and radiotherapy-related sore throat rather than infectious pharyngitis).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Zinc has local antiviral and mucosal-protective properties. Zinc lozenges release zinc ions directly in the oropharynx, where they may support mucosal repair and recovery of taste and sensation. This makes a benefit in throat inflammation biologically plausible.

The link is still indirect. The available studies cover sore throat after intubation and radiotherapy-related throat inflammation, not common infectious pharyngitis. The prediction should therefore be treated as a research question rather than an established use.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions:** 100 potential interactions were identified in the interaction data compiled for this report. The moderate-level interactions in the listed sample are:
- Alendronic acid
- Risedronic acid
- Ibandronate
- Tetracycline
- Baloxavir marboxil

These are likely to involve reduced absorption when taken together with zinc, so dose timing should be considered. Other listed interactions are minor, including corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone) and several immunosuppressants and biologics.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
One completed randomised placebo-controlled trial (post-operative sore throat) and one randomised trial in radiation-induced pharyngitis support an L2 evidence level. Neither addresses common infectious pharyngitis, and the trial has no posted results. This is not yet enough to support moving forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Posted or published results from NCT02405832, and full-text review of PMID 23720981 for the actual effect
- A controlled trial in infectious pharyngitis, using a zinc lozenge or other oropharyngeal formulation
- Mechanism-of-action data for zinc sulfate
- Review of the PI for warnings, contraindications and dosing, plus an interaction-timing plan for medicines that bind zinc
- Confirmation of the approved indication, product and dosage form details for the Australian licence
