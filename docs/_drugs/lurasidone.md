# Lurasidone: From Bipolar Disorder and Schizophrenia to Manic Bipolar Affective Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Lurasidone is an atypical antipsychotic originally used to treat bipolar disorder and schizophrenia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **manic bipolar affective disorder**,
but only **1 clinical trial** (withdrawn, with no participants enrolled) and **20 publications** (mostly reviews, guidelines and meta-analyses, none directly demonstrating efficacy in mania) are available, so evidence is limited.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, lurasidone is an atypical antipsychotic that antagonises D2, 5-HT2A and 5-HT7 receptors and is a partial agonist at 5-HT1A. This profile is inferred from general knowledge, not from the supplied data.

The original indications (bipolar disorder and schizophrenia) overlap closely with the predicted indication. Manic episodes are plausibly responsive to D2/5-HT2A blockade, as seen with other atypical antipsychotics. However, the high TxGNN score is consistent with the known bipolar indication and is not independent evidence for efficacy in mania. Much of the published bipolar literature on lurasidone concerns bipolar *depression*, which is a different clinical target. One case report describes lurasidone-induced mania.

## Safety Considerations

Label-based warnings and contraindications are not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for these.

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (not label text): orthostatic hypotension and related autonomic conditions, liver disease and failure, epilepsy, diabetes mellitus and related conditions (hyperglycaemia, diabetic ketoacidosis), hyperlipidaemia, neutropenia, leukopenia and agranulocytosis, cerebrovascular disease (stroke, transient ischaemic attack, cerebral infarction), renal impairment, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, tardive dyskinesia, hyperprolactinaemia and prolactin-producing pituitary tumour, and gallbladder disease.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were identified; the first 20 returned include:
  - **Major:** bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, clarithromycin.
  - **Moderate:** antidiabetic agents (acarbose, alogliptin, metformin, pioglitazone, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide, albiglutide), anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium), epinephrine, dexamethasone, lorcaserin, cimetidine, and bowel-preparation agents (picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol).
  - Interaction severities come from the DDInter database; the strong CYP3A4 inhibitor clarithromycin is of particular practical relevance.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only trial for this indication was withdrawn with no participants, and the supporting literature mostly addresses bipolar depression or general bipolar management rather than mania. The TxGNN score is not independent evidence, and label safety data are missing. Lower-ranked predictions (ranks 2-10, such as retinal dystrophy and hydranencephaly) have no trials, no literature and no supported mechanism, so they are not pursued.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the TGA Product Information and the current ARTG-approved indications, including whether mania is covered
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank)
- Targeted review of randomised trial data for lurasidone in acute mania, as distinct from bipolar depression
- Safety assessment for manic presentations, given the reported lurasidone-induced mania and the metabolic, cardiovascular and drug-interaction signals above
- If evidence supports it, a prospective trial design, possibly registered with ANZCTR
