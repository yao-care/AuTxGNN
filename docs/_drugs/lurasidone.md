# Lurasidone: From Schizophrenia/Bipolar Depression to Manic Bipolar Affective Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Lurasidone is an atypical (second-generation) antipsychotic used internationally for schizophrenia and depressive episodes of Bipolar I disorder (marketed overseas as Latuda; not currently registered in Australia). The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Manic Bipolar Affective Disorder**, supported by **15 clinical trials** and **20 publications** — though much of this evidence reflects Lurasidone's already-established role across the bipolar disorder spectrum rather than a genuinely novel mechanism-driven indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack (data gap DG002). Based on publicly available drug information, Lurasidone acts primarily as an antagonist at dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A/5-HT7 receptors, with partial agonist activity at 5-HT1A receptors — a receptor profile shared with other atypical antipsychotics used across the mood-disorder spectrum.

Bipolar disorder encompasses both depressive and manic/mixed episodes as phases of the same underlying illness. Lurasidone is already internationally approved for depressive episodes of Bipolar I disorder, so the predicted extension to manic bipolar affective disorder sits within the same disease category rather than representing an unrelated therapeutic area. This is consistent with the trial record: several Phase 3 studies of Lurasidone specifically enrolled Bipolar I patients for mania prevention, relapse/recurrence prevention, and adjunctive therapy with lithium or divalproex — indications closely related to acute mania.

Because much of the supporting evidence was generated for the already-recognised bipolar-depression indication, this candidate should be interpreted as **consolidating and extending an established drug class effect** within bipolar disorder, rather than as a mechanistically novel repurposing signal.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Clinical trial evidence for Lurasidone in bipolar disorder is strong (four or more completed Phase 3 randomised, placebo-controlled trials, Evidence Level L1), but the candidate cannot proceed past initial safety screening because TGA-approved product information (warnings and contraindications) has not yet been retrieved — a Blocking data gap. Lurasidone is also not currently registered or marketed in Australia (0 ARTG entries).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TGA-approved Product Information (warnings, precautions, contraindications) to unblock the S1 safety assessment (DG001)
- Detailed mechanism of action data via DrugBank (DG002)
- Drug interaction (DDI) profile, as the current query returned no results
- Confirmation of the Australian regulatory pathway/status, given the drug is not presently marketed here
- Clarification of how much incremental value this "new indication" offers, given Lurasidone's existing international approval for related bipolar depression
