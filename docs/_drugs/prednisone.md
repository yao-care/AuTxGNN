## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for prednisone are not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacology, prednisone is a systemic glucocorticoid with broad immunosuppressive and anti-inflammatory effects.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune attack on the hair follicle that breaks down the follicle's immune privilege. By suppressing T-cell activation and inflammatory cytokine production, prednisone could plausibly interrupt this process. Systemic corticosteroids have been used for this condition since the 1950s to 1970s.

There are important limits. Hair regrowth is often not sustained after the drug is withdrawn, and long-term systemic steroid toxicity restricts use. More recent work uses low-dose prednisone as an adjunct to methotrexate or baricitinib rather than as a stand-alone treatment.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction search returned 100 records, of which the main ones are listed. A major-level interaction was recorded with adalimumab. Moderate-level interactions include zidovudine, isotretinoin, acarbose, acebutolol, acetazolamide, ketorolac, nifedipine, ibuprofen, ethinylestradiol, butalbital, albiglutide, aldesleukin, alefacept, alemtuzumab and aliskiren; tetanus toxoid vaccine also has a moderate-level record. Formoterol and salbutamol interactions are minor.
- **Other safety points from the literature**: Older studies report acne, obesity, lenticular opacities and mild hypertension with prolonged systemic prednisone, and relapse after withdrawal.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Alopecia areata has a plausible immune mechanism for glucocorticoid action and a double-blind randomised trial studying low-dose prednisone as an adjunct. However, regrowth is often not sustained, long-term steroid toxicity is a concern, and the evidence is mainly small or older studies rather than completed Phase 3 trials.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications (not yet available in the compiled data)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Controlled data on prednisone as part of a regimen, with relapse after withdrawal as an outcome
- A monitoring plan for steroid adverse effects, with attention to hypertension, hyperglycaemia and infection risk, and review of interacting medicines such as adalimumab
