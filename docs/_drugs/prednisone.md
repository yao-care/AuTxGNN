## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, prednisone is a systemic glucocorticoid, its efficacy in several autoimmune and inflammatory conditions is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to alopecia areata.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune attack on the hair follicle that breaks the follicle's immune privilege. Prednisone suppresses T-cell activation and inflammatory cytokine production, so it plausibly interrupts this process. This links directly to its original use in autoimmune disease.

Systemic steroids have been used for alopecia areata since the 1950s to 1970s. However, hair regrowth is often not sustained after withdrawal, and long-term steroid toxicity limits use. Recent work uses low-dose prednisone as an adjunct to methotrexate or baricitinib.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions (from knowledge-graph contraindication links, not label text):** hypertension, congestive heart failure, hyperglycaemia, epilepsy, herpes simplex virus keratitis, measles, certain urea cycle disorders (such as carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I deficiency, citrullinaemia and hyperargininaemia), and pituitary hormone deficiency disorders.
- **Drug Interactions (100 interactions identified; selected examples):**
  - **Major:** adalimumab.
  - **Moderate:** zidovudine, isotretinoin, acarbose, acebutolol, acetazolamide, ketorolac, ibuprofen, nifedipine, ethinylestradiol, butalbital, albiglutide, aldesleukin, alemtuzumab, aliskiren and tetanus toxoid vaccine.
  - **Minor:** formoterol and salbutamol.
- **Literature-reported concerns:** one follow-up study of prednisone for alopecia areata noted acne, obesity, lenticular opacities and mild hypertension. Hair regrowth is often not sustained after withdrawal.

Please also refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Prednisone has a plausible immunological rationale and a long history of use in alopecia areata, and a recent randomised trial used it as an adjunct. However, evidence is limited to small studies, case series and one add-on trial, with no registered trials of prednisone itself. Relapse after withdrawal and long-term steroid toxicity limit its role.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for prednisone
- Product Information warnings and contraindications for the Australian product (Panafcort), as these were not available for this report
- Controlled trials of prednisone for alopecia areata, including dose, duration and relapse after withdrawal
- A monitoring plan for steroid adverse effects (blood pressure, glucose, bone health, infection), especially with long-term or repeated courses
- Review of interactions with immunosuppressants and biologics where combination therapy is considered
