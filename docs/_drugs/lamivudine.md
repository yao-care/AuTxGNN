## Lamivudine: From Chronic Hepatitis B and HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Lamivudine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor, originally used to treat chronic hepatitis B and HIV infection (including AIDS).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, but the supporting evidence is **animal-model literature only (15 publications, mostly macaque studies) and no registered clinical trials**.
This prediction largely restates lamivudine's existing anti-HIV activity in an animal model and is not a new human indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, lamivudine is a cytosine nucleoside analogue. Its active triphosphate form inhibits viral reverse transcriptase and terminates the growing DNA chain.

SIV is a lentivirus closely related to HIV and uses a similar reverse transcriptase enzyme. Activity against SIV is therefore biologically plausible. The macaque studies in the literature use lamivudine as part of combination antiretroviral regimens, and they have also studied the M184V resistance mutation that lamivudine selects.

The prediction does not describe a new human use. SIV infects non-human primates, so this is essentially the original HIV indication modelled in animals. It has little bearing on a human repurposing decision.

**Other predictions in the top 10** were reviewed and are summarised here for context.
- **Feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (rank 2):** the evidence is veterinary only, with no human trials.
- **Hepatitis B virus infection (rank 6):** this is already an approved indication, so it validates the model rather than offering a new opportunity. It has the strongest evidence (L1, with multiple completed Phase 3 trials, mostly using lamivudine as a comparator), and it would be the only prediction to reach "Proceed with Guardrails". Resistance (YMDD mutations) is common with monotherapy, and entecavir or tenofovir are now preferred first-line agents.
- **Hepatitis C virus infection (ranks 5 and 7):** there is no mechanistic basis, because lamivudine does not inhibit the HCV NS5B polymerase. The literature is general hepatitis reviews, and the trials are in HCV/HIV co-infected patients where lamivudine treats the HIV.
- **Ranks 3, 4, 8, 9 and 10 (neurodevelopmental disorder, obsolete familial combined hyperlipidaemia, idiopathic copper-associated cirrhosis, hepatoportal sclerosis, primitive portal vein thrombosis):** model prediction only, with no trials, no literature and no identifiable mechanistic link.

## Safety Considerations

The knowledge graph links lamivudine to the following conditions as contraindication or caution signals. This is graph-derived information and not product label text.
- **Liver and biliary:** liver failure, liver disease, fatty liver disease, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease
- **Renal:** kidney disease, pyoureter
- **Haematological:** neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia)
- **Muscle:** myopathy, muscular disease, skeletal muscle disease, myositis, dermatomyositis, familial periodic paralysis
- **Metabolic:** lactic acidosis, hypertriglyceridaemia, obesity disorder, monogenic obesity

Other listed interactions are of unknown severity (for example metformin, omeprazole, pantoprazole, lansoprazole, cimetidine, morphine, vancomycin, amphotericin B and glimepiride).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The SIV prediction is supported only by macaque and in vitro studies, with no human clinical trials. Lamivudine is already approved for HIV, so this is not a genuine new human indication. The evidence level is L4.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A clear decision on whether the SIV/animal-model prediction has any human relevance. If not, deprioritise it.
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Approved indication text for the ARTG entry and the TGA Product Information safety content
- If a human repurposing opportunity is wanted, evaluate other predictions on their merits. Hepatitis B is the only one with substantial trial evidence, and it is already an approved use.
