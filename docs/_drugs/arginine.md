## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for arginine is not available in the compiled data. The rationale below rests on general nitric oxide (NO) biology rather than on drug-specific mechanism data.

L-arginine is the substrate for nitric oxide synthase (nNOS) in the nitrergic neurons of the gut. NO-mediated relaxation is important for gastric accommodation and for relaxation of the pyloric sphincter. If this pathway is impaired, gastric emptying may be delayed.

Preclinical studies are consistent with this idea:
- In mice, glucocorticoid-induced gastroparesis was linked to depletion of L-arginine (PMID 25057793).
- In a Parkinson's disease rat model, nitrergic relaxation in the pyloric sphincter was impaired (PMID 35380456).
- In diabetic BB-rats, myenteric neuropathy with altered nNOS was described (PMID 18312542).

Together these suggest that arginine supplementation is biologically plausible. However, there are no human efficacy data. The remaining retrieved papers concern gastric electrical stimulation, sacral nerve stimulation, hyperglycaemia, anaphylaxis and ghrelin, and relate to arginine only indirectly. The very high TxGNN score is a computational prediction, not evidence of clinical benefit.

## Safety Considerations

No label-based warnings, contraindications or drug interaction data were available in the compiled data. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for safety information.

The TxGNN knowledge graph (not label text) links arginine to the following conditions as possible contraindications. These should be treated as signals for review, not confirmed label contraindications:
- **Renal:** kidney disease, chronic kidney disease, chronic renal failure, end-stage renal failure, nephrolithiasis, nephrocalcinosis, acute urate nephropathy
- **Acid-base and mineral metabolism:** metabolic acidosis, renal tubular acidosis, acidosis disorder, hypercalcaemia, humoral hypercalcaemia of malignancy, hyperparathyroidism, osteitis fibrosa, sarcoidosis, phosphorus metabolism disorders
- **Cardiovascular:** heart disease, cardiovascular disease, ventricular fibrillation, digitalis poisoning
- **Haematological:** sickle cell anaemia, hereditary persistence of fetal haemoglobin
- **Metabolic:** phenylketonuria and other disorders of phenylalanine metabolism
- **Other:** constipation, lytic metastatic bone lesions

Many of these (particularly renal impairment, cardiac disease and electrolyte disturbance) are relevant to people with gastroparesis, so they would need careful screening in any future study.
