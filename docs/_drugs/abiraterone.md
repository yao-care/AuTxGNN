## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Abiraterone is known to inhibit CYP17A1 and suppress androgen biosynthesis, and it is always given with a corticosteroid (prednisone, prednisolone or methylprednisolone), as the three ARTG products show.

A hormonal contribution to migraine (for example, fluctuation in sex steroids) is plausible, but this link is speculative. No mechanistic link has been established between CYP17A1 inhibition and migraine. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only and is not backed by trials or publications.

Profound androgen and cortisol suppression, together with the need for concomitant corticosteroid, makes a migraine use case unfavourable without supporting data.

The other nine predictions (migraine-related phenotypes, leprosy, pulmonary hypertension, nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis, rheumatoid arthritis, kyphoscoliotic heart disease, atrophoderma vermiculata and ulerythema ophryogenesis) also have no trials, and no mechanistic support. They are not evaluated further here.

**Drug Interactions** (100 interactions were identified; examples from the DDInter listing):

- **Moderate:** clarithromycin, levofloxacin, granisetron, metoclopramide, famotidine, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine, eluxadoline, and several laxatives (bisacodyl, castor oil, lactulose, lactitol, glycerin, magnesium citrate, magnesium hydroxide)

Mineralocorticoid excess from CYP17A1 inhibition can cause hypertension, fluid retention and hypokalaemia. This is a safety concern for any new use.
