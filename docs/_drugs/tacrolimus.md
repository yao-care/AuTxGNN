# Tacrolimus: From Organ Transplant Rejection to Seborrheic Dermatitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tacrolimus is a calcineurin inhibitor originally used systemically to prevent organ transplant rejection, with an established topical formulation (Protopic®) for atopic dermatitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Seborrheic Dermatitis**,

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data was not available in this evidence pack (data gap). Based on established clinical knowledge, Tacrolimus is a calcineurin inhibitor: it blocks calcineurin-mediated dephosphorylation of NFAT, suppressing antigen-specific T-cell activation and downstream pro-inflammatory cytokine (e.g. IL-2) release. This mechanism underlies both its systemic use in transplant rejection prophylaxis and its established topical use (Protopic®) in atopic dermatitis.

Seborrheic dermatitis is, like atopic dermatitis, a chronic inflammatory dermatosis with a T-cell-mediated inflammatory component, often with an associated *Malassezia* yeast trigger. The same local anti-inflammatory mechanism that benefits atopic dermatitis is therefore mechanistically plausible for seborrheic dermatitis — this is a modest extension within the same disease class (inflammatory dermatitis), not a cross-system inference, and topical calcineurin inhibitors are already reported as adjuncts to antifungal/anti-inflammatory management of seborrheic dermatitis in clinical practice.

This is further supported by two completed trials specifically in facial seborrheic dermatitis (a Phase 3 maintenance-treatment trial and a Phase 4 proactive-treatment trial), plus a body of literature including randomised controlled comparisons against antifungal cream and against ciclopiroxolamine for maintenance therapy.
