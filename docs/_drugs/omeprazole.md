# Omeprazole: From Gastric Ulcer and Reflux Disease to Duodenogastric Reflux

## One-Sentence Summary

Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor originally used for gastric and duodenal ulcers, reflux disease, oesophagitis and Zollinger-Ellison syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **duodenogastric reflux**, but the supporting evidence is thin: **1 registered trial** (an imaging study that does not test omeprazole) and **15 publications**, mostly small clinical or animal studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor that suppresses gastric acid secretion. Its efficacy in acid-related upper gastrointestinal disease is established, and mechanistically it may reduce mucosal injury from refluxed duodenal contents.

Small clinical studies in Barrett oesophagus (1998, 2000) and a prospective paediatric study of oesophageal bile reflux (2006) report effects on duodenogastric or bile reflux. However, bile reflux is not acid-driven, so any benefit is likely to be symptomatic rather than disease-modifying.

There is also a safety concern. Three animal studies (1996, 1999, 2020) suggest that acid blockade combined with duodenogastric reflux promotes mucosal growth stimulation and gastric carcinogenesis in rats. These findings have not been shown in humans, but they argue for caution.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (these are graph associations, not label text): liver failure, liver disease, gallbladder disease, congestive heart failure, osteoporosis, postmenopausal osteoporosis, bone fracture, chronic atrophic gastritis.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. Major interactions in the data reviewed include **acalabrutinib** and **atazanavir**. Moderate interactions include hydrochlorothiazide, alprazolam, atorvastatin, fenofibrate, apalutamide, armodafinil, amphetamine, anagrelide, and several amphotericin B and aminoglycoside agents (e.g. amikacin). Minor interactions include lidocaine, nifedipine, ambrisentan and aspirin.
- **Animal data**: acid blockade combined with duodenogastric reflux promoted mucosal growth and carcinogenesis in rats (see above).

Please also refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and precautions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only registered trial does not test omeprazole, human evidence is limited to small studies in Barrett oesophagus and paediatric bile reflux, and bile reflux is not acid-driven. Animal data also raise a carcinogenesis concern with acid suppression in the presence of duodenogastric reflux.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Randomised controlled trials of omeprazole in duodenogastric reflux with clear symptomatic or histological endpoints
- Assessment of long-term safety with acid suppression in duodenogastric reflux, given the animal carcinogenesis signals
- Mechanism of action data and TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications, which were not available for this report
- The approved indication text for the Australian licence (ARTG 11677N), which is currently blank
- Review of the major drug interactions (e.g. acalabrutinib, atazanavir) for any proposed use

**Other predicted indications (for context):** Of the other nine predictions, only two are potentially actionable. Gastrojejunal ulcer (marginal ulcer after bariatric surgery) has the strongest support (L2, with a recruiting Phase 4 trial, NCT05799105). Peptic ulcer perforation (L2) supports acid suppression as adjunct care only. Active peptic ulcer disease is already an approved indication and so is not true repurposing. The mastocytosis subtypes and glucagon secretion predictions have no meaningful evidence.
