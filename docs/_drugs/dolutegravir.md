# Dolutegravir: From HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Dolutegravir is an integrase strand transfer inhibitor originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, with **no registered clinical trials** and **14 publications** (almost entirely preclinical macaque and in vitro work) supporting this direction.
This is an animal-model or research use, not a new human indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From general knowledge, dolutegravir is an HIV-1 integrase strand transfer inhibitor that blocks viral DNA integration into the host genome.

SIV is a lentivirus closely related to HIV and encodes a similar integrase. Preclinical studies show that SIVmac239 is susceptible to integrase inhibitors and that integrase resistance mutations behave similarly in both viruses. Dolutegravir monotherapy in SIV-infected macaques selected for resistance mutations with variable virological outcomes. The prediction is therefore mechanistically plausible, but it essentially restates the approved HIV mechanism in an animal model. It does not represent a new human therapeutic indication.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 62 interactions were identified in the data compiled for this report. Most of those listed are Major and involve polyvalent cation products that can reduce dolutegravir absorption: aluminium hydroxide, magaldrate, kaolin, attapulgite, calcium salts (acetate, carbonate, citrate, gluconate, lactate, phosphate, glubionate), magnesium salts (carbonate, chloride, citrate) and iron. Minor interactions include aprepitant, cimetidine, clarithromycin, clotrimazole and dexamethasone.
- **Pregnancy**: The literature compiled for this report describes a neural tube defect signal with dolutegravir exposure around conception, which later surveillance studies have examined further. This is relevant to any use in women of child-bearing potential.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on preclinical macaque and in vitro data only, with no clinical trials. SIV infection is not a human disease, so it is a research-model use of the already approved HIV mechanism rather than a repurposing opportunity for Australian patients.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarification of whether a human indication exists. If not, the finding is better treated as a research tool for non-human primate HIV models.
- Mechanism of action data and the TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications.
- If a nearby human indication is of interest (for example HIV in pregnancy or paediatric HIV), a separate evaluation that includes the neural tube defect safety data.
