# Urea: From Atopic and Other Dermatitis to Dermatitis (Including Chemotherapy- and Radiation-Induced Skin Reactions)

## One-Sentence Summary

Urea is a topical humectant and keratolytic agent, originally used for eczematous and scaling skin conditions such as atopic, contact and seborrheic dermatitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatitis**, with **11 clinical trials** and **19 publications** compiled for this prediction.
Note that this prediction largely overlaps with urea's existing uses. The genuinely new component is its use in chemotherapy- and radiation-induced skin reactions.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on the published literature, urea is a component of the skin's natural moisturising factor. It is a humectant (it draws water into the stratum corneum), a keratolytic, and a skin-barrier-enhancing agent. It may also upregulate barrier proteins and antimicrobial peptides, and it helps regulate keratinocyte proliferation.

Dry-skin and eczematous dermatitis involve impaired barrier function and xerosis, so benefit from urea is mechanistically plausible. Radiation- and capecitabine-induced skin reactions (such as hand-foot skin reaction) also involve barrier damage and dryness, so the same mechanism may apply.

Dermatitis overlaps heavily with urea's established uses (atopic, contact and seborrheic dermatitis), so the prediction is mostly a confirmation of existing use. The true repurposing component is the extension to drug- and radiation-induced dermatitis.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge graph, not label text)**: The TxGNN knowledge graph lists purulent otitis media, ear infection and otitis media as disease contraindication associations. These are not drawn from product label text and should be interpreted cautiously.
- **Drug Interactions**: 54 interactions were identified in the interaction database, all in the portion reviewed being of Moderate level. The main ones involve:
  - Laxatives and bowel preparations (bisacodyl, picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol, lactulose, lactitol, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium citrate, sodium sulfate, castor oil, mineral oil, glycerin, phenolphthalein)
  - SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin)
  - Others (exenatide, sibutramine, iothalamic acid)

  These listed interactions are likely relevant mainly to systemic use, and their significance for topical urea is unclear.
- **Contact dermatitis**: The literature notes that topical products can themselves cause allergic or irritant contact dermatitis.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for warnings and contraindications. The label text was not available in the compiled data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- Urea has a plausible barrier-related mechanism, multiple reviews supporting its use in eczematous dermatitis, and several randomised and completed trials in capecitabine- and radiation-induced dermatitis. Most of that evidence is small, of unknown status, or does not isolate urea's effect.
- The L1 level assigned in the compiled data should be read cautiously. Only one completed Phase 3 trial with a clear urea arm is listed (NCT02247830, n=48), and it is small, so the evidence is weaker than L1 would normally imply.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The ARTG approved-indication text for Urederm, so the existing indication can be separated from the new one
- Label warnings and contraindications from the TGA Product Information, since these were missing from the compiled data
- Mechanism of action data confirmed from DrugBank
- Published results from the unknown-status trials (NCT05348278, NCT02251392) and the completed capecitabine hand-foot trials, to clarify whether urea prevents chemotherapy-related skin reactions (an earlier study was negative)
- Clarification of the knowledge-graph otitis-related contraindication signal, with advice against application in the ear canal until clarified

**Other predictions:** The remaining predicted indications (acne keloid, neonatal dermatomyositis, acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans, childhood interstitial lung disease, amyopathic dermatomyositis, familial hydroa vacciniforme, vulvar inverted follicular keratosis and familial pityriasis rubra pilaris) have no supporting trials or no mechanistic support, and are rated **Hold**. Acne (disease) has limited evidence at L3, a single multicentre study of benzoyl peroxide in a 10% urea vehicle that does not isolate urea's own effect, and is classed as a research question.
