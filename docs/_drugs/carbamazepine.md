## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Carbamazepine is generally understood to block voltage-gated sodium channels, which reduces abnormal, repetitive firing in nerve cells. This is the basis for its use in nerve pain conditions such as trigeminal neuralgia.

Tumours of the trigeminal nerve or nearby structures (for example schwannoma, meningioma, lymphoma or dermoid cyst) often present with trigeminal neuralgia-type facial pain. In several of the case reports retrieved, patients were first given carbamazepine for that pain, with variable results, and the tumour was found later.

There is no evidence that carbamazepine has an antitumour effect. The high score most likely reflects how closely related trigeminal neuralgia and trigeminal nerve disease are in the model's network, not a disease-modifying action. Any potential benefit would be limited to symptom control, so this is not true repurposing for the neoplasm.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions** (100 interactions were identified; examples of the major ones are listed):

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No clinical trial or publication tests carbamazepine as a treatment for trigeminal nerve neoplasm, so the evidence level is L5. The plausible benefit is symptomatic relief of tumour-related nerve pain, which is not a disease-modifying effect, and the case reports show carbamazepine often failed to control pain in these patients.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Product Information warnings and contraindications from the TGA-approved PI
- Mechanism of action data
- Controlled or cohort data on carbamazepine for pain caused by trigeminal nerve tumours
- A drug-interaction review for the patient population concerned
