# Rifampicin: From Tuberculosis to Conjunctivitis

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed structured mechanism of action data is not available for rifampicin in this evidence pack (flagged as a data gap, DG002). Based on the supporting literature contained in the pack itself, rifampicin is a rifamycin antibiotic that acts by inhibiting bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, and its efficacy against *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* and related organisms (including *Mycobacterium leprae*) is well established through decades of anti-tuberculosis and anti-leprosy treatment regimens repeatedly referenced across the trial and publication evidence provided.

Conjunctivitis is frequently caused by bacterial pathogens, including *Chlamydia trachomatis* (the cause of trachoma, a chronic form of conjunctivitis) and various Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms. The literature evidence in this pack specifically notes that rifampicin is "the antibiotic most active against *Chlamydia trachomatis*" on a weight basis (PMID 6635446), and documents historical topical use of rifampicin ointment in controlled trachoma trials (PMID 1096630) and preclinical anti-trachoma activity studies (PMID 5411121, *Nature*, 1970). This gives a plausible, though largely historical, mechanistic bridge between rifampicin's established antibacterial spectrum and bacterial/chlamydial conjunctivitis.

That said, the supporting evidence is predominantly decades-old (1970s–1990s), consists mainly of case reports, microbiological susceptibility surveys and narrative reviews rather than contemporary controlled trials, and no clinical trials specifically evaluating rifampicin for conjunctivitis are currently registered. This tempers the strength of the mechanistic rationale despite the very high TxGNN prediction score.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Rifampicin is not currently registered on the Australian ARTG (0 entries), a blocking safety data gap (TFDA/TGA label warnings and contraindications, DG001) prevents entry into an S1 safety review, and the conjunctivitis prediction — while mechanistically plausible via rifampicin's known anti-chlamydial activity — is supported only by older observational studies, case reports and one historical controlled trachoma trial (L3), with zero registered clinical trials specifically targeting this indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TGA-approved Product Information / label warnings and contraindications (resolves DG001)
- Structured mechanism of action data from DrugBank or equivalent source (resolves DG002)
- Confirmation of current or planned ARTG registration status for rifampicin in Australia
- Contemporary clinical trial evidence (ideally RCT) evaluating rifampicin — topical or systemic — specifically for bacterial or chlamydial conjunctivitis
- Route/formulation compatibility assessment, since no topical ophthalmic formulation data is documented in this evidence pack
