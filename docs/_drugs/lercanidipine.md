## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, lercanidipine is an L-type calcium channel blocker of the dihydropyridine class. It causes vasodilation and lowers blood pressure, and in principle could reduce pulmonary vascular resistance.

The link between hypertension and pulmonary hypertension is largely one of shared terminology and vascular biology. The two retrieved papers concern hypoxia signalling in glioma stem cells and in cardiomyocytes. Neither studies lercanidipine or pulmonary hypertension. Calcium channel blockers are useful in only a small subset of vasoreactive pulmonary arterial hypertension patients. In hypoxic lung disease they may worsen ventilation-perfusion matching and gas exchange. The high score should therefore be read as a prediction, not evidence of benefit.

Other predictions (malignant hypertensive renal disease, malignant renovascular hypertension, chronic pulmonary heart disease, migraine, Braddock syndrome) are also supported by no trials or literature. Most of them largely extend the original hypertension indication or rely on class-level rationale. The one prediction with some supporting material is cerebrovascular disorder (score 69.64%), covered below.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for safety information. No drug interaction records were found in the data compiled for this report. Calcium channel blockers carry a theoretical risk of worsening gas exchange and right heart function in hypoxic lung disease, which is relevant to the top predicted indication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top predictions (pulmonary hypertension and related terms) rest on model scores alone (L5), with no trials, no relevant literature, and a plausible safety concern in hypoxic lung disease. The cerebrovascular disorder direction (L4) is more promising as a research question, but the human evidence is indirect and largely reflects blood pressure control.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full-text review of the stroke-specific clinical studies (PMID 25425058 and 28300435) to determine whether any effect is independent of blood pressure lowering
- Mechanism of action data (e.g. from DrugBank)
- TGA Product Information, including the approved indication, warnings and contraindications
- Mapping or removal of the obsolete disease term "susceptibility to ischemic stroke"
- For any pulmonary hypertension work, controlled evidence in the relevant patient group and an assessment of gas exchange and right ventricular safety
