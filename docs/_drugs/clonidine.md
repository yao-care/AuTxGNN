## Clonidine: From Hypertension to Tourette Syndrome
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Clonidine is a centrally acting alpha-2 adrenergic agonist, originally used to treat hypertension, open-angle glaucoma and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD).
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is faciodigitogenital syndrome, but that prediction has no supporting evidence. The best-supported prediction in the data compiled for this report is **Tourette syndrome**, with **2 clinical trials** and **18 publications**, including European guidelines and a network meta-analysis.
This report therefore focuses on Tourette syndrome, and the other predicted indications are summarised briefly below.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Clonidine is a well-known alpha-2 adrenergic agonist. By stimulating alpha-2 receptors in the brain, it reduces noradrenergic activity, and this is thought to dampen tics, impulsivity and hyperactivity.

Tourette syndrome is a neurodevelopmental disorder that commonly occurs together with ADHD, one of clonidine's original indications. This overlap is the main reason the prediction is plausible. Clonidine has been used for tics since the late 1970s, and European clinical guidelines and a recent network meta-analysis include it as a treatment option, particularly when tics occur with ADHD.

The evidence level is capped at L3 because the supplied trials are small Phase 4 studies, not confirmatory Phase 3 RCTs. Guideline support is strong, and the level could be upgraded after review of the primary RCT literature.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindication signals from the knowledge graph** (these come from drug-disease contraindication links in the TxGNN knowledge graph, not from approved label text). They include:
- Cardiovascular: hypotensive disorder, myocardial infarction, myocardial ischaemia, coronary thrombosis, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease
- Metabolic: type 1 and type 2 diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, gout, potassium deficiency, hypercalcaemia
- Other: liver failure, anuria, pancreatitis, systemic lupus erythematosus, respiratory failure, anxiety and neurotic disorders

**Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded, and the 20 reviewed here were all rated Moderate. They include:
- Corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone)
- Antidiabetic agents (insulins, sulfonylureas such as glimepiride, glipizide and glyburide, and SGLT2 inhibitors such as canagliflozin, dapagliflozin and empagliflozin)
- Bupropion, morphine and dronabinol

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails** (Tourette syndrome only)

**Rationale:**
- Clonidine has a plausible mechanism, two small Phase 4 trials, and guideline and network meta-analysis support for Tourette syndrome, especially with comorbid ADHD. The trials are small and not confirmatory.
- The other predictions are not ready to progress. Faciodigitogenital syndrome, chondromyxoid fibroma, hypervitaminosis and 16p11.2 microdeletion syndrome should be held. Obesity, migraine, mania and specific developmental disorder remain research questions.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the primary RCT literature to confirm whether the evidence level can be upgraded
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Warnings and contraindications from the TGA Product Information, since this safety data was missing from the data compiled for this report
- The approved indication text for the Catapres ARTG entry
- A safety monitoring plan for paediatric use, covering low blood pressure, sedation and cardiovascular risk
