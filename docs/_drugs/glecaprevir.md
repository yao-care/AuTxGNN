## Glecaprevir: HIV infectious disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Glecaprevir is an HCV NS3/4A protease inhibitor, marketed in Australia as Maviret (co-formulated with pibrentasvir) for chronic hepatitis C virus infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **HIV infectious disease**, but the supporting evidence is almost entirely about treating HCV in people who also have HIV, not about treating HIV itself.
The data compiled for this report include **7 clinical trials** and **20 publications** linked to this prediction. They support HCV cure in co-infected patients rather than an anti-HIV effect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Glecaprevir is known to be an HCV NS3/4A protease inhibitor. It is used with pibrentasvir (an NS5A inhibitor) in a fixed-dose combination whose efficacy in chronic HCV is well established.

There is no established direct anti-HIV activity for glecaprevir. The high TxGNN score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases such as HIV and HCV, not biological evidence. The trials and publications retrieved concern HCV treatment in people with HIV co-infection. This is a co-infection use of the original indication, not true repurposing against HIV. The L3 label reflects mainly observational and review-level evidence; the Phase 3 trials below do not test an HIV endpoint.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 57 interactions were recorded. Those rated **Major** are rosuvastatin, simvastatin and edoxaban. Those rated **Moderate** include dexamethasone, esomeprazole, omeprazole, apixaban, betrixaban, cobicistat, deferasirox, metreleptin, naldemedine, lusutrombopag, revefenacin, griseofulvin and troglitazone. Those rated **Minor** include fidaxomicin, naloxegol, prucalopride and indacaterol. Interactions with antiretrovirals are particularly relevant in HIV co-infection and are covered by the pharmacokinetic study above.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The supporting trials and publications show that glecaprevir/pibrentasvir cures HCV in people with HIV co-infection. They do not show any effect on HIV itself, and there is no plausible anti-HIV mechanism. The high prediction score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- In vitro evidence of any anti-HIV activity, which would be needed before any HIV-directed development
- TGA Product Information for warnings, contraindications and approved indications
- Clarification of whether the intended question is HIV treatment or HCV treatment in HIV co-infection; the latter is already within the approved HCV use
