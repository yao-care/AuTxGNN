# Nivolumab: From Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer and Metastatic Melanoma to Non-Cutaneous Melanoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Nivolumab is a PD-1 immune checkpoint inhibitor, originally used for non-small cell lung cancer (including squamous cell lung carcinoma) and metastatic melanoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **non-cutaneous melanoma**, with **43 registered clinical trials** and **9 publications** linked to this prediction, although most trials are early-phase or combination studies and few are specific to this population.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Nivolumab blocks the PD-1 receptor (encoded by *PDCD1*) on T cells. This restores T-cell antitumour activity that tumours suppress through the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway. The mechanism is already validated in cutaneous melanoma, and nivolumab is established in advanced melanoma and several other cancers.

Non-cutaneous melanomas (mucosal, uveal, acral) share melanocytic origin and PD-1/PD-L1 pathway biology with cutaneous melanoma. Their lower mutational burden may reduce response rates. Even so, retrospective and pooled data show activity in non-cutaneous melanoma. Combination strategies are being tested to improve response.

Detailed mechanism-of-action text was not available in the data compiled for this report. The target information above comes from pharmacology data.

Several other melanoma subtypes were also predicted, with the same TxGNN score of about 98.3%. They are mucosal, acral lentiginous, nodular, superficial spreading, lentigo maligna, amelanotic, epithelioid cell, balloon cell and CDK4-linked melanoma. Of these, mucosal and acral lentiginous melanoma have the strongest supporting evidence (both L2). The remaining subtypes have limited evidence, mostly case reports, and CDK4-linked melanoma has none.

## Safety Considerations

Warnings and contraindications from the TGA-approved Product Information were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full safety information.

Literature in the data compiled for this report describes immune-related adverse events with nivolumab, including fulminant myocarditis with rhabdomyolysis, capillary leak syndrome, bullous pemphigoid, colitis and pulmonary sarcoid-like granulomatosis.

**Drug interactions** (14 recorded):
- **Major:** lenalidomide, pomalidomide, thalidomide
- **Moderate:** systemic corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, prednisolone, prednisone, methylprednisolone, deflazacort) and idelalisib

Corticosteroids are commonly needed to manage immune-related adverse events, so the interaction with nivolumab should be considered in clinical context.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Nivolumab is already marketed in Australia and is established in melanoma. For non-cutaneous melanoma, particularly mucosal and acral subtypes, a prospective Phase 2 trial, a pooled analysis and several cohort studies show activity. There is no completed Phase 3 evidence, response rates appear lower than in cutaneous melanoma, and adjuvant benefit in acral disease is uncertain.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The TGA-approved Product Information (warnings, contraindications and approved indications), which was not available for this report
- Confirmation of the current approved indications for Opdivo in Australia and whether non-cutaneous melanoma subtypes are covered
- Subtype-specific efficacy data (mucosal, acral, uveal) from prospective or randomised studies, including results from the recruiting mucosal melanoma trials
- A review of combination regimens (for example with ipilimumab) against nivolumab monotherapy for these subtypes
- An immune-related adverse event monitoring plan, including management of corticosteroid interactions
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data for formal documentation
