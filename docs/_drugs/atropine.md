## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Atropine is generally known as a non-selective muscarinic antagonist, so its effects would be expected to come from blocking cholinergic signalling.

Several preclinical studies link cholinergic and parasympathetic signalling to migraine biology. These include meningeal mast cell cholinergic modulation in a rat migraine model (PMID 36485173), plasma protein extravasation in rat dura mater after parasympathetic ganglion stimulation (PMID 9344563), and central cholinergic involvement in antinociception in rodents (PMID 8930196). A muscarinic antagonist could plausibly modulate these pathways.

However, none of these studies shows that atropine itself treats or prevents migraine. The link is hypothetical and should be treated as a hypothesis for further research.
