# Betamethasone: From Autoimmune and Inflammatory Conditions to Alopecia Areata

## One-Sentence Summary

Betamethasone is a potent glucocorticoid, originally used for inflammatory and autoimmune conditions such as Crohn disease, adrenocortical insufficiency and acute lymphoblastic leukaemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**, with **6 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Betamethasone is already used in this setting, so this is closer to confirming existing off-label use than to true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, betamethasone is a potent glucocorticoid with broad immunosuppressive and anti-inflammatory activity. Its efficacy in autoimmune and inflammatory conditions is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to alopecia areata.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune disease in which the immune privilege of the hair follicle collapses. Corticosteroids suppress the perifollicular lymphocytic infiltrate, and they are an established treatment class for this condition (topical, intralesional and oral pulse).

The link is therefore direct and biologically plausible. Betamethasone is already used in this setting, including as a comparator or treatment arm in multiple trials.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- A completed Phase 2 trial and several randomised studies support corticosteroid use, including betamethasone, in alopecia areata. Most trials use betamethasone as a comparator and are small, so the evidence is moderate (L2).
- The other nine predicted indications have weak or no supporting evidence (Hold, or a research question for steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome), and the steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome prediction is mechanistically contradictory.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications (not available in the compiled data) and the approved indication text for both ARTG entries
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Clarification of the formulation and route for each use (topical, intralesional or oral mini-pulse), as only topical products are registered in Australia
- Higher-quality head-to-head or placebo-controlled trials with betamethasone as the primary test arm
- A review of the major interaction (adalimumab) and the safety plan for patients with hypertension, diabetes or heart failure
