# Clotrimazole: From Superficial Fungal Infections to Acne (Top TxGNN Prediction)

## One-Sentence Summary

Clotrimazole is a topical azole antifungal, originally used for tinea, cutaneous and oral candidiasis, pityriasis versicolor and vulvovaginal candidiasis.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **acne**, supported by only **1 suspended combination-product trial** and **no publications**.
Its more credible predictions, such as **vulvovaginitis** (12 trials, 20 publications) and **superficial mycosis** (20 publications), largely restate on-label uses rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Clotrimazole is an azole antifungal. Its effect is generally attributed to inhibition of fungal CYP51 (lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase), which blocks ergosterol synthesis and disrupts fungal membrane integrity. It also has some reported antibacterial and anti-inflammatory activity.

**Acne (rank 1):** *Malassezia* and *Cutibacterium acnes* may contribute to acne-like lesions, so a topical antimicrobial effect is plausible. However, the only trial is a fixed-dose combination of beclomethasone, gentamicin and clotrimazole. The effect of clotrimazole cannot be separated from the corticosteroid and antibiotic. The trial is suspended and has no results. This prediction is hypothesis-level only.

**Vulvovaginitis (rank 2):** Vulvovaginal candidiasis is a core approved use of clotrimazole. The prediction matches existing practice, so it is not a genuine repurposing candidate.

**Other predictions:**
- Superficial mycosis overlaps closely with the approved indications.
- Trichomonal vulvovaginitis has weak support. Nitroimidazoles are the standard, and clotrimazole is at most an adjunct in mixed infections.
- Deep or hair-shaft dermatophyte infections (tinea profunda, Majocchi granuloma, ectothrix, endothrix, scalp or beard dermatophytosis) are mechanistically plausible. Topical penetration is poor, however, and systemic therapy is normally required.
- Postmenopausal atrophic vaginitis is caused by oestrogen deficiency, so there is no clear antifungal link.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications recorded in the knowledge graph (not label text):** adrenocortical insufficiency, Addison disease, skin atrophy, telangiectasis, capillary disease, hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia, peripheral vascular disease, intermittent claudication and peripheral arterial disease. These come from a drug-disease network and should be checked against the Product Information.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. The main ones flagged as **Major** are fentanyl, alfentanil, hydrocodone and amiodarone. Moderate interactions include abemaciclib, paclitaxel, acalabrutinib, nifedipine, amlodipine, apixaban, aprepitant, alprazolam, alfuzosin, aliskiren, estradiol, levonorgestrel and norethisterone. Dolutegravir and amphotericin B are Minor. Systemic exposure from topical use is generally low, but this applies particularly to vaginal or extensive use.
- **Pregnancy:** A first-trimester topical antifungal cohort study (PMID 41350226) and a placental steroidogenesis study (PMID 40886781) should be considered when using clotrimazole in pregnancy.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (acne, the top prediction); Proceed with Guardrails (vulvovaginitis and superficial mycosis)**

**Rationale:**
- The acne prediction rests on a single suspended combination trial with no results, so it cannot support a repurposing decision.
- Vulvovaginitis (L1) and superficial mycosis (L3) are well supported, but both largely match clotrimazole's existing approved uses.
- Deep and hair-follicle dermatophyte infections should stay on Hold because systemic therapy is the standard.
- Atrophic vaginitis has no evidence and no clear mechanistic link.
- Trichomonal vulvovaginitis is a research question only. Clotrimazole is at most an adjunct for mixed infections.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A TGA Product Information review for warnings and contraindications. This data was missing and blocks safety screening.
- Mechanism of action data.
- For acne, a controlled trial of clotrimazole alone, separate from corticosteroid and antibiotic components.
- For vulvovaginitis, confirmation of whether a vaginal clotrimazole formulation is registered in Australia, since only a topical cream is listed.
- Confirmation of the approved indication text for the ARTG entry.
- Diagnostic confirmation (KOH or culture) and limiting use to topical-responsive, non-deep infections.
- A pregnancy safety assessment.
