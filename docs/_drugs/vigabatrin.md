# Vigabatrin: From West Syndrome to ARX-Related X-Linked Intellectual Disability (with or without Seizures)

## One-Sentence Summary

Vigabatrin is an anti-epileptic drug originally used for West syndrome (infantile spasms) and developmental and epileptic encephalopathy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **intellectual disability, X-linked, with or without seizures, ARX-related**,

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, vigabatrin is an irreversible inhibitor of GABA-transaminase, which raises brain GABA levels. It is a first-line therapy for infantile spasms, and its efficacy in West syndrome is established.

ARX-related disorders involve impaired development of GABAergic interneurons and frequently present with infantile spasms and early-onset epilepsy. This gives a plausible **symptomatic** link: vigabatrin may help control seizures and spasms in these patients. It is not expected to modify the underlying disease or the intellectual disability itself.

The supplied literature consists of broad reviews and guidelines on neurogenetic or early-onset seizure conditions, plus case reports in other genetic disorders. None is specific to ARX with vigabatrin, so the link remains hypothesis-level.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (not label text): kidney disease, pyoureter, congestive heart failure, peripheral nervous system disease, peripheral neuropathy, anaemia, macrocytic anaemia and scotoma.
- **Drug Interactions** (65 recorded in total; examples):
  - *Major*: systemic and topical corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisone, prednisolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone), deferoxamine, and topical oxyquinoline.
  - *Moderate*: opioids (morphine, opium, diphenoxylate, difenoxin), cannabinoids (dronabinol, nabilone), metoclopramide, sibutramine and ethanol.
- **Other considerations from the prediction data:** vigabatrin is known for retinal toxicity (visual field defects) and reversible MRI changes (VABAM), which are particularly relevant in infants (see PMID 41004695).

Please also refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but the evidence is model-based with a plausible symptomatic mechanism only. There are no clinical trials and no publications specific to vigabatrin in ARX-related disorders. The regulatory data are also incomplete, so safety screening cannot yet proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and the TGA Product Information (warnings, contraindications and approved indication text) for Sabril
- Targeted literature search for vigabatrin in ARX-related epilepsy and infantile spasms (case series, registries)
- Assessment of retinal and MRI safety monitoring in infants and children with genetic encephalopathies
- Review of the relevant interacting drugs, especially corticosteroids, which are commonly used in infantile spasms
