## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for Vigabatrin is not available in this evidence pack (data gap DG002). Based on the supporting literature itself, Vigabatrin is used clinically as an antiepileptic in genetic seizure syndromes — two case reports describe it being used for hypsarrhythmia and West syndrome in infants with lissencephaly-type genetic disorders (PMID 41004695, 36436329).

ARX-related X-linked intellectual disability frequently presents with epilepsy as a core clinical feature, including infantile spasms. The literature set includes several ARX-specific papers describing this seizure phenotype directly: a mouse model showing ARX mutations cause epilepsy-like phenotypes (PMID 19605412), case reports of infantile spasms in ARX-related epilepsy (PMID 18468866, 18462864), and a broader review of antiepileptic therapy in neurogenetic disorders (PMID 23400943).

The mechanistic link is therefore indirect: the TxGNN prediction most plausibly reflects the fact that seizures are a shared symptomatic target between Vigabatrin's established use and the ARX-related disease phenotype, rather than evidence of a disease-modifying effect on the underlying genetic condition.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Safety data are missing (a blocking data gap), Vigabatrin has no ARTG registration in Australia, and there is no direct clinical trial or case evidence evaluating it specifically in ARX-related X-linked intellectual disability — only literature on shared seizure symptomatology in related genetic epilepsy syndromes.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TGA/overseas Product Information covering warnings, contraindications and drug interactions (resolves DG001)
- Confirmed original indication and mechanism of action documentation (resolves DG002)
- Clarification of the regulatory pathway if clinical use is being considered while the drug remains unregistered in Australia
- Clinical evidence specifically evaluating Vigabatrin in ARX-related XLID, rather than inferred from broader genetic epilepsy populations
- Genetic/phenotypic criteria for identifying eligible patients, given the rarity of this indication
