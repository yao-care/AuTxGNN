# Zinc Oxide: From Topical Skin Conditions to Acne

## One-Sentence Summary

Zinc oxide is a topical skin protectant, originally used for conditions such as dermatitis, fungal skin infections (tinea), haemorrhoids and burns.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acne (disease)**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **3 indirect publications** supporting this direction.
This is a computational prediction at an early research stage.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on known information, zinc oxide is a topical agent with established use as a skin protectant and soothing agent. It also has plausible anti-inflammatory, sebum-modulating and antibacterial activity, including against *Cutibacterium acnes*.

Acne is an inflammatory skin condition driven partly by bacterial colonisation. Zinc oxide's existing uses in inflammatory and infective skin conditions (dermatitis, tinea, candidiasis) make a link to acne mechanistically conceivable.

The supporting literature is indirect, though. It consists of a general nanoparticle review, a preclinical microneedle study and a small split-face assessment whose relevance to zinc oxide cannot be confirmed. The very high TxGNN score is a computational output and does not replace clinical evidence.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for safety information.

The knowledge graph also lists associations with several conditions, including hypertension, benign prostatic hyperplasia, sickle cell anaemia, primary hyperoxaluria, iron metabolism disorders (haemochromatosis, haemosiderosis), Wilson disease, coronary artery disease, heart conduction disease, hyperthyroidism and anxiety. These come from graph relationships, not label text, and should be verified against the PI before any interpretation. No drug interaction records were found.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is limited to indirect and preclinical sources, with no registered clinical trials. The high TxGNN score alone does not justify progression. The Australian listing also lacks the indication and product details needed for assessment.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- The TGA-approved PI for warnings, contraindications and approved indications
- Product details for the Australian listing (name, dosage form, indication)
- Human clinical data on topical zinc oxide in acne, such as a controlled trial or systematic review
- Confirmation of the relevance of the split-face study (PMID 15536660) to zinc oxide
