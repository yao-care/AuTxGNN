# Velpatasvir: From Chronic Hepatitis C to Hepatitis B Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Velpatasvir is an HCV NS5A inhibitor, used in fixed-dose combinations with sofosbuvir (and with voxilaprevir) to treat chronic hepatitis C virus infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatitis B virus infection**, but the supporting evidence is weak. The **5 linked clinical trials** and **literature** concern HCV treatment or HBV reactivation safety, not HBV efficacy of velpatasvir.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, velpatasvir is part of the sofosbuvir/velpatasvir combination (and the sofosbuvir/velpatasvir/voxilaprevir combination). It acts as an HCV NS5A inhibitor, and its efficacy in chronic hepatitis C is well established.

The link to HBV is weak. HBV is a DNA virus that replicates via reverse transcription, and velpatasvir has no established activity against it. The very high TxGNN score most likely reflects network proximity among viral hepatitis nodes in the knowledge graph rather than a shared drug target.

The related trials do not support an anti-HBV effect. In the trial of SOF/VEL with prophylactic tenofovir alafenamide (TAF), the HBV protection comes from TAF. The literature on HBV in this setting concerns HBV reactivation during HCV direct-acting antiviral treatment, which is a safety concern rather than a treatment indication.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 57 interactions are recorded. Examples include:
  - **Major**: dexamethasone and the proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole, pantoprazole, rabeprazole)
  - **Moderate**: antacids (aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium oxide, magnesium carbonate, magaldrate), H2 blockers (cimetidine, famotidine, nizatidine) and naldemedine
  - **Minor**: fidaxomicin, naloxegol and prucalopride
- **HBV reactivation**: The literature describes HBV reactivation during HCV direct-acting antiviral therapy in patients with past or current HBV infection. This is directly relevant to any HBV-related use.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Velpatasvir has no established anti-HBV activity. The high TxGNN score appears to be a graph-proximity artefact, and every linked trial and paper concerns HCV treatment or HBV reactivation safety. No study supports velpatasvir for HBV, so the evidence level is L4 at best.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, and in vitro evidence of anti-HBV activity
- Preclinical or early-phase studies of velpatasvir against HBV
- Approved indication text and PI safety information (warnings and contraindications) for the Australian products
- For HCV/HBV co-infection, guidance on HBV screening and monitoring (and antiviral prophylaxis) during SOF/VEL therapy, which is a safety management question rather than a new indication
