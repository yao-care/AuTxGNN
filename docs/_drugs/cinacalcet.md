# Cinacalcet: From Hyperparathyroidism to Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia

## One-Sentence Summary

Cinacalcet is a calcimimetic that activates the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) on parathyroid cells. It is used to lower parathyroid hormone (PTH) and serum calcium.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis**, but it has no supporting evidence. The best-supported prediction is **Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia (MEN)**, with **1 clinical trial** (indirect) and **19 publications**. The evidence supports treating the hyperparathyroidism manifestation of MEN1, not the neoplastic syndrome itself.

> Note: The first-ranked prediction (nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis, score 98.5%) has no clinical trials or literature (L5, Hold). This report focuses on rank 4, multiple endocrine neoplasia, because it has the strongest evidence. Other predictions are summarised briefly at the end.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, cinacalcet is an allosteric modulator of the CaSR. It sensitises the receptor to extracellular calcium, which reduces PTH secretion and serum calcium.

Primary hyperparathyroidism is the most common and earliest manifestation of MEN1 (and also occurs in MEN2A and MEN4). Cinacalcet's efficacy in lowering PTH and calcium in other forms of hyperparathyroidism makes it mechanistically applicable here. Several small cohort studies and clinical series report calcium reduction in MEN1-related primary hyperparathyroidism.

The evidence supports managing the hyperparathyroidism, not treating the underlying neoplastic syndrome. Use is best reserved for patients who are not surgical candidates or who have persistent or recurrent disease after surgery.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for warnings and contraindications.

- **Disease associations from the knowledge graph (not label text):** epilepsy, liver disease, gallbladder disease, hypotensive disorder, hypercalcaemia, nephrocalcinosis, phosphorus metabolism disease and hypophosphataemia. These should be verified against the PI.
- **Risks noted in the literature:** hypocalcaemia and QT prolongation (PMID 36090548).
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded. The main ones listed:
  - **Major:** eliglustat.
  - **Moderate:** clarithromycin, dexfenfluramine, eluxadoline, glycerol phenylbutyrate, metoclopramide, miconazole.
  - **Unclassified:** calcitriol, pantoprazole, omeprazole, lansoprazole, morphine, simvastatin, prednisone and others.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails** (multiple endocrine neoplasia, focused on MEN1-related hyperparathyroidism). All other predictions: **Hold**.

**Rationale:**
- Several small cohort studies and clinical series show calcium reduction with cinacalcet in MEN1-related primary hyperparathyroidism, and the mechanism is plausible. However, there are no randomised trials in MEN. The only registered trial is indirect, and the evidence applies to the hyperparathyroidism rather than the neoplastic syndrome.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Product Information (PI) warnings and contraindications for the Australian product, which are currently missing and block formal safety screening.
- The approved indication text for the ARTG entry, and confirmation of the original indications.
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank).
- Prospective or controlled data in MEN1 hyperparathyroidism.
- A patient selection plan (non-surgical candidates, or persistent or recurrent disease) with calcium and QT monitoring, and a review of interactions, particularly with eliglustat and CYP-interacting drugs.
