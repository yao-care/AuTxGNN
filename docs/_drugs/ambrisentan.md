# Ambrisentan: From Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension to PAH Associated with Connective Tissue Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Ambrisentan is an endothelin receptor antagonist, originally used to treat pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **PAH associated with connective tissue disease (CTD-PAH)**, with **2 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.
CTD-PAH sits within the broader PAH population, so this is better understood as subgroup confirmation than as a truly new indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on known information, ambrisentan is a selective endothelin type A receptor antagonist that reduces pulmonary vascular resistance. Its efficacy in PAH is established, and mechanistically it is applicable to CTD-PAH.

Endothelin-1 drives vasoconstriction, fibrosis and vascular remodelling. These processes are central to PAH in systemic sclerosis and other connective tissue diseases. Blocking the endothelin A receptor therefore targets the same pathway as in the approved PAH indication.

CTD-PAH is a recognised Group 1 PAH subtype. Patients with systemic sclerosis tend to respond less well than those with idiopathic PAH, which is why subgroup data matter. The available data (AMBITION and ARIES-E subgroup analyses, plus small trials) are consistent and relevant, but no Phase 3 RCT specific to CTD-PAH was identified in the data compiled for this report.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (these come from network relationships, not from product label text, so they should be verified against the PI):
- Congestive heart failure
- Anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia)
- Pulmonary veno-occlusive disease

**Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Examples include:
- **Moderate:** canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, eliglustat, naltrexone
- **Minor:** clarithromycin, miconazole, omeprazole

Many other entries have an unknown severity level (for example simvastatin, metformin, prednisone, morphine and pantoprazole). Interactions with antiretrovirals and background PAH or connective tissue disease therapies should be reviewed before use in these populations.

**Class considerations:** Endothelin receptor antagonists are teratogenic, and fluid retention and anaemia are relevant in some populations. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed trials (Phase 2 and Phase 4), a systematic review, a meta-analysis and consistent subgroup analyses support the use of ambrisentan in CTD-PAH. Trials are small and no CTD-PAH-specific Phase 3 RCT is evident, so the evidence level is L2. Because CTD-PAH already falls within the approved PAH population, the practical question is subgroup confirmation rather than a new indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Indication text and the full TGA-approved PI for the Australian product, including warnings and contraindications, which were not available in the data compiled for this report
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation that CTD-PAH falls within the ARTG-approved indication
- A review of the significant drug interactions and a monitoring plan covering embryo-fetal toxicity, fluid retention and anaemia
- Verification of the knowledge-graph contraindications (heart failure, anaemia, pulmonary veno-occlusive disease) against the PI
