## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, azacitidine is a hypomethylating nucleoside analogue. Its efficacy in myeloid malignancies is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to refractory anaemia in the myelodysplastic setting.

The retrieved trials are almost entirely in myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) and AML, so "aregenerative anaemia" most likely maps to MDS-associated refractory anaemia. Refractory anaemia is a classical MDS subtype, and azacitidine is an established therapy for MDS. A meta-analysis in lower-risk MDS (PMID 29118268) supports its use in transfusion-dependent anaemia.

Caveats: the mapping from "aregenerative anaemia" to MDS is indirect, most trials test azacitidine in combination with other agents, and several were terminated with minimal enrolment.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails** (for aregenerative anaemia, interpreted as MDS-associated refractory anaemia)

**Rationale:**
- Azacitidine is already established in MDS, and multiple Phase 1/2 trials, one Phase 2/3 programme and a lower-risk MDS meta-analysis support the direction. However, most trials test combinations, several were terminated, and no completed Phase 3 RCT specific to this indication was provided, so the evidence level is L2.
- All other predictions should remain on Hold or be treated as research questions.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm the underlying diagnosis (MDS versus other aplastic or aregenerative anaemia)
- Mechanism of action data and the approved indication text from the TGA Product Information
- Label-based warnings and contraindications (the PBS/PI safety data are currently missing, a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Haematological monitoring plan and dosing aligned with established MDS regimens
- Review of the major drug interactions against the patient's concomitant medicines
