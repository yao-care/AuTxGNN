# Temazepam: From Insomnia to Sleep Disorder (Initiating and Maintaining Sleep)

## One-Sentence Summary

Temazepam is a benzodiazepine hypnotic, originally used to treat insomnia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **sleep disorder, initiating and maintaining sleep**, but this is essentially the same condition as the approved use under a different ontology term, so it is not a true repurposing opportunity.
There are **no registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** (mainly guidelines and reviews) in the data compiled for this report.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, temazepam is a benzodiazepine receptor agonist that enhances GABA-A receptor activity, producing sedative-hypnotic effects that shorten sleep latency and support sleep maintenance.

The predicted indication is effectively the approved indication (insomnia) restated under a different ontology term. The high TxGNN score therefore reflects well-known pharmacology rather than a new therapeutic opportunity.

Other predicted conditions are weaker. Examples are agoraphobia and anxiety (class-level anxiolytic rationale, but indirect evidence), restless legs syndrome (a 1986 study of temazepam in nocturnal myoclonus, while current guidelines favour other agents) and alcohol withdrawal (strong class rationale, but longer-acting benzodiazepines are generally preferred and temazepam has abuse liability). Several others (benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy, Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome, childhood absence epilepsy) have no plausible mechanistic basis or clinical evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (drug-disease edges, not label text): anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, pyoureter, obstructive sleep apnoea syndrome, sleep apnoea syndrome, congenital central hypoventilation syndrome, Haddad syndrome, amnestic disorder, retrograde amnesia, substance-related disorder, and substance abuse/dependence.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 recorded; main ones shown):
  - **Major**: morphine, liposomal morphine (additive central nervous system and respiratory depression).
  - **Moderate**: bupropion, dronabinol, nabilone, metoclopramide, opium, sibutramine, teduglutide.
  - **Minor**: antacids and magnesium or calcium products (e.g. aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magnesium oxide, magnesium hydroxide).
- **Literature signals**: rebound insomnia on discontinuation, dependence risk after short prescriptions, abuse and injecting misuse (including gel capsule formulations in Australia), and safety concerns in older adults.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction restates the approved insomnia indication, so it offers no new repurposing value. Other predicted conditions have either indirect class-level evidence (anxiety, agoraphobia, alcohol withdrawal, restless legs syndrome) or no supporting evidence, and better-established treatments exist for each.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and the PI warnings and contraindications for the Australian product (currently missing)
- If a genuinely new indication is pursued (for example restless legs syndrome or alcohol withdrawal), temazepam-specific controlled trial evidence compared with current standard therapy
- A dependence, misuse and older-adult safety plan
