# Prednisolone Acetate: From Uveitis and Conjunctivitis to Conjunctival Folliculosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Prednisolone acetate is a topical corticosteroid eye drop, marketed in Australia with phenylephrine as Prednefrin Forte. It is used for ocular inflammatory conditions such as uveitis, iridocyclitis and conjunctivitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **conjunctival folliculosis**, but there are **no clinical trials and no publications** supporting this specific prediction (**evidence level L5**).
Of the other predictions, papillary conjunctivitis and vernal conjunctivitis have indirect supporting evidence (L4).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known pharmacology, prednisolone acetate is a glucocorticoid. Topical glucocorticoids suppress ocular surface and intraocular inflammation. Its efficacy in uveitis and conjunctivitis is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to other inflammatory conjunctival conditions.

For conjunctival folliculosis specifically, the link is weak. Folliculosis is typically a benign, often asymptomatic finding that rarely needs anti-inflammatory therapy, so the high model score does not translate into a clear clinical need.

Several other predictions are more plausible. Papillary and giant papillary conjunctivitis involve immune-mediated inflammation of the tarsal conjunctiva. Vernal keratoconjunctivitis is a chronic Th2- and mast cell-mediated allergic disease, and topical corticosteroids are an established treatment for severe flares. Serous non-viral conjunctivitis is likely a subtype of an existing indication rather than a true new use.

Some predictions are clinically doubtful. Acute hemorrhagic conjunctivitis is a self-limiting viral infection where steroids may prolong viral shedding. Parasitic conjunctivitis would need concurrent antiparasitic therapy. Postinfectious vasculitis is systemic, and this product has minimal systemic exposure.

## Safety Considerations

The knowledge graph lists the following conditions as contraindications or cautions for prednisolone acetate (drawn from graph edges, not label text):

- **Ocular:** herpes simplex virus keratitis, open-angle glaucoma, ocular hypertension, eye infectious disease, viral eye infection, fungal infection of eye, ocular tuberculosis, corneal pigmentation, conjunctivitis (infective)
- **Metabolic/systemic:** diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, G6PD deficiency, glycogen storage disease, cystinuria, galactosemia
- **Porphyrias:** porphyria, inherited porphyria, porphyrin metabolism disease

Literature for the predicted indications highlights steroid-induced intraocular pressure elevation, a particular concern with prolonged use and in young patients (for example in vernal disease). Long-term steroid use also carries infection risk.

No drug interaction records were found. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings, contraindications and interactions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The top prediction, conjunctival folliculosis, is supported only by the model score (L5). It has no trials or literature, and the condition rarely needs treatment, so clinical value is doubtful.
- Papillary conjunctivitis and vernal conjunctivitis are the more credible directions (L4, "Research Question"), but the available evidence concerns other drugs and does not directly test prednisolone acetate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications (the approved indication text is missing from the licence record)
- Direct comparative or randomised evidence of prednisolone acetate in vernal or papillary conjunctivitis
- An intraocular pressure and infection monitoring plan for any extended steroid use
- Confirmation of whether a steroid arm exists in NCT04705584
