## Phenylephrine: Nasal cavity disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Phenylephrine is an alpha-1 adrenergic agonist used as a nasal decongestant and eye-drop vasoconstrictor/mydriatic. It is already used for conditions such as allergic rhinitis, the common cold and pharyngitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **nasal cavity disease**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **5 publications**, mostly supportive or indirect.
This prediction largely overlaps with existing indications rather than representing a truly new use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for phenylephrine: "Phenylephrine hydrochloride is an α-1 adrenergic receptor agonist." ([DailyMed: Biorphen (phenylephrine hydrochloride) prescribing information, Dr. Reddy's Laboratories Inc.](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=2f715e4e-b269-b935-d2c9-003cb29770be), label version 2026-03-31; accessed 2026-11-19.) Based on known pharmacology, phenylephrine is an alpha-1 adrenergic agonist. It constricts blood vessels in the nasal mucosa, which reduces congestion, swelling and mucosal bleeding.

The predicted indication, "nasal cavity disease", is a broad category that already includes rhinitis, nasopharyngitis and the common cold, all existing indications for this drug. The very high TxGNN score (0.9997) probably reflects this closeness to established uses rather than a new therapeutic discovery.

The literature shows phenylephrine used in a combination nasal spray (Polydexa with phenylephrine) and as a vasoconstrictor in endoscopic sinus surgery. These are supportive uses, not controlled efficacy studies of phenylephrine alone.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (automated associations, not label text):** hypertension/hypertensive disorder, liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease, epilepsy, COPD, peptic and gastric ulcer, pancreatitis, thrombocytopenia, adrenocortical insufficiency/Addison disease, hypothyroidism, inflammatory bowel disease, psychotic and other mental disorders, porphyria, and others. These likely reflect the corticosteroid component of the combination product as well as phenylephrine, and need verification against the Product Information.
- **Drug Interactions:** 180 interactions were identified, all of the 20 listed being Moderate. Main groups:
  - Antidiabetic agents (e.g. acarbose, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, insulin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, exenatide)
  - Anticholinergics (e.g. atropine ophthalmic, hyoscyamine, clidinium)
  - Diethylpropion
- **Literature caution:** A case report describes toxicity with intranasal phenylephrine combined with cocaine, so use with other sympathomimetics or in hypertensive cardiovascular disease needs care.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for formal warnings, as label-based warnings were not available.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication overlaps heavily with existing rhinitis and nasopharyngitis uses, so it is not a clearly novel repurposing. There are no registered trials, and the supporting literature is mostly combination-product, surgical-adjunct or indirect. The Australian product is an ophthalmic eye drop, not a nasal formulation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications for safety screening
- Controlled studies of phenylephrine alone in a defined nasal condition
- Confirmation of a suitable nasal formulation and route in Australia
- Clarification of which nasal cavity conditions are genuinely new compared with existing indications
