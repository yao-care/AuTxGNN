# Selpercatinib: From RET-Altered Cancers to Pulmonary Hypertension

## One-Sentence Summary

Selpercatinib is a highly selective RET tyrosine kinase inhibitor, developed for RET fusion/mutation-positive cancers (non-small-cell lung cancer and medullary/papillary thyroid cancer).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Pulmonary Hypertension**,
but currently **no clinical trials** and only **2 loosely related publications** (neither addressing pulmonary hypertension directly) support this direction — and available safety information suggests hypertension may be a drug *risk* rather than a treatment benefit.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Selpercatinib is a highly selective RET tyrosine kinase inhibitor. Formal DrugBank mechanism-of-action data was not retrievable for this pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap), but the pipeline's own rationale confirms the RET-selective kinase inhibitor mechanism, consistent with its approved oncology use.

The proposed link to pulmonary hypertension rests on a theoretical association between RET-GDNF signalling and pulmonary vascular remodelling reported in some preclinical models. However, this evidence pack found **no direct evidence** that selpercatinib treats pulmonary hypertension. More importantly, the pack notes that selpercatinib's known safety profile includes **systemic hypertension as an adverse effect** — meaning the pharmacological relationship may run in the opposite direction (cardiovascular risk) rather than supporting a therapeutic benefit.

Given this, the TxGNN score should be read as a knowledge-graph association only, not as mechanistic or clinical support for repurposing.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There is no clinical trial or disease-specific literature supporting selpercatinib for pulmonary hypertension, the drug is not marketed in Australia, and a blocking data gap (missing PI warnings/contraindications) prevents even a preliminary safety assessment. Available safety signals also suggest hypertension may be an adverse effect of selpercatinib rather than a therapeutic target, working against the predicted direction.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/TGA-approved Product Information (warnings, contraindications) — currently a blocking data gap
- Confirmed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Disease-specific preclinical or clinical evidence linking RET inhibition to pulmonary vascular benefit (rather than risk)
- Any future ARTG registration or Australian market status update
