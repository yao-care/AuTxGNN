# Oxcarbazepine: From Partial Epilepsy to Visual Epilepsy

## One-Sentence Summary

Oxcarbazepine is an antiseizure medicine originally used to treat partial (focal) epilepsy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **visual epilepsy** (visually triggered reflex seizures), but there are **no registered clinical trials** and the **16 publications** retrieved are mostly general antiseizure medication reviews, so evidence specific to this indication is very limited.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. From the retrieved literature, oxcarbazepine acts mainly through its active metabolite, a monohydroxy derivative (MHD). It is understood to block voltage-gated sodium channels and limit repetitive neuronal firing, and it is established for partial-onset seizures.

Visually triggered (photosensitive) epilepsy can include focal features, so there is a plausible mechanistic overlap with the original indication. The prediction therefore appears to reflect a shared focal-seizure mechanism.

There are important caveats. The retrieved papers are general antiseizure medication reviews and guidelines, and none evaluates oxcarbazepine in visually induced seizures. Photosensitive generalised epilepsies are typically treated with valproate, levetiracetam or lamotrigine. Sodium channel blockers may aggravate some generalised syndromes.

## Safety Considerations

**Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from drug-disease relationships, not product label text): anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, impaired renal function, inappropriate ADH syndrome (hyponatraemia-related), and nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis.

**Drug interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 interactions. The 20 listed were all of Moderate severity, including hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, cholecalciferol, calcifediol, calcitriol, pioglitazone, saxagliptin, linagliptin, chlorpropamide, aprepitant, morphine, omeprazole, dexlansoprazole, clarithromycin, difenoxin, diphenoxylate, dronabinol and eliglustat.

**Seizure aggravation:** The retrieved literature includes a case report of oxcarbazepine-induced myoclonic status epilepticus in juvenile myoclonic epilepsy. Sodium channel blockers may worsen generalised epilepsy syndromes, which is relevant to photosensitive epilepsy.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a plausible shared focal-seizure mechanism, but there are no clinical trials and no literature testing oxcarbazepine in visually triggered seizures. Sodium channel blockade may aggravate some photosensitive generalised syndromes, and established alternatives exist.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Indication-specific clinical data (case series or controlled studies) in visually triggered epilepsy, with seizure syndrome classification (focal versus generalised)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Product Information warnings and contraindications for the registered Australian product, and its approved indication text
- A safety plan addressing seizure aggravation risk, hyponatraemia, renal impairment and the interactions above
- If a repurposing programme is pursued, consider restless legs syndrome as the more evidence-supported secondary candidate, although it is still limited to case reports
