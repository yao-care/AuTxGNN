## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Silodosin is a selective alpha-1A adrenergic receptor antagonist. It relaxes smooth muscle in the prostate and bladder neck, which eases urinary outflow in BPH. According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for silodosin: "Silodosin is a selective antagonist of post-synaptic alpha-1 adrenoreceptors, which are located in the human prostate, bladder base, bladder neck, prostatic capsule, and prostatic urethra. Blockade of these alpha-1 adrenoreceptors can cause smooth muscle in these tissues to relax, resulting in an improvement in urine flow and a reduction in BPH symptoms." ([DailyMed: silodosin prescribing information, PD-Rx Pharmaceuticals, Inc.](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=94bca72c-c74a-4181-8b6a-a0a2f654a0a7), label version 2026-10-30; accessed 2026-11-19.) This description reflects general knowledge of the drug class.

On current evidence, the prediction is **not mechanistically convincing**. Ambras syndrome is a rare congenital hypertrichosis linked to genomic rearrangements affecting regulation of the *TRPS1* gene. This is not an adrenergic pathway, and alpha-1 blockers are not established hair-growth agents. The very high score (0.9999) comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence.

Other top-ranked predictions show the same pattern. They include hypertrichosis, hair shaft abnormalities, trichomegaly and several congenital malformation syndromes, and none has a clear mechanistic link or any supporting trials or literature.
