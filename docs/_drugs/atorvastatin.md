## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for atorvastatin is not available in the data compiled for this report. It is a statin, and statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase to lower LDL cholesterol. They also have anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects, including reduced monocyte/macrophage activation and lower systemic inflammation markers.

These effects are relevant in HIV. People with HIV on antiretroviral therapy often have chronic immune activation, and protease inhibitors are associated with raised cholesterol and triglycerides. This increases cardiovascular risk. The supporting trials mostly target PI-associated dyslipidaemia, immune activation and inflammation, and HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder, rather than viral suppression.

Atorvastatin is metabolised by CYP3A4, and protease inhibitors and boosters (ritonavir, cobicistat) strongly inhibit this enzyme. Dose limits and monitoring are therefore needed.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. The main ones listed are:
  - **Major**: clarithromycin.
  - **Moderate**: aprepitant, omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, cimetidine, clotrimazole, miconazole, dexamethasone, eliglustat, eluxadoline, metronidazole, naltrexone, pectin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin.
  - **Minor**: aluminium hydroxide, magnesium hydroxide, liraglutide.
- **Relevance to HIV treatment**: protease inhibitors and boosters (ritonavir, cobicistat) strongly inhibit CYP3A4, raising atorvastatin exposure. Dose limits and monitoring for muscle toxicity are needed.
- **Conditions of concern**: the data compiled for this report flag liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease, gallbladder disease, hypothyroidism, myopathy and other muscle disease, rhabdomyolysis, and substance abuse/dependence. These are not taken from label text.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Completed Phase 3 and Phase 2 trials and several randomised studies in people with HIV support the use of atorvastatin for lipid and inflammation-related goals, and there are no completed trials showing antiviral benefit. The trials are small, and the CYP3A4 interactions with protease inhibitors are clinically important.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarify the target outcome (dyslipidaemia and cardiovascular risk in people with HIV, rather than antiviral effect).
- Larger trials with clinical endpoints, since the completed trials enrolled 24 to 53 participants.
- Mechanism of action data and the TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications.
- A dosing and monitoring plan for patients taking protease inhibitors or boosters.
