## One-Sentence Summary

Modafinil is a wake-promoting agent that is already marketed in Australia. The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant to **insomnia**, but the supporting evidence is weak: 2 registered clinical trials and 19 retrieved publications, none of which shows a demonstrated benefit in insomnia. The evidence level is **L5** and the recommended decision is **Hold**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this report. Modafinil is known as a wake-promoting agent that is pharmacologically different from classical stimulants.

The prediction is hard to justify mechanistically. A drug that promotes wakefulness would be expected to work against insomnia treatment, not for it. The retrieved literature describes modafinil mainly for daytime sleepiness and fatigue, including in neurological conditions such as Parkinson's disease. It does not describe use for insomnia itself.

The high TxGNN score (0.9985) is a model output reflecting how closely modafinil sits to sleep-related conditions in the model's data. It is not evidence of therapeutic benefit and should not be read as a clinical claim.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only insomnia-specific trial of modafinil (NCT00124384) has no available results, and the other trial was terminated after 2 participants. The mechanism of a wake-promoting agent also runs counter to the proposed use. The evidence level is therefore L5, and the score alone does not justify progressing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results from NCT00124384, or new controlled trials of modafinil in insomnia
- A clear mechanistic rationale for how a wake-promoting agent could benefit insomnia
- Mechanism of action data
- Review of the TGA Product Information for warnings, contraindications and interaction management
