# Olodaterol: From Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease to Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Olodaterol is a once-daily long-acting beta2-agonist (LABA), originally used for maintenance treatment of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). In Australia it is marketed in combination with tiotropium (Spiolto Respimat).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **bronchitis**, but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **2 publications** (both COPD-focused reviews) behind this prediction.
The prediction is best read as reflecting how closely bronchitis is related to COPD, not as evidence of bronchitis-specific benefit.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on drug class knowledge, olodaterol is a LABA. It activates beta2-adrenoceptors and raises cyclic AMP in airway smooth muscle, causing bronchodilation and reducing airflow obstruction. Its efficacy in COPD is well established.

Chronic bronchitis is a clinical phenotype within COPD, so the link to the approved indication is indirect. It rests on overlap with the COPD population rather than on any bronchitis-specific data. The very high TxGNN score most likely reflects this close disease-ontology relationship.

Acute bronchitis is usually viral and self-limiting, and there is no plausible rationale for LABA maintenance therapy in it. Any benefit would be confined to patients whose bronchitis is part of COPD, which is already covered by the existing label.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The bronchitis prediction has no bronchitis-specific trials and only two COPD-focused reviews behind it, so it rests on the drug class and the disease relationship to COPD. For COPD, the approved indication, there is substantial Phase 3 evidence for olodaterol and the tiotropium/olodaterol combination. That evidence does not demonstrate benefit in bronchitis outside COPD, and it is not a case for repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarification of which bronchitis population is intended (COPD-associated chronic bronchitis versus acute or other bronchitis)
- Bronchitis-specific clinical evidence, such as a registered trial or controlled study
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the PI, followed by a cardiovascular and interaction monitoring plan
