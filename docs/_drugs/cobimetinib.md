# Cobimetinib: From Metastatic Melanoma to Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Cobimetinib is a MEK1/2 inhibitor originally used to treat metastatic melanoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)** with a very high score, but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting this direction, so the prediction is based on computational modelling alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Cobimetinib is known to be a MEK1/2 inhibitor, and its efficacy in BRAF-mutant metastatic melanoma (in combination with a BRAF inhibitor) is established. That role rests on blocking the MAPK/ERK signalling pathway.

A hypothetical link to ALS exists. In preclinical ALS models, MAPK/ERK pathway activation has been implicated in neuroinflammation and motor neuron stress. Blocking this pathway could, in theory, reduce those processes.

This link is speculative. The TxGNN score is high, but it is a computational prediction only, and no trials or publications were found. Cobimetinib's penetration into the central nervous system is also limited (it is a P-glycoprotein substrate), which weakens the translational plausibility.

Other high-scoring predictions follow the same pattern. Most are ALS variants or related motor neuron disorders (for example ALS susceptibility, ALS type 22, Mills syndrome, lower motor neuron syndrome and monomelic amyotrophy). These overlap with the main ALS prediction and should not be counted as independent evidence. The remaining predictions are rare developmental or skeletal conditions with no established mechanistic link.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predictions rest entirely on a computational model. There are no clinical trials or publications for any of the ten predicted indications (evidence level L5), and the mechanistic rationale for ALS is hypothetical and limited by poor CNS penetration. Cobimetinib's toxicity profile is also substantial for diseases that are slowly progressive or self-limiting.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data to support a mechanistic-link analysis
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications for safety screening
- Preclinical evidence in ALS models, including CNS exposure data
- A literature and trial registry search (including ANZCTR) for MEK inhibitors in motor neuron disease
- A risk-benefit assessment given the drug's toxicity profile in the target populations
