# Doxorubicin: From Lymphoma and Leukaemia to Ewing Sarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Doxorubicin is an anthracycline cytotoxic agent, originally used for lymphoblastic leukaemia, Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and related blood cancers.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Ewing sarcoma**, with **28 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Doxorubicin is already a backbone drug in standard multi-agent Ewing sarcoma regimens (VDC/IE), so the prediction is consistent with established clinical use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Doxorubicin is generally understood to be a topoisomerase II inhibitor and DNA intercalator, which is cytotoxic to rapidly dividing tumour cells. This general description comes from standard pharmacology rather than the compiled data.

Its efficacy in lymphoid cancers is established. Ewing sarcoma is also a rapidly proliferating, chemotherapy-sensitive tumour. Doxorubicin is already a core component of the standard multi-agent regimens used there (vincristine, doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide, alternating with ifosfamide and etoposide).

The high TxGNN score (0.999) is therefore in line with current practice. Importantly, doxorubicin's effect cannot be separated from the other drugs in these combinations, because it is almost never given alone in Ewing sarcoma.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (these are model-derived associations, not label text): liver failure and liver disease, hyperbilirubinaemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia, heart disease, cardiomyopathy, congestive heart failure, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest, active infectious disease (including fungal infection), G6PD deficiency, and gallbladder disease.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Major-level interactions include **dolasetron**, **cisapride** and **papaverine**. Moderate-level interactions include **ondansetron, granisetron, palonosetron, aprepitant, rolapitant, dexamethasone, clarithromycin, levofloxacin, famotidine, loperamide and eliglustat**, among others. Several of these are common supportive-care drugs, so antiemetic and supportive regimens need review.
- **Cardiotoxicity**: the literature highlights cumulative anthracycline cardiotoxicity (PMID 30231396), especially important in children and young adults, who make up most Ewing sarcoma patients.

Warnings and contraindications from the TGA Product Information were not available. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several large completed Phase 3 randomised trials and multiple RCTs use doxorubicin-containing regimens as standard therapy in Ewing sarcoma, which supports L1 evidence for the regimen. However, these studies test the combination, not doxorubicin alone, and both formulations on the ARTG are listed without approved indication text.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm the approved indications for Adriamycin and Caelyx from the TGA Product Information, and check whether Ewing sarcoma is already covered or would be off-label
- Obtain the Product Information warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing
- Obtain detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Establish a cardiac monitoring plan with a cumulative dose limit, and consider cardioprotection (e.g. dexrazoxane, as studied in NCT00038142 and NCT00003667)
- Review the interactions with antiemetics and other supportive drugs, particularly the major-level interactions
- Define the paediatric versus adult treatment setting and the supporting regimen (VDC/IE)
