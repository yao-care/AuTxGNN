## Bosentan: endothelin receptor antagonist → rheumatoid arthritis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Bosentan is a dual endothelin receptor antagonist (ET-A/ET-B) used in pulmonary arterial hypertension and related connective tissue conditions. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **rheumatoid arthritis**. No clinical trials are registered for this use, and the supporting literature consists mainly of reviews and animal-model studies, so the evidence is **preclinical and indirect only**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Bosentan is known as a dual endothelin receptor antagonist (ET-A and ET-B), blocking the effects of endothelin-1.

Endothelin-1 is implicated in synovial inflammation, vasculopathy and fibrosis. In a collagen-induced arthritis mouse model, bosentan reduced disease severity, with a proposed role for TNF-α in inducing endothelin system genes. A separate mouse study found that endothelins modulate the inflammatory reaction in zymosan-induced arthritis through LTB4, TNF-α and CXCL-1. Bosentan's use in connective-tissue-disease-associated pulmonary hypertension gives an indirect link to rheumatic disease.

These findings are mechanistic and preclinical. No human trial of bosentan for the joint disease of rheumatoid arthritis was identified, so the prediction remains a research question.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but no clinical trial studies bosentan in rheumatoid arthritis. The supporting evidence is limited to mouse models and indirect reviews of connective-tissue-related pulmonary hypertension. Although the literature includes animal studies of bosentan in arthritis, no study in patients with the disease was identified, and the evidence is best viewed as hypothesis-generating.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data to support the mechanistic-link analysis
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications for a safety review
- Human proof-of-concept data (for example, a small phase 2 trial in rheumatoid arthritis, with attention to liver function monitoring and interactions with usual rheumatoid arthritis therapies)
