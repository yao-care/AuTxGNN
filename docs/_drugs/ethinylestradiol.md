## One-Sentence Summary

Ethinylestradiol is a synthetic estrogen, originally used for conditions such as acne, postmenopausal osteoporosis and postmenopausal atrophic vaginitis, and it is widely marketed in Australia within combined hormonal contraceptives.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **elevated plasma zinc**, but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, ethinylestradiol is a synthetic estrogen used in combined hormonal products (oral contraceptives and a vaginal ring). Its effects in its established uses are well known, but a mechanistic link to elevated plasma zinc cannot be verified from the data compiled for this report.

One plausible but unconfirmed link is that estrogens are known to alter circulating zinc and copper levels, as seen in oral contraceptive users. This is not supported by the data supplied here, and the direction of effect (raising or lowering zinc) is not established.

Elevated plasma zinc is a laboratory finding rather than a condition that clearly calls for drug treatment. The score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence, so it may not point to a true therapeutic opportunity. The very high score (rank 2283 among predictions) should be interpreted with caution.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a high model score, with no clinical trials, no literature and no verified mechanism. Elevated plasma zinc is a laboratory finding rather than a clear treatment target, so the case for repurposing is not established. Safety data from the label is also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank) and a mechanistic link to zinc handling
- A literature review of estrogen and oral contraceptive effects on plasma zinc, including direction of effect
- Clarification of whether elevated plasma zinc is a clinically meaningful treatment target
- Product Information warnings and contraindications from the TGA/PBS source, to allow safety screening
- Approved indication text for the Australian entries
- A review of the contraindication list above, which is particularly relevant for any population with elevated zinc, such as those with kidney or liver disease
