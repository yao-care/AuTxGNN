# Idelalisib: From Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia and Follicular Lymphoma to Mantle Cell Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Idelalisib is an oral PI3K delta inhibitor originally used for B-cell malignancies, including chronic lymphocytic leukaemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL) and follicular lymphoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Mantle Cell Lymphoma**, with **5 clinical trials** (all early-phase, none Phase 3) and **20 publications** (mostly reviews and preclinical studies) currently supporting this direction.
The evidence is early and does not yet show clear efficacy in this disease.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From known pharmacology, idelalisib selectively inhibits PI3K delta, a key enzyme in B-cell receptor signalling that supports the survival and proliferation of malignant B cells.

Mantle cell lymphoma is a B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma that also depends on B-cell receptor and PI3K delta signalling, which is the mechanistic basis for the prediction. Preclinical work shows idelalisib suppresses mantle cell lymphoma cell growth through translation-regulatory mechanisms. Combination strategies (with a p300/CBP inhibitor or palbociclib) and resistance mechanisms (such as CBX5 loss) have also been described.

An important caveat: the literature notes that idelalisib has shown intrinsic resistance in some mantle cell lymphoma models. No Phase 3 evidence supports mantle cell lymphoma specifically.

## Safety Considerations

- **Key Warnings and Contraindications:** Not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI). The literature and trial records describe serious toxicities with idelalisib (hepatotoxicity, severe diarrhoea/colitis, pneumonitis and infections), and an increased rate of deaths and serious adverse events in some trials led to early terminations.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mantle cell lymphoma evidence consists of Phase 1 studies and one terminated Phase 2 study with mixed populations, supported mainly by preclinical and review literature. There is no Phase 3 data, reported resistance, and a well-documented serious toxicity profile. Mechanistic plausibility is reasonable, but it does not yet justify a move forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mantle cell lymphoma-specific efficacy results (for example, published outcomes from NCT01838434)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- The TGA-approved Product Information, including warnings, contraindications and approved indications
- A risk-benefit comparison against established agents such as BTK inhibitors, given idelalisib's toxicity profile
- A safety monitoring plan covering hepatotoxicity, colitis, pneumonitis and infection
