## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the known pharmacology of the sulfone class, dapsone inhibits dihydropteroate synthase in the folate synthesis pathway. This is the same target as sulfonamides such as trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX).

*Pneumocystis jirovecii* depends on this folate pathway, so the mechanism is plausible. Dapsone is already described in the literature as a second-line agent for PCP prophylaxis, particularly for people who cannot tolerate TMP-SMX. It is also used with trimethoprim for mild-to-moderate PCP treatment. The compiled reviews and network meta-analyses describe dapsone-based regimens as established alternatives.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions of concern (from reference relation data, not label text)**: liver disease, gallbladder disease, anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia), G6PD deficiency, glycogen storage disease, cystinuria, galactosaemia, pyruvate dehydrogenase E3 deficiency, and methaemoglobinaemia due to methaemoglobin reductase deficiency.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 potential interactions were identified. The main moderate-level ones include aprepitant, metronidazole, picosulfuric acid, nateglinide, nitisinone, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, tinidazole, activated charcoal and troglitazone.
- **Reported adverse effects in the literature**: methaemoglobinaemia and hypoxia, hepatic complications, acute pancreatitis, and dapsone hypersensitivity syndrome (HLA-B*1301 association).

Please also refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full safety information.
