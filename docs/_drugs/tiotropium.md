## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for tiotropium is not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on known pharmacology, tiotropium is a long-acting muscarinic (M3) receptor antagonist that acts directly on airway smooth muscle, blocking acetylcholine-induced bronchoconstriction — this is the core mechanism underlying treatment of obstructive lung disease (COPD and, in some contexts, asthma).

The predicted indication, "Obstructive Lung Disease," overlaps almost completely with tiotropium's already-established clinical role. This is reflected throughout the evidence: dozens of the listed trials use tiotropium (Spiriva®) as the **active control** against which newer bronchodilators (e.g. olodaterol, indacaterol/glycopyrronium, umeclidinium/vilanterol) are benchmarked, rather than testing tiotropium itself as a novel candidate.

As a result, the model's own scoring rationale notes this is **not true repurposing but confirmation of evidence within the existing label scope**. The prediction is mechanistically sound and well supported, but its practical value lies in validating the model's ability to recover known drug-disease relationships, rather than surfacing a new clinical opportunity.

- Clarification on whether this candidate should be scoped as a genuine repurposing opportunity or reclassified as a standard new-registration assessment, given the near-complete overlap between the predicted and known indications
