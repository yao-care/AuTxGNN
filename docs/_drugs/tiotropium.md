# Tiotropium: From Chronic Airway Diseases (Asthma, Bronchitis, Emphysema) to Obstructive Lung Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Tiotropium is a long-acting inhaled muscarinic antagonist, originally used for airway diseases such as bronchitis, asthma and pulmonary emphysema.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **obstructive lung disease**, with **more than 30 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction.
Note that obstructive lung disease is a parent category of tiotropium's existing indications (including COPD), so this is essentially an on-label use rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, tiotropium is a long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA). It blocks M3 receptors on airway smooth muscle, which reduces cholinergic tone and produces sustained bronchodilation. Published reviews describe slow dissociation from M3 receptors, which allows once-daily dosing.

Obstructive lung disease is the broader category that includes COPD, asthma, emphysema and chronic bronchitis, which are the drug's known indications. The prediction is therefore mechanistically sound, but it largely restates existing use rather than identifying a new one.

The other predictions in the data set (ranks 2 to 10) are weaker. Several are rare or structural conditions (for example tracheal stenosis, interstitial emphysema, Rienhoff syndrome) with no supporting studies and no plausible mechanistic link. They are not recommended for further pursuit at this stage.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text):** benign prostatic hyperplasia, impaired renal function, angle-closure (closed-angle) glaucoma, and bladder neck obstruction. These are consistent with the anticholinergic class and should be checked against the TGA-approved Product Information (PI).
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were identified in total. The main ones are additive anticholinergic effects with other antimuscarinic agents, rated Moderate: hyoscyamine, atropine, glycopyrronium (including topical), clidinium, dicyclomine, mepenzolate, methscopolamine, propantheline, scopolamine and trospium. Other Moderate interactions include eluxadoline, prucalopride and inhaled human insulin. Cimetidine is rated Minor.
- **Ocular safety:** one completed study (NCT06525051, n=108) examined the effect of tiotropium on anterior chamber parameters and intraocular pressure, which is relevant given the glaucoma-related contraindication.

Product-specific warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple large, completed Phase 3 randomised trials and several systematic reviews support tiotropium in COPD and asthma, so the evidence for obstructive lung disease is strong (L1). However, this is an on-label use rather than a true repurposing finding, and the highest-scoring novel predictions have no supporting evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank to replace the general pharmacology description
- The TGA Product Information (warnings, contraindications) for the safety screen
- Confirmation of the approved indication wording on the ARTG entry
- A clear decision on whether the goal is label clarification or new repurposing, since the lower-ranked predictions (ranks 2 to 10) should stay on Hold without further evidence
