## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, riluzole is generally described as a glutamate-modulating agent. Its efficacy in ALS is established, though the survival benefit is modest (roughly 2-3 months in the literature compiled for this report).

The data compiled for this report show no mechanistic link between glutamate modulation and a cortical developmental malformation such as polymicrogyria. The high TxGNN score is a model prediction only, and no trials or publications back it. This prediction should be treated as a hypothesis, not a supported repurposing opportunity.

Other predictions ranked highly by the model are more biologically plausible because they sit close to ALS. These include lower motor neuron syndrome with late-adult onset, monomelic amyotrophy, Mills syndrome and ALS type 22. None of them has trial or literature support in the data either.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions flagged as contraindications or cautions in the knowledge graph** (these come from a drug-disease database, not from approved label text): neutropenia, agranulocytosis, substance abuse/dependence, hyperbilirubinaemia (including hereditary forms and Gilbert syndrome), interstitial lung disease, and drug-induced liver injury.
- **Drug Interactions**: 44 interactions were recorded. Those listed with a Moderate severity include:
  - Obeticholic acid
  - Naltrexone
  - Deferasirox
  - Osilodrostat
  - Anagrelide
  - Asparaginase *Escherichia coli*
  - Brentuximab vedotin
  - Clofarabine
  - Methotrexate
  - Epirubicin
- Minor interactions include omeprazole, caffeine, theophylline and aminophylline.
- **Liver safety**: Published material notes that about 50% of ALS patients on oral riluzole tablets have ALT above the upper limit of normal, 8% above 3× ULN and 2% above 5× ULN.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full safety information, as label warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has only a model score behind it (Evidence Level L5). There are no trials or publications, and no plausible mechanistic link to a cortical malformation. Safety signals, particularly liver injury and neutropenia, add to the case for caution.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism of action data for riluzole
- A plausible biological rationale linking glutamate modulation to polymicrogyria, with preclinical evidence
- Approved indication text and PI warnings and contraindications for the Australian product
- A review of the closer-to-ALS predictions (for example ALS type 22, monomelic amyotrophy, lower motor neuron syndrome), which may be better candidates for further evaluation
