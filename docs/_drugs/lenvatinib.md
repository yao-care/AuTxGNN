## Lenvatinib: From Renal Cell Carcinoma and Thyroid Carcinoma to Liposarcoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Lenvatinib is an oral multi-kinase inhibitor originally used to treat renal cell carcinoma and thyroid carcinoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **liposarcoma**, with **1 clinical trial** (a completed Phase Ib/II study) and **4 publications** (only one of them a clinical study) currently supporting this direction.
Evidence is early-stage and comes from a single-arm study of lenvatinib combined with eribulin.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for lenvatinib was not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the mechanistic rationale compiled, lenvatinib inhibits VEGFR1-3, FGFR1-4, PDGFRα, KIT and RET. This may suppress tumour angiogenesis and growth signalling.

Its efficacy in renal cell carcinoma, a highly vascular tumour, is established. Sarcomas also depend on angiogenic and growth-factor signalling, so the same mechanism may be applicable. In the LEADER study, lenvatinib was paired with eribulin, a microtubule inhibitor. The two drugs are thought to act through complementary anti-angiogenic and cytotoxic mechanisms.

CDK4 amplification is common in well-differentiated and dedifferentiated liposarcoma. This provides biomarker context but is not a direct lenvatinib mechanism. Because the prediction is based on a combination study, the contribution of lenvatinib alone cannot be separated out.

**Drug Interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs; the first 20 returned are summarised below.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails** (as a research question only; not recommended for routine use)

**Rationale:**
- There is one completed single-arm Phase Ib/II study (n=30) of lenvatinib plus eribulin, with a published report. This supports L2 evidence but not a randomised comparison, and the effect of lenvatinib alone cannot be separated from eribulin.
- Lenvatinib is already marketed in Australia, which lowers the barrier to further study. Safety information from the label was not available.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the full LEADER results (response rate, progression-free survival, toxicity) specific to the liposarcoma cohort
- A randomised or comparative trial against current standard therapy in liposarcoma
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Review of the Australian PI for warnings and contraindications, and of the full interaction list (particularly QT-prolonging drugs)
- Clarification of the approved indication text for ARTG 10952K and the funding (PBS) position for any new use
