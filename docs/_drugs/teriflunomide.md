# Teriflunomide: From Multiple Sclerosis to Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Teriflunomide is an oral immunomodulatory drug originally used to treat multiple sclerosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis**. This is essentially the approved use rather than a true repurposing, and it is supported by **13 clinical trials** and **20 publications**, including several Phase 3 trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data was not available in the data compiled for this report. However, teriflunomide is well known to inhibit dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH), an enzyme needed for de novo pyrimidine synthesis. This reduces the proliferation of activated T and B lymphocytes, which drive the inflammation seen in multiple sclerosis (MS).

Relapsing-remitting MS is the most common course of MS, so the predicted indication is a subtype of the original indication. The prediction is therefore consistent with existing knowledge and is not a new-use signal. Phase 3 trials and active-comparator studies support its efficacy and safety.

Other conditions also appeared in the prediction list. The most notable is thrombocytopenia, specifically immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), where three Phase 2 trials are registered with no reported results. This is an early-stage research question only. The remaining predictions (for example hereditary thrombocytopenias, fibromatoses and chronic hepatitis C) have a model score only and no supporting trials or literature.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The data compiled for this report list 100 interactions, with 20 shown. Most flagged are Major, including:
  - Corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone)
  - Antidiabetic agents (acarbose, pioglitazone)
  - Bupropion, acetylsalicylic acid, clarithromycin, minocycline
  - Chenodeoxycholic acid, oxandrolone, oxymetholone
- Moderate interactions include alosetron, cimetidine, empagliflozin and sitagliptin. Minor interactions include zinc sulfate and zinc acetate.
- **Additional guardrails noted in the analysis**: monitor for hepatotoxicity, avoid use in pregnancy because of teratogenicity, and use the accelerated elimination procedure when required. Haematological adverse effects are a recognised concern.

Product-specific warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Teriflunomide's efficacy in relapsing MS is established by Phase 3 RCTs, and it has been used widely as an active comparator in later pivotal trials. The prediction mostly confirms an existing indication. Other predicted conditions are either model-only (Hold) or, for ITP, early Phase 2 research with a possible haematological safety signal.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the TGA-approved PI for warnings, contraindications and the approved indication wording (this data was unavailable)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Liver function monitoring and pregnancy-prevention plans, including the accelerated elimination protocol
- Review of the major drug interactions, particularly with other hepatotoxic or immunosuppressive agents
- For ITP: results from the registered Phase 2 trials before any further consideration
