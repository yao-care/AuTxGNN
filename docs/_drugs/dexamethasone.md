## Dexamethasone: From Adrenocortical Insufficiency and Inflammatory Conditions to Alopecia Areata
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Dexamethasone is a potent glucocorticoid originally used for conditions such as adrenocortical insufficiency, Crohn disease, acute lymphoblastic leukaemia and congenital adrenal hyperplasia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**,
with **no registered clinical trials** but **several publications** (including one randomised controlled trial in children, a network meta-analysis and multiple cohort studies) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available for this drug. Based on known information, dexamethasone is a potent glucocorticoid with broad immunosuppressive and anti-inflammatory effects, and its efficacy in immune-mediated conditions such as Crohn disease and eosinophilic pneumonia is established.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune attack on the hair follicle, with collapse of the follicle's immune privilege. Dexamethasone is thought to suppress cytotoxic T-cell activity and pro-inflammatory cytokine signalling around the follicle, which plausibly supports hair regrowth. Oral mini-pulse dosing (for example, 5 mg on two consecutive days per week) is an established regimen in the dermatology literature.

The other nine model predictions (for example alopecia mucinosa, telogen effluvium and folliculitis decalvans) rest mainly on the model score. They have little or no supporting evidence and are not assessed further here.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level contraindications from the knowledge graph** (not label text): hypertension, epilepsy, hyperglycaemia, congestive heart failure, herpes simplex virus keratitis, measles, urea cycle disorders (for example citrullinemia, hyperargininemia) and various pituitary hormone deficiency conditions.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded, 20 shown in the data compiled for this report). The main ones are:
  - **Major:** fentanyl, adalimumab
  - **Moderate:** paclitaxel, abemaciclib, acalabrutinib, isotretinoin, ibuprofen, ketorolac, nifedipine, acetazolamide, acarbose, zidovudine, codeine, dihydrocodeine, acebutolol, tetanus toxoid vaccine
  - **Minor:** dolutegravir, lidocaine, formoterol

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for formal warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Alopecia areata has a plausible immune-mediated rationale for dexamethasone and a body of published clinical experience, including one small RCT in children and a network meta-analysis. However, there are no registered trials, the evidence is largely small or observational, and the only Australian product is an intravitreal implant.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the TGA-approved Product Information, since label warnings and contraindications were not available
- Confirmation of a suitable systemic dexamethasone product and route in Australia (the registered product is intravitreal)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A comparison against current standard-of-care options, including JAK inhibitors
- A safety and monitoring plan for repeated systemic steroid pulses, especially in children and patients with hypertension, diabetes or heart failure
