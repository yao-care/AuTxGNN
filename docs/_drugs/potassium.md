## Potassium: From an Unspecified Original Indication to Hypertensive Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Potassium is an essential electrolyte, marketed in Australia as oral powder products (Kindergen and Renastart), and the approved indication text is not available in the data compiled for this report.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **hypertensive disorder**, with a score of 0.9916.
The evidence includes **23 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, but most trials involve potassium only as a salt component of other drugs. The strongest support comes from meta-analyses of potassium intake and blood pressure.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for this product is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the published literature, increased potassium intake promotes sodium excretion (natriuresis), may relax blood vessels through effects on potassium channels and Na+/K+-ATPase in endothelial and vascular smooth muscle cells, and blunts the blood-pressure-raising effect of sodium.

Dose-response meta-analyses of randomised controlled trials, together with a systematic review and meta-analysis, support a blood-pressure-lowering effect of higher potassium intake. This is a nutritional and electrolyte mechanism, so the relationship to the original indication is unclear. The original indication could not be determined from the data available.

Other predicted indications (pulmonary hypertension, malignant renovascular hypertension and malignant hypertensive renal disease) have little or no direct clinical evidence. They should be treated as computational predictions only and kept on hold.

## Safety Considerations

Specific warnings, contraindications and drug interaction data were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for safety information.

From the repurposing assessment, the main concern is **hyperkalaemia**. This is particularly relevant in:
- Chronic kidney disease or impaired potassium handling
- Patients taking RAAS inhibitors (ACE inhibitors, ARBs) or potassium-sparing diuretics

Serum potassium and renal function would need monitoring.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Meta-analyses of randomised trials support a blood-pressure-lowering effect of higher potassium intake, but direct trial evidence for these specific products is limited. Most listed trials only involve potassium salts of other drugs, and the hyperkalaemia risk is significant in common hypertensive populations. The other four predicted indications should remain on **Hold**, because they rest on prediction scores or indirect mechanistic literature alone.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Approved indication text and PI safety information (warnings, contraindications, interactions) for the Australian products
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Clarification of the original indication and its relationship to hypertension
- A patient selection and monitoring plan (serum potassium, renal function, concomitant RAAS inhibitors and potassium-sparing diuretics)
- Review of dose and formulation, since the Australian products are oral powders and may not suit chronic blood pressure management
- Adequately powered trials of potassium supplementation specifically in hypertension, ideally including Australian patients
