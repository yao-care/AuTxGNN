## One-Sentence Summary

Eltrombopag is an oral thrombopoietin receptor (TPO-R) agonist marketed in Australia. The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant to **HIV infectious disease**, with a score of **0.9926**. The studies found concern platelet and blood-count problems in people with HIV, or other conditions. None tests eltrombopag as a treatment for HIV infection itself, so the evidence level is **L5**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Eltrombopag is known to be a TPO-R agonist. It stimulates megakaryocyte growth and platelet production.

In people with HIV, low platelets can be immune-mediated or caused by bone marrow suppression. Stimulating the TPO receptor could raise platelet counts in these patients. This benefit would be supportive, correcting a blood-count problem. It would not be antiviral.

One laboratory screen of approved drugs (PMID 32977702) suggests eltrombopag might modulate HIV-1 proviral transcription. This is hypothesis-generating only. The published clinical reports describe eltrombopag treating HIV-associated blood-count problems, not HIV infection.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No cited study tests eltrombopag as a treatment for HIV infection. The available reports concern platelet or blood-count problems in people with HIV, and the one registered Phase 3 trial was terminated and involved chronic liver disease. This is model prediction only (L5).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarify whether the intended goal is treating HIV infection or supporting HIV-associated thrombocytopenia or marrow failure, since the existing reports support only the latter.
- Prospective controlled studies in the chosen HIV population, with clear platelet-count and bleeding endpoints.
- Mechanism of action data to assess the preliminary in vitro finding on HIV-1 transcription.
- Review of the TGA Product Information for safety, and a monitoring plan for people with HIV who take antiretroviral therapy.
