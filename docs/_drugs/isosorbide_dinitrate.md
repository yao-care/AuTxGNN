## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Isosorbide dinitrate is known as an NO donor and vasodilator. A hypothetical link to alopecia is improved scalp microcirculation, similar in concept to minoxidil, which is a vasodilator and potassium channel opener.

This link is speculative. No trials or literature support it, and a high TxGNN score is not clinical evidence. The model's other hair-related predictions include hypertrichosis (excess hair growth, the opposite of alopecia) and hereditary hair disorders. This suggests the score comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence or a drug-specific mechanism.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The alopecia prediction has no supporting trials or publications, and its mechanistic basis is hypothetical. Systemic vasodilation also carries blood pressure risks that would need to be weighed against any hair benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Preclinical or early clinical evidence that NO donation affects hair growth in alopecia
- A scalp-appropriate formulation or route, since only a sublingual tablet is currently registered
- Safety data on hypotension risk in a non-cardiac population, and the TGA PI warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data to support the proposed link

Other predictions in the same analysis have more supporting data and may be better candidates for follow-up. Pulmonary hypertension has a body of small haemodynamic studies, and vascular disease has one completed Phase 3 trial in diabetic foot ulcers (NCT02789033, n=68, results not available).
