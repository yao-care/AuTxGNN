# Isoleucine: From Maple Syrup Urine Disease Dietary Management to Gastroparesis

## One-Sentence Summary

Isoleucine is an essential branched-chain amino acid. In Australia it is marketed in single-ingredient and amino acid formula products used in the dietary management of maple syrup urine disease (MSUD).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **gastroparesis**, but there are currently **no clinical trials** and **no publications** supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, isoleucine is a branched-chain amino acid used in nutrition and metabolic formulas. Its role in the original setting appears to be nutritional and metabolic, and any mechanistic link to gastroparesis remains speculative.

Gastroparesis involves delayed gastric emptying caused by impaired motility (for example, diabetic or idiopathic causes). The data compiled for this report do not establish a pathway from isoleucine to this condition. One speculative route is through nutrient and amino acid sensing that may influence gastric emptying, but nothing in the data supports it.

The high TxGNN score (99.32%) is a computational prediction only. It should be treated as a hypothesis for investigation, not as evidence of efficacy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone (Evidence Level L5). There are no clinical trials, no literature and no established mechanism. The knowledge graph also flags a number of renal, cardiac and metabolic contraindication signals that have not been assessed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank) and a plausible pathway linking isoleucine to gastric motility
- A literature and trial registry search (including ANZCTR) for amino acid effects on gastric emptying
- Official Product Information warnings and contraindications for the registered products, and review of the knowledge-graph contraindication signals
- Confirmation of the approved indications for the ARTG entries
- Assessment of route and formulation suitability for gastroparesis
- Preclinical or small exploratory studies before any clinical consideration
