# Formaldehyde: From Influenza to Leishmaniasis, Diffuse Cutaneous

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Formaldehyde is known as a non-selective cross-linking agent and tissue fixative. It reacts with proteins and nucleic acids, which is why it is used to inactivate viruses and toxins in vaccine production and to preserve tissue.

The link between influenza and diffuse cutaneous leishmaniasis is weak. The first is a viral respiratory infection, and the second is a parasitic skin disease caused by an intracellular protozoan. The high TxGNN score appears to be a graph-based association. It does not reflect a demonstrated therapeutic mechanism.

Formaldehyde's cross-linking activity is not selective. It cannot be used safely in the body to target intracellular *Leishmania* parasites. It is also classified as a human carcinogen (IARC Group 1), so there is no plausible route to systemic therapeutic use for this disease.

Other predictions in the data compiled for this report show the same pattern. Mucocutaneous leishmaniasis, mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase deficiency and hemophagocytic syndromes have no supporting evidence. The literature retrieved for pyelonephritis, streptococcal pneumonia, trigonitis, malaria and X-linked lymphoproliferative syndrome describes formaldehyde as a laboratory fixative, a vaccine manufacturing reagent or an occupational hazard. None of it evaluates formaldehyde as a treatment.

## Literature Evidence

Currently no related literature available for diffuse cutaneous leishmaniasis.

Literature was retrieved for other lower-ranked predicted indications, but none of it evaluates formaldehyde as a treatment:

- **Pyelonephritis:** Four papers cover nephrocalcinosis, a hamster lymphoma agent, a soil microbe as a uropathogen and an animal vaccine study. A loose indirect link exists through methenamine, which releases formaldehyde in acidic urine.
- **X-linked lymphoproliferative syndrome:** The papers are mainly occupational epidemiology (for example, [PMID 31870335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31870335/), a 2019 meta-analysis, and [PMID 41146278](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41146278/), a 2025 case-control study) linking formaldehyde exposure to lymphoid malignancy risk. This points toward harm, not benefit.
- **Plasmodium falciparum malaria, streptococcal pneumonia and trigonitis:** The papers use formaldehyde as a fixative or vaccine reagent, or are anatomical and laboratory method studies.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for safety information.

The literature compiled for this report consistently identifies formaldehyde as a human carcinogen (IARC Group 1). Occupational exposure has been associated with nasopharyngeal cancer and leukaemia, and possibly with lymphoma subtypes. No drug interactions were found in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

The prediction rests on a graph-based score alone, with no clinical trials and no relevant publications. Formaldehyde is a non-selective, carcinogenic cross-linking agent, so there is no plausible route to safe therapeutic use for diffuse cutaneous leishmaniasis or the other predicted conditions.
