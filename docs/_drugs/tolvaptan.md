## Tolvaptan: Renal-hepatic-pancreatic dysplasia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Tolvaptan is a selective vasopressin V2 receptor antagonist, originally used for syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH), nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis and polycystic kidney disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **renal-hepatic-pancreatic dysplasia**, supported by **20 publications** (including 2 pivotal RCTs and a Cochrane systematic review) but **no registered clinical trials** in the data compiled for this report.
This prediction is best seen as a subtype-level extension of an already established indication (ADPKD), not a new repurposing opportunity.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Tolvaptan is a selective vasopressin V2 receptor antagonist. In polycystic kidney disease, vasopressin-driven cAMP signalling in renal tubular cells promotes cyst growth and fluid secretion. Blocking the V2 receptor lowers cAMP and slows cyst expansion. Preclinical work and the clinical literature (for example, van Gastel and Torres, 2017) support this vasopressin pathway as a disease driver.

Polycystic kidney disease is already among tolvaptan's original indications. The predicted disease is a subtype-level variant (PKD3, with or without polycystic liver disease) of the same cystic kidney biology. The prediction is therefore mechanistically coherent, but it adds little beyond the existing ADPKD indication. Any use would remain limited to patients with rapidly progressing disease.

Detailed mechanism of action data was not available in the structured drug record, so the mechanism above comes from the repurposing rationale and the published literature.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (not label text): liver failure, cirrhosis of liver, substance abuse/dependence, active peptic ulcer disease, demyelinating disease.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were identified, of which the following are examples.
  - **Major:** clarithromycin.
  - **Moderate:** aprepitant, dexamethasone, empagliflozin, loperamide, miconazole, naltrexone, rifaximin, rolapitant and several potassium preparations (citrate, bicarbonate, chloride, gluconate).
  - **Minor:** fidaxomicin, naloxegol, prucalopride.
- **Guardrails from the repurposing assessment:** hepatotoxicity monitoring with regular liver testing, aquaresis-related adverse effects (such as thirst and polyuria), and use limited to patients with rapidly progressing disease.

Label-based warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is well established and pivotal RCTs support tolvaptan in ADPKD, so the PKD3 subtype prediction is credible. Because this is an extension of an existing indication and hepatotoxicity is a known risk, use needs structured safeguards.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of the TGA-approved indication text for Jinarc (ARTG 11588X), which was blank in the data compiled
- Label warnings and contraindications from the TGA PI, including liver monitoring requirements
- Subtype-specific data for PKD3 with or without polycystic liver disease, as the pivotal trials covered ADPKD broadly
- Patient selection criteria (rapidly progressing disease) and a liver safety monitoring plan
- Review of the major clarithromycin interaction and other CYP3A-related interactions in prescribing guidance

Other predictions in the list (ranks 2 to 10) are mostly scores from the TxGNN model (deep learning) with no clinical support; these scores may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence. They should remain on Hold, with Joubert syndrome with renal defect flagged only as a research question requiring preclinical evaluation, since a urinary concentrating defect in nephronophthisis may make V2 antagonism unsuitable.
