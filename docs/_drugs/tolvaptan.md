# Tolvaptan: From Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (ADPKD) to Polycystic Kidney Disease 3 (PKD3)

## One-Sentence Summary

Tolvaptan is a vasopressin V2-receptor antagonist with an internationally established role in slowing cyst growth and kidney function decline in autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD). The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Polycystic Kidney Disease 3 (with or without polycystic liver disease)**, a rarer PKD1-contiguous-gene subtype, with **2 pivotal Phase 3 RCTs** and **20 supporting publications** currently underpinning this direction — though none of the trials enrolled PKD3 patients specifically.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed formal mechanism-of-action documentation for tolvaptan is not available in this evidence pack (data gap). However, the repurposing rationale itself sets out the pharmacological pathway: tolvaptan antagonises the vasopressin V2 receptor (V2R) in the renal collecting duct, which suppresses intracellular cAMP signalling. Because cAMP is a key driver of cyst-lining epithelial proliferation and fluid secretion, blocking this pathway slows cyst growth and kidney volume expansion — the exact mechanism for which tolvaptan is already approved in classic ADPKD.

PKD3 sits within the same disease spectrum as ADPKD. It typically arises from a contiguous gene deletion involving *PKD1* (and, in the TSC2/PKD1 contiguous gene syndrome variant, *TSC2*), producing a phenotype that overlaps substantially with typical *PKD1*-driven ADPKD — progressive renal cyst formation, frequently with co-existing polycystic liver disease. Because the downstream cystogenic pathway (V2R–cAMP–proliferation) is shared, the biological rationale for extrapolating tolvaptan's effect from ADPKD to PKD3 is mechanistically sound.

That said, the strength of this prediction rests on evidence generated in *typical* ADPKD (TEMPO 3:4 and REPRISE), not in genetically confirmed PKD3 cohorts. The extrapolation is biologically plausible but currently unproven in this specific subgroup — this distinction should be made clear to prescribers before treatment decisions are guided by it.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed pivotal Phase 3 RCTs (TEMPO 3:4, REPRISE) and a strong supporting literature base establish tolvaptan's efficacy in classic ADPKD via the V2R–cAMP cystogenesis pathway, and this mechanism plausibly extends to PKD3. However, no trial has specifically enrolled or confirmed efficacy in genetically defined PKD3 patients, and critical local safety/regulatory data (TGA PI, ARTG status, hepatotoxicity monitoring protocol) are currently missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TGA-approved Product Information (warnings, contraindications, hepatotoxicity monitoring requirements) — currently a blocking data gap
- Confirmation of ARTG registration status and available formulations in Australia
- Formal drug-target/MOA documentation to replace the current data gap
- PKD3-specific case series or genetic-subgroup analyses from existing ADPKD trial cohorts, if available
- Specialist nephrology/genetics input on applicability to *PKD1*-contiguous gene deletion phenotypes before any guideline-level recommendation
