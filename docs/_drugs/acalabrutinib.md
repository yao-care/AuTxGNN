# Acalabrutinib: From Mantle Cell Lymphoma to Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (Familial)

## One-Sentence Summary

Acalabrutinib is an oral Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor, originally used for mantle cell lymphoma and other B-cell neoplasms.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **lymphoma, non-Hodgkin, familial**,
with **14 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently linked to this prediction. Note that the "familial" label is a knowledge-graph term, and the clinical evidence applies to B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) in general.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, acalabrutinib is a covalent BTK inhibitor. BTK is a central node in B-cell receptor signalling, which drives survival and proliferation in many B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphomas.

The predicted indication sits in the same disease class as the original indications (mantle cell lymphoma and other B-cell neoplasms). This is largely an expansion within the original indication class rather than true repurposing. Trials in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), follicular and other indolent NHL, and relapsed/refractory aggressive B-cell lymphoma support the biological plausibility.

The "familial" subtype is an ontology label. No evidence specific to familial NHL was found.

## Safety Considerations

No warnings or contraindications were available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for safety information.

**Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified, and the first 20 were reviewed. Major-level interactions include:
- Aprepitant
- Acetylsalicylic acid
- Clarithromycin
- Dexlansoprazole
- Esomeprazole
- Lansoprazole

Moderate-level interactions include antacids (aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magnesium salts, magaldrate), H2 blockers (cimetidine, famotidine), clotrimazole, dexamethasone, fenfluramine and dexfenfluramine, glycerol phenylbutyrate, and metreleptin. Acid-reducing agents and CYP3A modulators warrant particular attention when prescribing.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Acalabrutinib is an established BTK inhibitor in B-cell malignancies, and several Phase 2 trials, including randomised combination studies in DLBCL and Richter's syndrome, support use across B-cell NHL. The prediction largely extends within the original indication class. The evidence is Phase 2 level, mostly combination studies, and nothing is specific to the familial subtype.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Product Information warnings and contraindications for the Australian product (currently missing)
- The approved indication text for the ARTG entry
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Results from the ongoing randomised Phase 2 trials (NCT04546620, NCT03899337)
- A review of the interactions with acid-reducing agents and CYP3A modulators

**Other predicted indications (for reference):**
- **Neoplasm of mature B-cells** (L2, Proceed with Guardrails): largely overlaps with the approved indication class.
- **Lymph node cancer** (L3, Research Question): the literature is mostly CLL/SLL-focused and several items are not about acalabrutinib efficacy.
- **MALT lymphomas (small intestine, thyroid, breast)** (L5, Research Question): mechanistically plausible but prediction only.
- **Tonsillar lymphoma, small intestinal Burkitt lymphoma** (Hold): plausible but without direct evidence.
- **Colon adenocarcinoma, polyclonal hypergammaglobulinemia** (Hold): no established mechanistic link and no supporting evidence.
