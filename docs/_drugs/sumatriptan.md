# Sumatriptan: From Migraine to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Sumatriptan is a 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist, originally used for acute treatment of migraine and cluster headache.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura** (score 99.7%), but this prediction is a close extension of the approved migraine indication and the data compiled for this report contain **no clinical trials** and **18 publications** (mostly reviews) with no subtype-specific evidence.
The wider headache-related predictions (headache disorder, trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia) are better supported, but they largely overlap with existing indications.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From the published literature, sumatriptan is a selective 5-HT1B/1D agonist. It is thought to act by constricting cranial blood vessels and inhibiting release of vasoactive neuropeptides from trigeminal nerve endings.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a migraine subtype and shares core pathophysiology with the approved indication (trigeminovascular activation, CGRP release). The high TxGNN score likely reflects this close relationship rather than a true repurposing signal.

There is also a caution. Triptans have historically been used carefully in brainstem aura because of theoretical vasoconstriction concerns, and the literature supplied contains no subtype-specific trials.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (these are graph-derived associations, not label text):
- Cardiovascular and cerebrovascular: coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, congestive heart failure, heart disease, cardiovascular disease, peripheral vascular disease, stroke, cerebral infarction, cerebrovascular disorder, hypertension
- Hepatic and renal: liver disease, liver failure, kidney disease
- Other: epilepsy, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, substance abuse/dependence, blood coagulation disease, obesity, gallbladder disease, haemoglobinopathy and anaemia

**Drug interactions** (100 recorded; main ones listed):
- **Major:** dexfenfluramine, fenfluramine, lorcaserin, sibutramine (serotonergic agents), and 5-HT3 antagonists dolasetron, granisetron, ondansetron, palonosetron
- **Moderate:** morphine (including liposomal)

Other recorded interactions have unknown severity.

Other points from the literature and trial data: medication-overuse headache risk, and the theoretical vasoconstriction concern in brainstem aura. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- For migraine with brainstem aura, the TxGNN score is high but the evidence is model-based only (L4): there are no trials and no subtype-specific literature. The prediction mostly mirrors the approved migraine indication, and there is a theoretical vascular safety concern in this subtype.
- Headache disorder and trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia (L1) could be assessed as "Proceed with Guardrails", but they are on-label extensions rather than novel repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Subtype-specific clinical data on triptan safety and efficacy in brainstem aura
- Mechanism of action data and TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications (a blocking data gap for safety screening)
- Confirmation of the approved indication text for ARTG 10694W
- For cluster headache, confirmation of availability of subcutaneous or intranasal sumatriptan in Australia
- A cardiovascular screening plan and a serotonergic interaction review
