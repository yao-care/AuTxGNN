Alprazolam is a benzodiazepine that enhances GABA-A receptor signalling. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **insomnia**, with a prediction score of 0.9981. ## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known class pharmacology, alprazolam is a benzodiazepine that enhances GABA-A receptor signalling, which produces sedative and hypnotic effects.

This gives a plausible mechanistic link to insomnia, particularly insomnia that co-occurs with anxiety. This link rests on general class pharmacology rather than on data supplied for this report.

Benzodiazepines are generally limited to short-term use for insomnia because of tolerance, dependence, next-day impairment and fall risk. These limits weigh heavily on any repurposing decision.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L3. The only directly relevant study is a retrospective observational study in which alprazolam was part of the treatment for insomnia in coronary heart disease patients, and there are no registered trials or randomised data. Known benzodiazepine risks (tolerance, dependence, next-day impairment, falls) and a major interaction with opioids further limit the case for repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Randomised controlled trial evidence of alprazolam specifically for insomnia
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Safety information from the TGA-approved Product Information, including warnings and contraindications
- A plan for short-term use limits and monitoring in higher-risk groups (older adults, patients taking opioids)
