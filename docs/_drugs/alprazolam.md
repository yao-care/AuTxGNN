# Alprazolam: From Anxiety Disorder to Insomnia

## One-Sentence Summary

Alprazolam is a benzodiazepine originally used to treat anxiety disorders and mixed anxiety and depressive disorder.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **insomnia**, but **no clinical trials** and only **indirect publications** currently support this direction.
The data compiled for this report show stronger support for the lower-ranked predictions of agoraphobia and phobic disorder, which largely overlap with alprazolam's existing anxiety and panic uses.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, alprazolam is a benzodiazepine that enhances GABA-A receptor-mediated inhibition. This produces anxiolytic, sedative and hypnotic effects, which gives a plausible link to insomnia.

Anxiety and insomnia frequently occur together, and sedative-hypnotic effects are shared across benzodiazepines. However, the retrieved literature is largely indirect. It includes a panic disorder review, pharmacokinetic and utilisation studies, a COVID-19 insomnia survey, and a zolpidem deprescribing study. No trial of alprazolam specifically for insomnia was identified. Alprazolam is not an established insomnia therapy, and dependence, tolerance and fall risks are significant concerns.

## Safety Considerations

No label-based warnings or contraindications were available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for safety information.

**Contraindications or cautions from the knowledge graph (not label text):** anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, open-angle and angle-closure glaucoma, obesity, obstructive sleep apnoea and other sleep apnoea syndromes, congenital central hypoventilation syndrome, and substance-related disorders or dependence. Sleep apnoea and substance dependence are particularly relevant to any use in insomnia.

**Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; examples):
- **Major:** morphine (including liposomal morphine), with a risk of additive central nervous system and respiratory depression.
- **Moderate:** aprepitant, bupropion, cimetidine, clarithromycin, clotrimazole, miconazole, dronabinol, eliglustat, glycerol phenylbutyrate, metoclopramide.
- **Minor:** antacids (aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magnesium salts, magaldrate) and corticosteroids (betamethasone, dexamethasone).

**Literature safety signals:** dependence, withdrawal and rebound on discontinuation, memory impairment, fall risk in older people, and potential birth defect concerns with benzodiazepine use in pregnancy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The insomnia prediction rests on a plausible class-wide sedative effect, but there are no clinical trials and no direct evidence for alprazolam in insomnia, and the dependence and fall risks are substantial. The better-supported predictions (agoraphobia, phobic disorder) mostly reflect existing anxiety and panic uses rather than true repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data to support mechanistic analysis
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications for safety screening
- Controlled trial evidence of alprazolam in insomnia, compared with established hypnotics
- A clear assessment of benefit versus dependence, tolerance and fall risk, particularly in older patients and those with sleep apnoea
- If pursuing agoraphobia or phobic disorder, a review of how far the evidence in panic disorder extends beyond the current labelled anxiety indications
