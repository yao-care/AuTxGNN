# Cefuroxime: From Bacterial Infections (Otitis Media, Pneumonia, Meningitis and Others) to Urinary Tract Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Cefuroxime is a second-generation cephalosporin antibiotic, originally used for infections such as otitis media, bacterial pneumonia, meningitis, gonorrhoea and Lyme disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **urinary tract infection**, the highest-ranked prediction with any substantive supporting evidence.
This is supported by **5 registered clinical trials** (mostly observational or prophylaxis studies) and **20 publications**, but there is no Phase 3 RCT with cefuroxime as the investigational arm.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this report. Based on known pharmacology, cefuroxime is a beta-lactam cephalosporin that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis and is bactericidal. It is active against many Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms.

Urinary tract infections are commonly caused by Enterobacterales such as *E. coli*, which are within cefuroxime's spectrum. Cefuroxime is also excreted renally, giving high urinary concentrations. Its established use in other bacterial infections makes this a plausible extension.

This is an on-class antibacterial use rather than novel repurposing. Urinary tract infection is not among the indications listed in the data compiled for this report, although clinical use is well established. Local resistance patterns, including ESBL-producing organisms, limit empirical use in some settings.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications and conditions of concern (from the knowledge graph, not label text):** kidney disease, pyoureter, liver disease, gallbladder disease, blood coagulation disease, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotising enterocolitis and congenital prothrombin deficiency.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified; all those listed are Moderate severity. The main groups are:
  - Acid-suppressing drugs: famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, nizatidine, and the proton pump inhibitors omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole, pantoprazole and rabeprazole
  - Antacids: aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium carbonate, magnesium oxide and magaldrate
  - Aminoglycosides: kanamycin and neomycin
  - Balsalazide

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Cefuroxime has a plausible, on-class rationale for urinary tract infection, with supporting reviews and a randomised comparison with aztreonam in upper UTI. However, the trial evidence is observational or prophylaxis-focused, and no Phase 3 RCT with cefuroxime as the investigational arm was identified. Other predictions (for example hyperviscosity syndrome, hyperamylasemia and analbuminemia) have no supporting evidence and should remain on Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the TGA Product Information, since the Australian licence data did not include approved indication text or warnings
- Confirmation of whether UTI falls within the approved or guideline-supported use of the Zinnat tablet
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Australian local resistance data (including ESBL prevalence) for common uropathogens
- Dose and duration guidance, with attention to renal impairment, given the knowledge graph flags kidney disease
- Monitoring plan for *C. difficile* colitis and interactions with acid-suppressing drugs and antacids
