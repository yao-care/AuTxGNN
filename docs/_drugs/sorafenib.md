## One-Sentence Summary

Sorafenib is an oral multi-kinase inhibitor, originally used to treat hepatocellular carcinoma, renal cell carcinoma and thyroid carcinoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **liposarcoma**,
but direct evidence is thin: **1 clinical trial** (a Phase 2 study in mixed soft tissue sarcomas) and **8 publications**, mostly reviews and preclinical work, are currently linked to this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the wider information, sorafenib inhibits the RAF/MEK/ERK signalling pathway and receptor tyrosine kinases including VEGFR, PDGFR and c-KIT. These pathways drive tumour angiogenesis and proliferation in several cancers, which is why it is used in hepatocellular and renal cell carcinoma.

Soft tissue sarcomas also rely on these pathways. Preclinical work shows sorafenib inhibits MAPK signalling and growth in sarcoma-type cell lines, including malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour and dedifferentiated liposarcoma cell lines.

However, the supporting evidence concerns soft tissue sarcoma as a group. Liposarcoma-specific clinical data are limited, and liposarcoma is historically less responsive to this class than angiosarcoma or other vascular subtypes. Current sarcoma reviews emphasise histology-driven treatment, with trabectedin, doxorubicin and ifosfamide as the established options for liposarcoma.

Minor interactions include dapagliflozin, naloxegol and metronidazole.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but the supporting evidence is indirect: a single mixed-sarcoma Phase 2 trial, reviews, and preclinical data. Liposarcoma has better-established options, and no liposarcoma-specific efficacy signal is available.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Subtype-specific results for liposarcoma from the completed Phase 2 trials (NCT00217620 and SWOG S0505)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the TGA Product Information, and the approved indication text for the ARTG entry
- A review of the knowledge-graph contraindication signals and the major dolasetron interaction before any new-use protocol is designed

The remaining predictions (including renal cell carcinoma subtypes, which largely overlap the existing indication) have no supporting trials or sorafenib-specific literature and are rated Hold.
