## One-Sentence Summary

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Vinblastine is generally known as a vinca alkaloid: it binds tubulin, disrupts microtubule assembly and arrests cells in mitosis. This is plausibly relevant to rhabdomyosarcoma, a highly proliferative paediatric sarcoma.

Vincristine, a related vinca alkaloid, is a standard member of paediatric sarcoma regimens. Vinblastine would therefore be a class-based extension rather than an independently validated option. Preclinical work in human rhabdomyosarcoma xenografts has examined the selectivity of vinca alkaloids, and case reports describe vinblastine as part of combination regimens. The specific contribution of vinblastine cannot be separated from the other agents used.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence for rhabdomyosarcoma is limited to case reports of vinblastine-containing combination regimens (L4), with no registered trials. The effect of vinblastine alone cannot be separated from the other agents, and vincristine is the established vinca alkaloid in paediatric sarcoma regimens.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications for safety screening
- Comparative or prospective clinical data showing a vinblastine-specific contribution in rhabdomyosarcoma
- Confirmation of the approved indication for the Australian product
- A safety and monitoring plan for paediatric and adult use, including neurotoxicity, myelosuppression and interaction management
