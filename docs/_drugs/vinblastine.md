## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on well-established pharmacology, Vinblastine is a vinca alkaloid that binds tubulin and inhibits microtubule polymerisation, arresting rapidly dividing cells in mitosis and triggering apoptosis. Historically, its clinical utility has centred on Hodgkin lymphoma, testicular cancer and other rapidly proliferating malignancies.

Rhabdomyosarcoma is a highly proliferative paediatric soft-tissue sarcoma, and vinca alkaloids as a class (vincristine, vinorelbine) are already core components of standard rhabdomyosarcoma regimens (e.g. VAC — vincristine, actinomycin D, cyclophosphamide). The mechanistic rationale for vinblastine — a closely related vinca alkaloid with a shared antimitotic mechanism — extending to rhabdomyosarcoma is therefore biologically plausible, and is supported by preclinical xenograft data (PMID 3329524) directly comparing vinca alkaloid sensitivity in human rhabdomyosarcoma models.

It is important to note that much of the supporting literature in this evidence pack concerns **vinorelbine**, a related but distinct vinca alkaloid, rather than vinblastine itself. Direct vinblastine evidence in rhabdomyosarcoma is largely limited to individual case reports of combination chemotherapy regimens.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Vinblastine is not currently registered in Australia (0 ARTG entries), no clinical trials evaluate this drug specifically for rhabdomyosarcoma, and TGA/PI safety data (warnings, contraindications) is a documented Blocking gap. Direct evidence is largely limited to historical case reports rather than vinblastine-specific rhabdomyosarcoma trials.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
