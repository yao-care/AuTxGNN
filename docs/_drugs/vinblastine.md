## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, vinblastine is a vinca alkaloid that binds tubulin and disrupts microtubule assembly. This causes mitotic arrest in rapidly dividing cells.

Rhabdomyosarcoma is a highly proliferative paediatric sarcoma, so an antimitotic mechanism is plausible. Vinca alkaloids already have a place in paediatric sarcoma regimens, although vincristine is the standard member. Vinblastine would therefore be a class-based extension rather than an independently validated option.

The published support is limited to case reports, small pilot or regimen studies and preclinical work. In several reports vinblastine was given within multi-drug combinations, so its individual contribution cannot be separated out.

Other predicted indications in the data were also reviewed. Prostate embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma has only case-level support from the 1980s. Monocytic leukaemia has older clinical series from the 1970s. Neuroblastoma has preclinical support. Several other rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes and ganglioneuroblastoma rest on the model prediction alone.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Support for rhabdomyosarcoma comes from a high model score, an established antimitotic mechanism and a handful of case reports, small pilot studies and preclinical studies. No registered trials exist, and vinblastine's independent contribution is unproven (vincristine is the standard vinca alkaloid). The data therefore support a research question, not a treatment recommendation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data confirmed from DrugBank
- The TGA Product Information, to obtain approved indications, warnings and contraindications. Safety screening cannot proceed without it
- Comparative evidence against standard vincristine-based regimens, ideally prospective data or a registered trial (including searching ANZCTR)
- A review of vinblastine's contribution within combination regimens
- A safety monitoring plan covering myelosuppression, neurotoxicity and the major interaction with clarithromycin
