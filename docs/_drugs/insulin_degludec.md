# Insulin Degludec: From Diabetes Mellitus to Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

## One-Sentence Summary

Insulin degludec is an ultra-long-acting basal insulin analogue, originally used to treat diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **type 1 diabetes mellitus**, which is largely a subset of its existing use, so this is not a true repurposing case.
The data compiled for this report include **more than 40 registered clinical trials** and **19 publications** supporting this use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, insulin degludec is a basal insulin analogue that binds the insulin receptor and replaces deficient endogenous insulin. In type 1 diabetes, the body produces little or no insulin, so basal insulin replacement is the foundation of treatment.

The predicted indication sits within the drug's original indication of diabetes mellitus. The very high TxGNN score is consistent with this established mechanism. The product registered in Australia (Ryzodeg) is a co-formulation of insulin degludec and insulin aspart (70/30). The trial evidence below covers both degludec alone and the degludec/aspart co-formulation.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials and several systematic reviews support insulin degludec in type 1 diabetes. This is an on-label use, so the main task is confirming the Australian product's labelled use rather than demonstrating new efficacy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The approved indication text for ARTG 11426J, which is blank in the data compiled. Confirm that type 1 diabetes is covered by the Ryzodeg registration, since the trials above largely involve degludec alone.
- Warnings and contraindications from the TGA Product Information, as this safety data was not available.
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank).
- A hypoglycaemia monitoring plan, individualised dosing, and a review of interacting medicines (351 known interactions).
