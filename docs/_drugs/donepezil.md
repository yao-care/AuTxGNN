## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, donepezil is a cholinesterase inhibitor with an established role in Alzheimer's disease, and it may be mechanistically applicable to tic disorders.

Striatal cholinergic dysfunction has been proposed in tics and ADHD. Cholinergic and nicotinic modulation of dopaminergic and serotonergic pathways in the striatum could reduce tics. Mouse studies support this: donepezil reduced the DOI-induced head twitch response, a model of tic-like behaviour. Small clinical reports in children and adolescents with tics and ADHD point in the same direction.

This link is plausible but unproven. No controlled trials were retrieved, and the human data are limited to an open-label study and case reports.

Other predictions were reviewed (see the table below). Most have no evidence, and the evidence for the rest is indirect or conflicting.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindication-related conditions (from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not product label text):** epilepsy, asthma (including intrinsic and allergic asthma), bladder neck obstruction, active peptic ulcer disease, sick sinus syndrome (including familial), and atrioventricular block.

**Drug interactions (DDInter, 100 interactions recorded; a selection is shown):**
- **Major:** bupropion
- **Moderate:** famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, hyoscyamine, atropine (including ophthalmic), glycopyrronium, clidinium, dicyclomine, loperamide, dexamethasone, metronidazole, dolasetron, picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol (3350 with electrolytes)
- **Minor:** aprepitant, acetylsalicylic acid, lorcaserin, clarithromycin

**Indication-specific concerns:**
- A 2025 systematic review and several case reports link cholinesterase inhibitors to movement disorders (for example Pisa syndrome and jaw tremor), which conflicts with the proposed use in movement disorders.
- Peptic ulcer risk matters for the tremor-nystagmus-duodenal ulcer syndrome prediction.
- Safety in porphyria has not been evaluated.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The top-ranked prediction (psychogenic movement disorders) has no trials or literature. The best-supported prediction, chronic tic disorder, rests on one small open-label study, case reports and mouse data, so it is a research question rather than a candidate for clinical adoption. Evidence for the movement-disorder predictions also contains conflicting benefit and harm signals.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled trials (ideally randomised, placebo-controlled) of donepezil in chronic tic disorder, with registration on ClinicalTrials.gov or ANZCTR
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications for Aricept, and the approved indication text for ARTG 2479L
- A safety review for the target populations (especially children and adolescents, patients with cardiac conduction disorders, asthma, epilepsy or peptic ulcer disease) and for movement-disorder worsening
- Confirmation of Australian PBS listing and indication status, which was a flagged gap in the data compiled for this report
