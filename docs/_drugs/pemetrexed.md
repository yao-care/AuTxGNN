# Pemetrexed: From Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer and Pleural Mesothelioma to Malignant Peritoneal Mesothelioma

## One-Sentence Summary

Pemetrexed is an antifolate chemotherapy originally used for non-small cell lung cancer and malignant pleural mesothelioma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **malignant peritoneal mesothelioma**,
with **7 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Most of this evidence comes from small Phase 2 studies, retrospective series and reviews.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, pemetrexed is a multi-targeted antifolate that inhibits thymidylate synthase, dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR) and glycinamide ribonucleotide formyltransferase (GARFT). This blocks purine and pyrimidine synthesis in rapidly dividing cells.

Peritoneal and pleural mesothelioma share the same mesothelial histology and folate-dependent proliferation. Pemetrexed plus cisplatin is the established first-line chemotherapy for pleural mesothelioma, supported by a pivotal Phase 3 trial, and is commonly extrapolated to the peritoneal form. Because peritoneal mesothelioma is rare, prospective disease-specific trials are scarce, and systemic therapy recommendations are mostly extrapolated from pleural disease.

Other TxGNN predictions in this list are not equally credible. Pleural mesothelioma and its epithelioid, sarcomatoid and biphasic subtypes are on-label or subtypes of an approved use, so they are not true repurposing. Iminoglycinuria and pleural adenomatoid tumour appear to be knowledge-graph artefacts. This report focuses on peritoneal mesothelioma.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (knowledge graph, not label text)**: kidney disease, impaired renal function, pyoureter, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia and macrocytic anaemia.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. The 20 listed in the compiled data are all rated Moderate. They include:
  - Famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine
  - Metformin
  - Amphotericin B (including lipid, cholesteryl sulfate and liposomal forms)
  - Amoxicillin
  - Aspirin
  - Mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine
  - Vancomycin, kanamycin, neomycin, polymyxin B, levofloxacin
  - Sapropterin

  Many of these are nephrotoxic or compete for renal tubular secretion, which may raise pemetrexed exposure.

Product label warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Pemetrexed has a strong mechanistic rationale and is already the standard backbone for pleural mesothelioma. Peritoneal-specific evidence consists of small Phase 2 trials, retrospective cohorts and reviews, with no completed Phase 3 trial, so the evidence level is L2. This is reasonable for specialist-led use with safeguards, not for routine adoption.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The TGA Product Information for Pemetrexed Accord, including indication text, warnings and contraindications, since label data were unavailable.
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank, which could not be retrieved.
- Results from the recruiting randomised peritoneal mesothelioma trials (NCT05001880, NCT03875144, NCT06057935).
- Multidisciplinary review of each patient, including the role of cytoreductive surgery with HIPEC and immunotherapy options.
- A monitoring plan for renal function and blood counts, with a medication review for nephrotoxic or interacting drugs.
- Standard vitamin supplementation and dosing per the PI, which should be confirmed from the label.
