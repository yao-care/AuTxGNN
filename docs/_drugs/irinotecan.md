## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, irinotecan is a topoisomerase I inhibitor. Its active metabolite, SN-38, causes DNA damage in rapidly dividing tumour cells.

Breast carcinoma has an indirect link to this mechanism. Sacituzumab govitecan, a Trop-2-directed antibody-drug conjugate that carries SN-38, is clinically validated in breast cancer, including HR+/HER2- metastatic disease. This shows topoisomerase I inhibition is a pharmacologically valid approach in breast cancer. It is not direct evidence for irinotecan itself.

Direct evidence for irinotecan is limited. One older review described irinotecan and mitomycin as having only marginal activity in advanced breast cancer, although preclinical work suggested synergy. A recent Phase 2 study (PHENOMENAL, 2026) of liposomal irinotecan in HER2-negative breast cancer with brain metastases is the most relevant newer signal. Its abstract in the compiled data did not include results, and liposomal irinotecan is a different formulation from the product registered in Australia.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and the TxGNN score is high, but direct evidence for irinotecan in breast cancer is limited to one small Phase 1 trial and older reviews. Most supporting data concern SN-38 antibody-drug conjugates or liposomal and experimental formulations, which cannot be extrapolated to the registered product. Label safety data are also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Phase 2 or larger efficacy data for irinotecan itself in breast cancer, including results from the liposomal irinotecan PHENOMENAL study
- A clear comparison of the benefit of conventional irinotecan against established breast cancer options, including sacituzumab govitecan
- The TGA Product Information to confirm indications, warnings and contraindications
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A plan for UGT1A1 genotype-guided dosing and monitoring for neutropenia and diarrhoea
