# Oxazepam: From Alcohol Withdrawal Delirium and Anxiety to Insomnia

## One-Sentence Summary

Oxazepam is a short-acting benzodiazepine, originally used for alcohol withdrawal delirium and anxiety.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known class pharmacology, oxazepam is a benzodiazepine that enhances GABA-A receptor signalling, producing anxiolytic and sedative-hypnotic effects. Its efficacy in anxiety and alcohol withdrawal is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to insomnia.

Anxiety and insomnia commonly occur together, and the GABA-A pathway that drives anxiolysis also shortens sleep latency and may help sleep maintenance. Short-acting benzodiazepines have long been used as hypnotics. Oxazepam's lack of active metabolites and its glucuronidation-based clearance are sometimes cited as advantages in older people.

The supporting studies are old (1980s), small, and methodologically limited. Reviews also highlight dependence, falls and cognitive effects in older adults. This is class-level inference rather than new oxazepam-specific evidence.

The model also ranked 9 other diseases. Only alcohol withdrawal falls within the original indication scope, so it is not a true repurposing candidate. The remaining candidates have no meaningful therapeutic evidence: either no literature at all, or only toxicology and analytical papers.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication signals (from knowledge-graph drug-disease links, not label text):** anxiety and neurotic disorders, porphyria, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, psychotic and personality disorders, hypotensive disorders, sleep apnoea syndromes, respiratory failure and lung disease, substance-related disorders and dependence, and intermittent explosive disorder. Several of these (substance dependence, respiratory disease, sleep apnoea) are directly relevant to a hypnotic indication.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded. Major interactions include **morphine** (including liposomal morphine), consistent with additive central nervous system and respiratory depression. Moderate interactions include bupropion, dronabinol, nabilone, metoclopramide, opium, sibutramine and teduglutide. Antacids and calcium or magnesium products are minor.
- **Literature-based concerns:** dependence, withdrawal (including rebound insomnia), abuse liability, and falls and cognitive effects in older adults.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for the full warnings and contraindications, as these were not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and short-term efficacy is suggested by small, old trials, but there are no registered trials, the evidence is rated L3, and the benefit-risk profile is unfavourable compared with current insomnia options because of dependence, withdrawal and sedation risks. The predicted indication is therefore best treated as a research question, not a basis for a change in use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for oxazepam
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Approved indication text for the ARTG entry
- Comparison against current guideline-recommended insomnia treatments, with a focus on older adults
- A defined short-term, low-dose use case, and a dependence and withdrawal risk-mitigation plan, supported by modern controlled trial data

**Other predictions (not recommended to progress):** alcohol withdrawal is within the original indication scope. The following should be held because evidence is absent or indirect only: cauda equina syndrome, barbiturate abuse, hallucinogen abuse, antidepressant type abuse, myofascial pain syndrome, acute encephalopathy with biphasic seizures and late reduced diffusion, and phencyclidine abuse.
