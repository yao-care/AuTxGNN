# Flecainide: From Atrial Fibrillation to Stroke Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Flecainide is a class IC sodium channel blocker, originally used for rhythm control in atrial fibrillation.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **stroke disorder**, but the link is indirect (through atrial arrhythmia prevention), with **1 Phase 3 clinical trial** and **about 20 publications** currently related to this direction. No study shows a direct stroke benefit.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, flecainide is a class IC sodium channel blocker (antiarrhythmic), its use in rhythm control for atrial fibrillation is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to stroke only indirectly.

Atrial fibrillation is a major cause of cardioembolic stroke. If flecainide maintains sinus rhythm and prevents atrial arrhythmia, it could in theory reduce the risk of some cardioembolic events. However, this is a surrogate pathway. The available trial measures arrhythmia, not stroke recurrence, and the literature is mostly atrial fibrillation management reviews and observational studies.

Applicability is also limited because class IC agents are generally avoided in structural heart disease and after myocardial infarction, conditions common in stroke populations.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (not label text; must be verified against the Product Information): myocardial infarction, coronary thrombosis, atrioventricular block, sick sinus syndrome (including familial forms), kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, potassium deficiency, hemoglobinopathy, pyoureter.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major interactions include **dolasetron** and **eliglustat**. Moderate interactions include famotidine, cimetidine, loperamide, bupropion, lorcaserin, clarithromycin, levofloxacin, palonosetron, trospium, and several laxatives and bowel preparations (bisacodyl, picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol with electrolytes, sodium sulfate, lactitol, lactulose). Minor interactions include ascorbic acid, potassium citrate and metronidazole.
- **Literature signals**: proarrhythmic risk in patients with structural heart disease or ischaemia, Brugada pattern unmasking, and pharmacokinetic interactions between antiarrhythmics and DOACs, which is relevant in a stroke-prevention population.

Please also refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and precautions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is indirect: the only Phase 3 trial targets post-PFO-closure arrhythmia rather than stroke, and the literature does not show a direct stroke benefit. Many stroke patients also have conditions (structural heart disease, prior myocardial infarction) in which flecainide is contraindicated, and safety screening cannot proceed without the label warnings.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results of NCT05213104 (estimated completion September 2026), particularly any stroke or thromboembolic outcomes
- Extraction of the warnings and contraindications from the TGA Product Information, and the approved indication text for the ARTG entry
- Mechanism of action data
- Analysis of stroke endpoints in existing class IC and EAST-AFNET 4 data
- A defined target population (for example, AF patients without structural heart disease) and an assessment of DOAC co-prescribing interactions
