# Potassium Citrate: From Potassium Deficiency, Renal Tubular Acidosis and Urate Nephropathy to Nephrolithiasis

## One-Sentence Summary

Potassium citrate was originally used to treat potassium deficiency, renal tubular acidosis and acute urate nephropathy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **nephrolithiasis (kidney stones)**, with **15 registered clinical trials** and **15 publications** currently supporting this direction.
This is largely an established, standard-of-care use rather than a true new indication, so the evidence is mechanistically strong but mostly consists of small or surrogate-endpoint studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, potassium citrate is metabolised to bicarbonate, which raises urinary pH and increases urinary citrate excretion.

Citrate is a potent inhibitor of calcium stone crystallisation. Raising urinary pH also helps dissolve uric acid stones. This fits closely with the original indications: renal tubular acidosis and urate nephropathy both involve disturbed acid-base handling and urinary crystal or stone risk, and hypocitraturia is common in distal renal tubular acidosis.

Nephrolithiasis is therefore best seen as an on-label or adjacent use. Two cautions apply. In calcium phosphate stone formers, excessive alkalinisation may raise supersaturation, so urinary pH should be monitored. Hyperkalaemia is a risk in renal impairment and with potassium-sparing drugs, ACE inhibitors or ARBs.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (these are database associations, not label text): kidney disease and impaired renal function, anuria, adrenal insufficiency/Addison disease, hypertension, peptic ulcer disease, pancreatitis, hypercalcaemia, nephrocalcinosis, rhabdomyolysis, coronary artery disease/myocardial infarction, diarrhoeal disease, benign prostatic hyperplasia and metabolic acidosis.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; examples):
  - *Major:* amiloride, benazepril, olmesartan, valsartan (hyperkalaemia risk); amitriptyline; chlorpheniramine, acrivastine, phenyltoloxamine; atropine, hyoscyamine, homatropine, methscopolamine.
  - *Moderate:* aliskiren, ibuprofen, ketorolac, aspirin, doxycycline, pseudoephedrine, amphetamine, dextroamphetamine.
- **Practical cautions:** monitor serum potassium and renal function, and monitor urinary pH to avoid over-alkalinisation (especially in calcium phosphate stone formers).

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for formal warnings and contraindications, as label-based data were not available.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Potassium citrate has a well-established mechanism and standard-of-care role in preventing and treating kidney stones, supported by a completed Phase 3 trial, a meta-analysis and several reviews. Most registered trials are small or use surrogate endpoints, and the high interaction and hyperkalaemia burden calls for careful patient selection.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Product Information review (warnings, contraindications, approved indication) for the Australian ARTG entry, including product name and dosage form
- Confirmation of the approved Australian indications, to determine whether nephrolithiasis is already on-label
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Monitoring plan for serum potassium, renal function and urinary pH, with attention to patients on ACE inhibitors, ARBs or potassium-sparing diuretics
- Results from larger randomised trials with clinical stone-recurrence endpoints, particularly in calcium phosphate stone formers

Other predicted indications (for example familial visceral myopathy, Pendred syndrome, cystinosis and exocrine pancreatic insufficiency) have no or only indirect evidence and are recommended as **Hold**.
