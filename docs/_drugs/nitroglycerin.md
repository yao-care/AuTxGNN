## One-Sentence Summary

Nitroglycerin is a nitric oxide donor vasodilator. It is registered in Australia as a transdermal patch (Transiderm-Nitro 25), but the data compiled for this report contain no approved indication text.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pulmonary hypertension**, with **3 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently linked to this direction.
The evidence is mostly small, early-phase or older haemodynamic studies, so it supports further research rather than a clinical change.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on established pharmacology, nitroglycerin is a nitric oxide (NO) donor. NO activates soluble guanylate cyclase and raises cGMP in vascular smooth muscle, causing vasodilation.

Pulmonary hypertension involves persistent pulmonary vasoconstriction and vascular remodelling. A vasodilator acting through the NO–cGMP pathway is therefore biologically plausible. Delivery by inhalation or nebulisation may give relatively selective pulmonary vasodilation and limit systemic hypotension. Early haemodynamic studies in pulmonary hypertension, including COPD-associated disease, point in the same direction.

The original indication is not recorded in the compiled data, so the relationship between the original and new indications cannot be assessed directly. The available trials focus on acute vasoreactivity testing or perioperative and neonatal adjunct use, not long-term disease modification. Note also that the trials use nebulised or inhaled nitroglycerin, whereas the registered Australian product is a transdermal patch.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions from the knowledge graph** (not label text): the TxGNN knowledge graph links nitroglycerin to contraindication or caution with hypotensive disorder, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, methaemoglobinaemia, sulfhaemoglobinaemia, anaemia, macrocytic anaemia, intracerebral haemorrhage, constrictive pericarditis, restrictive cardiomyopathy and cardiac tamponade.
- **Drug Interactions**: 228 interactions were identified. Moderate-level examples include bupropion, morphine, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, dronabinol, nabilone, rosiglitazone and sapropterin. Minor-level examples include aspirin, atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium, scopolamine and omeprazole.
- Systemic vasodilation carries a risk of hypotension, which is particularly relevant in right ventricular dysfunction or perioperative settings.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for label warnings and contraindications, as these were not available in the compiled data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is mechanistically plausible and the TxGNN score is high. However, the supporting trials are small, early-phase or non-phased, with no Phase 2 or 3 RCT, and they use nebulised or inhaled delivery rather than the registered transdermal patch. The original indication is also unknown, so novelty cannot be confirmed. This is best treated as a research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The TGA-approved Product Information, including indications, warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data confirmed from DrugBank
- Adequately powered Phase 2/3 RCTs with clinical outcomes, not only acute vasoreactivity
- Evaluation of the formulation and route (nebulised or inhaled vs transdermal) and whether a suitable product is available in Australia
- A safety plan addressing hypotension and right ventricular function in pulmonary hypertension patients
