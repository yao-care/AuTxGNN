## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Rifabutin is a rifamycin that inhibits bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase (rpoB). It has no direct activity against HIV.

The link to HIV is indirect. People with advanced HIV are prone to opportunistic mycobacterial infections, namely *Mycobacterium avium* complex (MAC) and tuberculosis. Rifabutin has been studied for preventing and treating these infections. It is a weaker CYP3A inducer than rifampicin, so it is often preferred when a patient also takes protease inhibitors. It still interacts significantly with several antiretrovirals (for example dolutegravir, lopinavir/ritonavir and long-acting cabotegravir/rilpivirine), and these interactions need dose adjustment and monitoring.

The prediction is best read as "HIV-associated MAC/TB management", not as treatment of HIV infection. No cited study evaluates rifabutin for HIV infection itself, which is why the evidence level is L5.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interacting drugs were identified. Examples:
  - **Major**: eliglustat.
  - **Moderate**: clarithromycin, doxycycline, metronidazole, pioglitazone, saxagliptin, linagliptin, aprepitant, morphine, and corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone).
  - **Minor**: dolasetron.
- **Haematological risk**: severe neutropenia led to early termination of one paediatric study of rifabutin with protease inhibitors, and treatment-limiting neutropenia occurred in children in a related study (see the literature table). Monitoring of blood counts is advisable.
- **Antiretroviral interactions**: the trials and publications above describe significant interactions with several antiretrovirals, requiring dose adjustment and monitoring.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No cited study treats HIV infection itself. The trials and publications concern mycobacterial disease (MAC and TB) in people with HIV, or drug interactions with antiretrovirals, so the evidence level is L5. Rifabutin has no antiviral mechanism.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A clear definition of the intended indication (for example HIV-associated MAC/TB prophylaxis or treatment rather than HIV infection itself), supported by studies that directly address it
- Mechanism of action data and the TGA PI warnings and contraindications
- A drug-interaction management plan for common antiretroviral regimens
- A haematological monitoring plan, particularly for children and patients on protease inhibitors
