## Rifabutin: From Mycobacterial Infection to HIV infectious disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Rifabutin is a rifamycin antibacterial. Its original approved indication text is not available in the data compiled for this report, although it is marketed in Australia as Mycobutin.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **HIV infectious disease**, with **35 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently linked to this direction.
The evidence is best read as support for treating and preventing HIV-associated *Mycobacterium avium* complex (MAC) and tuberculosis (TB), not for treating HIV itself.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, rifabutin is a rifamycin that inhibits bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase (rpoB). It does not act on HIV itself.

Its value in HIV lies in treating and preventing opportunistic mycobacterial infections, mainly MAC and TB, in people living with HIV. Rifabutin is a weaker CYP3A inducer than rifampicin, so it is often preferred when a patient also takes protease inhibitors.

It still has substantial drug interactions (for example with dolutegravir, lopinavir/ritonavir and long-acting cabotegravir/rilpivirine) that need dose adjustment and monitoring. The model's HIV prediction is therefore reasonable only when framed as HIV-associated MAC/TB.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from drug–disease links, not label text): thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotising enterocolitis. The paediatric literature above also reports treatment-limiting neutropenia.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 recorded; examples):
  - **Major:** eliglustat
  - **Moderate:** clarithromycin, doxycycline, metronidazole, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, morphine, aprepitant, pioglitazone, saxagliptin, linagliptin, chlorpropamide
  - **Minor:** dolasetron
- Uveitis has been reported with rifabutin prophylaxis combined with itraconazole (PMID 8967681).

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials support rifabutin-containing regimens for MAC prevention and treatment in people with HIV, and pharmacokinetic studies support its use with antiretrovirals. However, the evidence concerns HIV-associated opportunistic infections, not HIV itself, and most efficacy trials predate modern antiretroviral therapy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm the approved indication text for ARTG 6195C and the TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications
- Define the indication as HIV-associated MAC/TB, not HIV infection per se
- Interaction and dosing guidance for current antiretrovirals (protease inhibitors, dolutegravir, long-acting cabotegravir/rilpivirine)
- Haematological monitoring plan (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia), with extra caution in children
- Mechanism of action data to be added to the record

**Other predictions (all Hold unless noted):** Leprosy has only preclinical and case-report support (research question). Endocarditis and pneumocystosis have indirect evidence only. Multiple endocrine neoplasia, sclerosing cholangitis, the neurodevelopmental disorder, conjunctivitis and the feline and simian immunodeficiency predictions have no clinical support and appear to be model artefacts.
