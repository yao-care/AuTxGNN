## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacology, bupropion inhibits the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine (an NDRI) and has no serotonergic activity.

These are the same catecholaminergic pathways targeted by established ADHD medicines. Stimulants such as methylphenidate and amphetamines act by increasing dopamine and norepinephrine, and atomoxetine acts on noradrenaline. Bupropion is therefore mechanistically plausible as a non-stimulant option, and published reviews list it among antidepressant-class medicines used in ADHD, particularly where stimulants are not tolerated or are unsuitable.

This link rests on general pharmacology, not on a mechanism record supplied with the data.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged for review:** The data compiled for this report list conditions with a recorded contraindication relationship to bupropion, which are not label text. These include epilepsy, hypertension, liver failure and cirrhosis, kidney disease, bipolar mania, psychotic disorders, anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa, substance and opiate dependence, recent myocardial infarction, and central nervous system tumours. They should be confirmed against the TGA-approved Product Information.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Major-level interactions in the data include fentanyl, lidocaine, dihydrocodeine, codeine, tramadol, methylphenidate, hydrocortisone, aldesleukin, alfentanil and amantadine. Moderate-level interactions include ethanol, alprazolam, nifedipine, hydrochlorothiazide, cetirizine, chlorpheniramine, acebutolol and alfuzosin.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 3 randomised placebo-controlled trial, several further completed trials in ADHD populations, and a Cochrane review of bupropion in adult ADHD directly address the predicted disease. The evidence is limited to one Phase 3 trial, with small comorbid-population studies, so it is not yet at the strength of two Phase 3 trials. Seizure-threshold and cardiovascular considerations also require careful patient selection.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full results and comparator details of NCT00048360, and review of the Cochrane findings on efficacy and tolerability
- Mechanism of action data from a primary source
- Review of the TGA Product Information for warnings and contraindications, including the seizure risk and blood pressure effects
- A screening and monitoring plan for patients with comorbid substance use, bipolar disorder, eating disorders or epilepsy
- A check of interaction risks with opioids and other concurrent medicines
- Confirmation of whether the single registered product (Zyban) can be used for this purpose, or whether a separate regulatory pathway is needed
