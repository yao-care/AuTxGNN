## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for benzocaine is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on its known pharmacology, benzocaine is a topical ester local anaesthetic that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels and reduces nerve signal transmission at the site of application.

Theoretically, this could relieve ocular surface discomfort or itch. However, papillary conjunctivitis is driven by immune-mediated processes (allergic) or mechanical irritation (for example contact lenses or prostheses). A local anaesthetic does not address these underlying causes, so any benefit would be symptomatic at best.

Prolonged use of topical ocular anaesthetics is also associated with corneal toxicity, which is a safety concern for this setting. The link between benzocaine and this condition should be regarded as a model-derived hypothesis that cannot be verified from the available data.
