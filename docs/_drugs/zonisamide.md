## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed structured mechanism-of-action (MOA) data for zonisamide was not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on the evidence available, zonisamide is characterised as a broad-spectrum anticonvulsant acting through multiple ion-channel mechanisms — blockade of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels and T-type Ca²⁺ channels — mechanisms already established in the treatment of partial and generalised seizures.

Unlike classic drug repurposing across unrelated disease areas, absence epilepsy sits within the same disease spectrum as zonisamide's existing anticonvulsant use. The clinical trial evidence in this pack largely supports zonisamide's efficacy and long-term safety in partial-onset epilepsy generally, while the literature evidence — including a paediatric case series showing 51.1% seizure freedom and a dedicated case series in drug-resistant juvenile absence epilepsy — provides more direct support specifically for absence seizures.

It is worth noting that this evidence pack also flagged several other TxGNN-predicted associations (Tourette syndrome, trichotillomania, Prinzmetal angina, fibromyalgia, conjunctivitis) with weak or no supporting evidence (L4–L5, Hold), and two predictions (methemoglobinemia, methemoglobin reductase deficiency) that most likely represent a **known sulfonamide-class safety risk rather than a therapeutic opportunity** — these should not be read as repurposing candidates.

## Safety Considerations

As zonisamide is not currently registered in Australia, no TGA-approved Product Information is available, and formal warnings, contraindications and drug interaction data were flagged as a **Blocking** data gap in this pack. Prescribers should refer to an overseas regulatory-approved Product Information (e.g. FDA or EMA label) for full prescribing safety details before any clinical use is considered.

**Known class-related safety signals** (drawn from the evidence pack's mechanistic rationale, not yet confirmed against a formal label):
- As a sulfonamide-derivative anticonvulsant, zonisamide carries a recognised, if rare, risk of **methemoglobinemia**, particularly relevant in patients with G6PD or methemoglobin reductase deficiency. This is a **safety risk**, not a therapeutic indication, despite appearing among the TxGNN model's top disease associations.
- Sulfonamide-class drugs, including zonisamide, are associated with **acute angle-closure glaucoma**. A TxGNN association with conjunctivitis is most plausibly explained by this ocular adverse-effect profile rather than any treatment benefit.
