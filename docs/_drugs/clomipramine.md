## Clomipramine: Anxiety disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Clomipramine is a tricyclic antidepressant with potent serotonin reuptake inhibition. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **anxiety disorder**, and the data compiled for this report include **8 clinical trials** and **20 publications**. Most of this evidence comes from closely related anxiety-spectrum conditions such as panic disorder and compulsive-spectrum disorders, rather than from generalised anxiety trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Clomipramine is known to be a potent serotonin reuptake inhibitor. Its active metabolite, desmethylclomipramine, adds noradrenergic reuptake inhibition.

Serotonergic and noradrenergic modulation of fear and anxiety circuits (amygdala and prefrontal cortex) is shared across anxiety disorders and closely related conditions. This offers a plausible reason for clomipramine to help in anxiety disorders.

The supporting literature is consistent with this. A Cochrane network meta-analysis in panic disorder (2023) and the WFSBP guidelines support antidepressants, including clomipramine, in anxiety-spectrum disorders. Several randomised and controlled studies also tested clomipramine directly in panic disorder, as listed below.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug interactions:** The data compiled for this report list 100 interacting drugs. The following are classified as **Major**:
  - epinephrine
  - bupropion
  - lorcaserin
  - potassium citrate
  - cisapride

  Moderate interactions include:
  - famotidine
  - cimetidine
  - clarithromycin
  - morphine
  - acetylsalicylic acid
  - chlorpropamide and acetohexamide
  - several anticholinergic agents (hyoscyamine, atropine, glycopyrronium, clidinium)
  - loperamide
  - activated charcoal
  - picosulfuric acid
  - polyethylene glycol with electrolytes

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high (0.9993). Trials and systematic reviews of clomipramine in anxiety-spectrum conditions, including panic disorder, are broadly supportive, so the evidence is rated L1. However, the registered trials are mostly in related populations, and the panic disorder studies are largely older and small. The local licence text does not record an anxiety indication, so any use would be outside the recorded approval.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the TGA-approved PI for warnings and contraindications, including seizure risk, cardiac effects and anticholinergic effects.
- A medication-interaction check, particularly for the Major interactions listed above.
- Detailed mechanism of action data.
- A clinical protocol that defines the target anxiety disorder subtype, dosing, ECG and adverse-effect monitoring, and exclusion criteria.
- Confirmation against current guidelines that clomipramine is appropriate where first-line agents have failed or are unsuitable.
