## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Clomipramine is a potent serotonin reuptake inhibitor. Its active metabolite, desmethylclomipramine, also has noradrenergic activity. Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report, so the description above relies on the published rationale.

Serotonergic modulation of fear and anxiety circuits (amygdala and prefrontal cortex) is shared across anxiety disorders and OCD, which is closely related to anxiety. A network meta-analysis of panic disorder treatments and the WFSBP guidelines on anxiety, obsessive-compulsive and posttraumatic stress disorders discuss antidepressants, including clomipramine, in this setting. This makes the prediction mechanistically plausible.

Evidence level note: the registered trials identified for this prediction study OCD populations, or study other drugs, and none directly studies anxiety disorder with clomipramine as the tested treatment. Under the evidence rules, the level is therefore L5, even though the supporting publications on panic disorder and agoraphobia (conditions within the anxiety spectrum) are encouraging.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic rationale and publications on panic disorder and agoraphobia are supportive, but none of the registered trials directly tests clomipramine in anxiety disorders, so the evidence level for this prediction is L5. Current clinical guidance also favours other agents for anxiety, and clomipramine carries a notable adverse-effect burden.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A trial or systematic analysis that directly studies clomipramine in a defined anxiety disorder (for example panic disorder)
- Mechanism of action data and TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications for the Australian product
- A comparison of benefit and risk against current first-line anxiety treatments, including cardiac, seizure and anticholinergic risks
