## Ethanol: From an Unspecified Original Indication to Migraine Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Ethanol is registered in Australia, but the data compiled for this report contain no approved indication text for it.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and the retrieved literature largely describes ethanol as a migraine **trigger**, not a treatment.
Overall this is a model-only signal that the available evidence does not support.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for ethanol. Known pharmacology includes GABA-A positive modulation and central nervous system depression, but no therapeutic link to migraine has been established.

The data compiled for this report argue against the prediction. Alcohol is a recognised migraine trigger. Preclinical work shows ethanol is metabolised to acetaldehyde, which acts through CGRP receptor and TRPA1 signalling in Schwann cells to produce periorbital allodynia in mice. Genetic studies link ADH2 genotype and GABA receptor variants to migraine risk, but they do not show a treatment effect.

The high score (0.9929) most likely reflects network proximity to migraine-related pathways (GABA, CGRP, TRPA1, KATP channels) and not a therapeutic effect. Therapeutic repurposing is not supported.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on model score alone. The literature indicates ethanol triggers migraine, with no clinical trials and no therapeutic evidence. Ethanol is a cause of sleep disruption and neuropathy in the retrieved studies.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Approved indication text and product details for the two ARTG entries
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications
- Controlled clinical evidence of benefit in migraine, which is currently absent and contradicted by existing data
