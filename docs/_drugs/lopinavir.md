## Lopinavir: From HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Lopinavir is an HIV-1 protease inhibitor, originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, an animal disease with no human clinical indication.
Support is limited to **3 preclinical publications** in macaques and **no registered clinical trials**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Lopinavir is known to be an HIV-1 protease inhibitor. It is marketed as a fixed-dose combination with ritonavir (Kaletra, 200 mg/50 mg tablet), and its efficacy in HIV infection is established.

SIV is a closely related primate lentivirus with a structurally similar protease, so activity against it is mechanistically plausible. The supporting literature consists of macaque studies in which lopinavir was one component of antiretroviral combinations. This is indirect, preclinical-model evidence.

SIV infection is an animal disease and has no human clinical indication. The high TxGNN score probably reflects the similarity to the original HIV indication rather than a genuine new therapeutic opportunity. In practice, SIV and SHIV macaque models are mainly useful as research tools for testing antiretroviral regimens.

Two other predictions were also generated: feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (score 99.90%, no supporting studies, veterinary indication, and feline protease is less sensitive to HIV protease inhibitors) and a rare neurodevelopmental disorder with ataxic gait, absent speech and decreased cortical white matter (score 99.90%, no plausible mechanistic link, possibly a knowledge-graph artefact). Neither is recommended for further action.

## Safety Considerations

The following comes from knowledge-graph contraindication associations (not product label text) and from drug interaction screening.

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication associations** (conditions linked to lopinavir):
  - Cardiac and conduction: long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, atrioventricular block, cardiomyopathy (including hypertrophic), myocardial ischaemia, coronary artery disease
  - Hepatic and biliary: liver disease, cirrhosis, chronic hepatitis B and C infection, gallbladder disease, hyperbilirubinaemia (including Gilbert syndrome)
  - Metabolic and pancreatic: pancreatitis, hyperglycaemia, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, hypertriglyceridaemia, familial combined hyperlipidaemia, potassium deficiency
  - Other: haemophilia
- **Drug interactions** (65 recorded; examples from the screening data):

Product Information (PI) warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by indirect preclinical macaque studies, and SIV infection has no human clinical indication. The other predicted indications have no supporting evidence. Lopinavir already has an established role in HIV, so this prediction offers no new therapeutic opportunity.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation that a human-relevant indication exists, since SIV and feline immunodeficiency are not human diseases
- Mechanism of action data (MOA) from DrugBank
- The TGA Product Information (PI), including warnings, contraindications and approved indication text
- For the neurodevelopmental disorder prediction, a gene or pathway-level mechanistic link before any further consideration
