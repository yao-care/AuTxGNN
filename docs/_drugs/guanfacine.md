## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Guanfacine is known to be a selective alpha-2A adrenergic agonist. It strengthens prefrontal cortical network signalling and reduces noradrenergic tone. It is already used for ADHD.

Tics in Tourette syndrome involve prefrontal-striatal circuits and noradrenergic modulation. About half of children with Tourette syndrome also have ADHD. Stimulants, the usual ADHD treatment, can worsen tics, so an alpha-2 agonist that treats both conditions is clinically attractive. Guanfacine already appears in pharmacological management reviews and guidelines for tic disorders, including the European clinical guidelines (PMID 34757514).

The other nine predictions are much weaker. Most have no trials or publications. "Specific developmental disorder" largely overlaps with the existing ADHD indication, so it is closer to a label extension than a new use. Migraine has only indirect support (commentary and hypothesis papers). For manic bipolar affective disorder, the guanfacine-specific literature reports secondary mania in children, which is a potential harm signal.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications and conditions of concern** (from knowledge graph drug-disease links, not product label text): orthostatic hypotension and other hypotensive disorders, multiple system atrophy, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, atrioventricular block and heart block, myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease, cerebrovascular disorder, chronic kidney disease and end-stage renal failure, liver disease, gallbladder disease, anxiety and neurotic disorders, dysthymic disorder, constipation, and haemoglobinopathy.

**Drug interactions** (100 interactions recorded; the main ones listed):
- **Major:** aprepitant, dexamethasone, clarithromycin
- **Moderate (examples):** corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisone, prednisolone, budesonide, betamethasone, triamcinolone), metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin), cimetidine, miconazole, cannabinoids (dronabinol, nabilone)

**Safety signals in the literature:**
- Syncope in children with Tourette syndrome treated with guanfacine (PMID 16229000)
- Secondary mania in children (PMID 10467976, 9730081, 27228067), relevant to patients with mood disorder risk
- Sinus pause in a combined olanzapine and guanfacine overdose (PMID 31447925)

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for official warnings and contraindications, as label text was not available.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Tourette syndrome has a completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trial and a Phase 4 study, and guanfacine is referenced in guideline-level literature. However, both trials are small (n=35 and n=34), and systematic reviews note limited overall evidence quality for tic drugs. The other nine predictions (including the top-scoring faciodigitogenital syndrome, chondromyxoid fibroma, and variably protease-sensitive prionopathy) lack evidence and should remain on Hold. Migraine and "specific developmental disorder" are research questions only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The TGA Product Information, to confirm warnings, contraindications and the approved indications for Intuniv (the licence record is blank), which also resolves a blocking safety data gap
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of effect sizes from larger, adequately powered controlled trials in Tourette syndrome
- A monitoring plan covering blood pressure, heart rate, sedation and syncope, with a caution against abrupt discontinuation
- Screening for cardiac conduction disorders, hypotension and mood disorder history, and review of interacting medicines (particularly CYP3A4 inhibitors such as clarithromycin)
- Confirmation of whether any ANZCTR-registered trials exist
