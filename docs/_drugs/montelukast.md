## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Montelukast belongs to the leukotriene receptor antagonist class. These drugs block the cysteinyl leukotriene pathway, which drives airway inflammation.

Several of the registered studies are built on this inflammatory link. For example, the RSV bronchiolitis trial NCT00524693 notes that cysteinyl-leukotriene levels are raised in that illness. The trials in non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis test whether adding montelukast to inhaled budesonide improves cough and reduces airway eosinophilia.

The evidence is mixed in design. Small controlled trials address eosinophilic bronchitis and recurrent obstructive bronchitis in children. Larger trials address bronchiolitis in infants, and the registry summaries do not state their results. The prediction is therefore plausible, but the benefit has not been established.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The evidence level is L2. Small controlled trials of montelukast exist in non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis and recurrent obstructive bronchitis. The larger bronchiolitis trials address a related condition, and their results are not stated in the data compiled for this report. The model score is very high (0.9995), but the clinical benefit for bronchitis remains unproven.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the published results of the eosinophilic bronchitis and recurrent obstructive bronchitis trials (NCT01121016, NCT04613180), and of the Cochrane conclusions, to confirm whether any benefit is shown
- Mechanism of action data and a clear definition of the target bronchitis population (for example eosinophilic or paediatric)
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications, which are not in the compiled data
- A review of the moderate-severity interactions listed above before any use in a new population
