## Montelukast: Bronchitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Montelukast is a leukotriene receptor antagonist, originally used for allergic rhinitis, vasomotor rhinitis, and intrinsic and allergic asthma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis** (including bronchiolitis and eosinophilic bronchitis), with **17 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this prediction. The evidence is mixed: the largest trial and a Cochrane review addressed bronchiolitis in infants, while the strongest signals are in related post-transplant and eosinophilic conditions.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, montelukast is a selective cysteinyl leukotriene receptor 1 (CysLT1) antagonist. Its efficacy in asthma and allergic rhinitis is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to inflammatory airway conditions such as bronchitis.

The original indications (asthma and rhinitis) and the predicted indication share airway inflammation driven by leukotrienes. Cysteinyl leukotrienes are raised in RSV bronchiolitis, and eosinophilic airway inflammation is a feature of non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis. This provides a plausible rationale. Note that "bronchitis" here covers several distinct conditions (viral bronchiolitis, eosinophilic bronchitis, recurrent obstructive bronchitis, and post-transplant bronchiolitis obliterans), and the evidence differs by condition.

## Safety Considerations

- **Neuropsychiatric events**: A boxed warning was issued by the US FDA in 2020 for neuropsychiatric adverse effects. A systematic review and nationwide cohort studies have examined this risk, particularly in children, and findings across studies are not fully consistent.
- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (not label text): anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, anxiety, hypereosinophilic syndrome and cutaneous vasculitis.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified in the interaction databases. The main moderate-level interactions are dexamethasone, metronidazole, miconazole, nateglinide, nitisinone and troglitazone. Many others (for example pantoprazole, omeprazole, metformin and morphine) have an unknown severity level.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The largest bronchiolitis trial and a Cochrane review exist, but the data compiled do not show consistent benefit for bronchitis or bronchiolitis, and results for most trials are not reported. "Bronchitis" spans very different conditions, and the Australian licence text is missing. For context, the data's own assessment found much stronger support for asthma (an existing indication, so not a true repurposing case) and a plausible but unproven case for atopic eczema.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Extract the results of NCT00076973 and the Cochrane review (PMID 25773054) to determine whether montelukast shows benefit in bronchiolitis
- Define which bronchitis subtype is the target (viral bronchiolitis, eosinophilic bronchitis, or post-transplant bronchiolitis obliterans) and assess each separately
- Obtain the TGA Product Information, including the approved indications, warnings and contraindications
- Obtain mechanism of action data to support the mechanistic-link analysis
- A neuropsychiatric safety monitoring plan, especially for paediatric use
