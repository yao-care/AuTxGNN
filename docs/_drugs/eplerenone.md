# Eplerenone: From Hypertension to Pulmonary Hypertension Owing to Lung Disease and/or Hypoxia

## One-Sentence Summary

Eplerenone is a selective mineralocorticoid receptor (MR) antagonist, originally used to treat hypertension.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **pulmonary hypertension owing to lung disease and/or hypoxia**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **preclinical and indirect literature** supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, eplerenone is a selective MR antagonist. Its efficacy in hypertension is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to hypoxia-related pulmonary vascular disease.

Aldosterone and MR signalling are involved in vascular inflammation, fibrosis and remodelling. In rodent models of chronic intermittent hypoxia, eplerenone attenuated renal fibrosis and atrial remodelling. Preclinical pulmonary arterial hypertension work also suggests that aldosterone drives pulmonary vascular fibrosis and smooth muscle cell abnormalities under hypoxia, and that MR blockade improves pulmonary vascular remodelling. This provides a plausible link between the original indication (hypertension, a disease of vascular tone and remodelling) and the predicted indication.

However, this evidence is almost entirely animal or cell-based, and no direct human evidence in pulmonary hypertension due to lung disease or hypoxia was found. One animal study also found that MR antagonism with losartan was ineffective in experimental right ventricular failure, so the evidence is mixed.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** liver disease, gallbladder disease, diabetic nephropathy, impaired renal function disease.
- **Drug Interactions:** the interaction query returned 100 interacting drugs. Examples of **Major** interactions:
  - Aprepitant
  - Clarithromycin
  - Clotrimazole
  - Potassium chloride, potassium citrate and potassium bicarbonate (hyperkalaemia risk)
- **Moderate** interactions include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone), aspirin, SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin), bupropion, miconazole, dronabinol and nabilone.

Please refer to the TGA-approved Product Information (PI) for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high (99.50%), but the supporting evidence is preclinical or indirect, with no registered clinical trials in pulmonary hypertension due to lung disease or hypoxia (Evidence Level L4). Safety label data are also unavailable, so this remains a research question rather than an actionable repurposing candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- TGA Product Information warnings and contraindications for Inpler
- Human clinical evidence (pilot or Phase 2 studies) in pulmonary hypertension due to lung disease or hypoxia
- A hyperkalaemia and renal function monitoring plan, given the major interactions with potassium products and the renal contraindication signals
- Confirmation of the approved indication text for the ARTG entry
